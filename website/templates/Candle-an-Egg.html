Part 1: What is Candling?

Candling is a non-invasive technique used in poultry farming and hobby incubating to examine the internal development of eggs during incubation. By placing a bright light against an egg, the candler can visualize the contents and assess the embryo's progress or any potential abnormalities. Candling helps identify eggs that are not developing properly, preventing them from rotting and potentially harming other eggs in the incubation chamber.

Part 2: How to Candle Eggs

Equipment:

  • Bright light source (e.g., flashlight, candling lamp)
  • Hole slightly smaller in diameter than the egg

Procedure:

1. Determine Candle Schedule:

  • First Candle: Before placing eggs in the incubator
  • Second Candle: Day 7 of incubation
  • Third Candle: Day 14 of incubation

2. Proper Egg Placement:

  • Hold the egg horizontally with the larger end (containing the air sac) facing the light.
  • Gently rotate the egg to view its contents.

3. Signs of Normal Development:

  • Blood vessels: Gradually spreading outwards from the center
  • Embryo Outline: Visible as a dark silhouette
  • Movement: Slight movement of the embryo, particularly after day 7

4. Signs of Problems:

  • Blood Ring: A reddish circle inside the shell, indicating a dead embryo
  • Blood Spots or Streaks: Scattered or streaked blood, indicating a developing embryo with potential health issues
  • Missing or Stunted Embryo: No visible embryo or an embryo that appears underdeveloped

5. Uncertainty Resolution:

If an egg exhibits questionable signs, it's advisable to wait and observe it further. Candle the egg again on day 14 to confirm its status. If problems persist, the egg should be removed from the incubator to prevent contamination.

Additional Considerations:

  • Handle eggs carefully to avoid damaging the delicate membranes.
  • Candle eggs in a dark environment to enhance visibility.
  • Keep candling sessions brief to minimize egg exposure to light.
  • Dispose of any eggs exhibiting signs of severe abnormalities or developmental issues.

Conclusion:

Candling is an essential technique for successful egg hatching. By regularly checking the eggs under a bright light, you can monitor their development and identify any potential problems. Early detection of non-viable eggs allows you to remove them from the incubator, preventing spoilage and preserving the health of the remaining eggs. Additionally, candling provides valuable information about the progress of incubating eggs, giving you insights into the growth of the embryo and the viability of the egg. By incorporating candling into your incubation routine, you can significantly increase your chances of successfully hatching healthy chicks or ducklings.
