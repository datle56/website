Car Engine Diagnosis Made Easy: A Step-by-Step Guide

1. Warm Up and Prepare:

  • Start the engine and let it run until it reaches its normal operating temperature. This allows the engine to stabilize and makes diagnosing easier.
  • Once warm, turn off the engine and unplug any electrical sources if working on an electric vehicle.
  • For safety, wear protective gloves and glasses to prevent any potential injuries.

2. Disable Electrical and Fuel Systems:

  • Locate and remove both the fuel pump fuse and ignition coil fuse. These fuses help prevent any electrical or fuel supply during the diagnosis process.
  • Disconnect all spark plug wires. This prevents any ignition or electrical shocks while inspecting the spark plugs.

3. Remove Spark Plugs:

  • Using a spark plug socket wrench, carefully unscrew the spark plugs.
  • Label each spark plug with the corresponding cylinder number to ensure they are installed back in the correct position.
  • Inspect the spark plugs for any unusual wear, deposits, or damage that could indicate an underlying issue.

4. Test Compression:

  • Insert the compression gauge into the engine's first cylinder. Ensure a tight fit to prevent air leaks.
  • Crank the engine without starting it for 4-10 times or until the gauge reaches its peak reading.
  • Record the pressure reading indicated on the gauge.
  • Repeat this process for each cylinder, following the same procedure.

5. Interpret Results:

  • Optimal compression pressure varies depending on the engine type:
    • Standard engines: 125-175 pounds per square inch (PSI)
    • Diesel engines: 275-400 PSI
  • The variation in pressure between cylinders should be less than 10%. If the deviation exceeds this threshold, further diagnosis is required.

6. Possible Issues:

  • Low cylinder pressure: Could indicate worn piston rings, damaged valves, or a head gasket issue.
  • Adding oil to the cylinder and re-testing can help identify the issue: If adding oil temporarily increases compression, it suggests worn piston rings or valve problems.
  • Multiple low-pressure cylinders: Could be a sign of a more significant problem, such as engine timing issues or a blown head gasket.

Consult a Mechanic:

If the compression test results are significantly different from the optimal range or indicate multiple low-pressure cylinders, it's advisable to consult a qualified mechanic for further diagnosis and repair.
