Craft a Historic Steam Engine with the Step-by-Step Guide (Illustrated)

Kids' Soda Can Steam Engine

Materials:

  • Empty aluminum can
  • Copper tubing (12-14 inches)
  • Scissors
  • Pencil
  • Candle
  • Container of water

Instructions:

  1. Cut the Can: Use scissors to cut the aluminum can to a height of approximately 2 inches. Flatten the bottom of the can by pressing it down on a flat surface to ensure stability.
  2. Create Holes for Tubes: Make two small holes on opposite sides of the can, near the top edge. These holes should be just large enough to accommodate the diameter of the copper tubing.
  3. Make the Copper Coil: Wrap the copper tubing around a pencil to create a coil. Make sure the coil is snug and fits neatly inside the can.
  4. Bend the Tube Ends: Bend the ends of the coil into small jets. These jets will direct the steam outward.
  5. Fill with Water: Place the can in a container of water, ensuring that the jet ends are submerged.
  6. Fill the Tubes: Using a syringe or faucet, fill the copper tubes with water through one of the ends.
  7. Light the Candle: Place a lighted candle inside the can to heat the water.
  8. Generate Steam: As the water boils, it will create steam. The steam will push out of the jets, causing the can to spin.

Adults' Paint Can Steam Engine

Materials:

  • Large paint can (1-gallon or larger)
  • Smaller paint can (1/2-gallon or less)
  • Copper tubing (2 feet)
  • Charcoal briquettes
  • Mesh platform
  • Legs (for supporting the platform)
  • Plastic tubing (capped on one end)
  • Dowel rod
  • Skewer
  • Drill with drill bits

Instructions:

  1. Cut the Opening: Use a drill or hole saw to create a large opening in the larger paint can. This opening will allow for the insertion of the platform and coil.
  2. Create the Platform: Cut a piece of wire mesh or hardware cloth to fit inside the larger can. Attach legs to the platform to support it off the bottom of the can.
  3. Make the Copper Coil: Wrap the copper tubing around a pencil or other cylindrical object to create a coil. Place the coil on top of the platform.
  4. Insert Charcoal and Close the Lid: Place charcoal briquettes around the coil and close the lid of the larger can.
  5. Prepare the Smaller Can: Drill several small holes around the rim of the smaller paint can. Insert capped plastic tubing into each hole.
  6. Connect the Cans: Connect the smaller can to the larger can using one of the plastic tubes. This will create a passage for water and steam flow.
  7. Insert the Piston: Insert a dowel rod into the smaller can. Attach a skewer to the end of the rod to act as a piston.
  8. Heat the Charcoal: Ignite the charcoal briquettes to produce steam.
  9. Observe the Movement: As steam builds up, it will push against the piston, causing it to move up and down. This movement can be used to drive other mechanisms or toys.

Precautions:

  • Always exercise caution when handling the steam engine, as it can reach high temperatures.
  • Never operate the steam engine indoors or near flammable materials.
  • Keep children and teenagers away from the engine when it is operating.
