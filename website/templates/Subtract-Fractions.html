Method 1: Finding the Least Common Multiple (LCM)

Step 1: Determine the Least Common Multiple

If the numbers below the fractions (denominators) are different, we need to find the smallest number that both denominators can divide into evenly. This number is called the Least Common Multiple (LCM).

  • Example: Find the LCM of 4 and 6.
    • Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, ...
    • Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, ...
    • The smallest number that both 4 and 6 can divide evenly is 12. So, the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

Step 2: Make the Denominators Equal

Once you have found the LCM, multiply the top and bottom of each fraction by a number that makes the denominator equal to the LCM.

  • Example: Convert 1/4 and 3/6 to fractions with a denominator of 12.
    • Multiply 1/4 by 3/3 (which is equivalent to 1): 1/4 * 3/3 = 3/12
    • Multiply 3/6 by 2/2: 3/6 * 2/2 = 6/12

Step 3: Subtract the Numerators

Now that the denominators are the same, subtract the top numbers (numerators) of the fractions to find the difference.

  • Example: Subtract 3/12 from 6/12:
    • 6/12 - 3/12 = 3/12

Simplified Result:

3/12 can be simplified further by reducing the fraction to its lowest terms. Since 3 and 12 share a common factor of 3, we can divide both the numerator and denominator by 3.

  • 3/12 ÷ 3/3 = 1/4

Therefore, the difference between 1/4 and 3/6 is 1/4.

Method 2: Subtracting Mixed Numbers

Step 1: Convert Mixed Numbers to Improper Fractions

Mixed numbers, such as 2 3/4, have a whole number part and a fraction part. To subtract mixed numbers, we need to turn them into improper fractions with bigger top numbers than bottom numbers.

  • Example: Convert 2 3/4 into an improper fraction.
    • Multiply the whole number part (2) by the denominator (4): 2 * 4 = 8
    • Add the result (8) to the numerator (3): 8 + 3 = 11
    • The improper fraction is 11/4

Step 2: Find the Least Common Denominator (LCD)

Similar to the LCM, the LCD is the smallest number that both denominators can divide into evenly.

  • Example: Find the LCD of 4 and 12.
    • Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, ...
    • Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48, ...
    • The smallest number that both 4 and 12 can divide evenly is 12.

Step 3: Make All Fractions Have the Same Denominator

Multiply the top and bottom of each fraction by a number that makes the denominator equal to the LCD.

  • Example: Convert 11/4 and 5/12 to fractions with a denominator of 12.
    • Multiply 11/4 by 3/3: 11/4 * 3/3 = 33/12
    • Multiply 5/12 by 1/1: 5/12 * 1/1 = 5/12

Step 4: Subtract the Numerators

Now that the denominators are the same, subtract the top numbers (numerators) of the fractions to find the difference.

  • Example: Subtract 5/12 from 33/12:
    • 33/12 - 5/12 = 28/12

Simplified Result:

28/12 can be simplified further by reducing the fraction to its lowest terms. Since 28 and 12 share a common factor of 4, we can divide both the numerator and denominator by 4.

  • 28/12 ÷ 4/4 = 7/3

Therefore, the difference between 2 3/4 and 5/12 is 7/3.
