Get Car Support: A Comprehensive Guide to Fixing Struts

Struggling to fix your car's shaky handling and worn-out suspension? Look no further! Our expert guide will guide you through the process of replacing your car's struts, step-by-step. From locating the strut to installing a new one, we've got you covered. Get ready to empower yourself and restore your car's smooth and safe ride!


Step-by-Step Guide to Fixing a Strut

Part 1: Exposing the Strut

  1. Locate the Strut: Identify the strut, a cylinder-shaped component with a spring surrounding it. It's typically located near the wheel well.
  2. Lift the Car: Use a jack to raise the vehicle and place a jack stand securely beneath the chassis for safety. Remove the affected wheel.
  3. Disconnect Brake Line Bracket (if applicable): If your vehicle has a bracket holding the brake line to the strut, unbolt and detach it.
  4. Remove Sway Bar Attachments (if applicable): If the strut is connected to any metal stabilizer bars (called sway bars), unbolt and remove them temporarily.

Part 2: Removing the Old Strut

  1. Disconnect Steering Knuckle Bolts: Locate the bolts that connect the strut to the steering knuckle (the part that connects the wheel to the suspension). Carefully remove these bolts.
  2. Remove Top Mount Bolts: Open the car's hood and find the bolts located at the top of the strut where it mounts to the vehicle's body. Remove these bolts.
  3. Extract Old Strut: With the bolts removed, carefully pull the old strut out of its housing.

Part 3: Installing a New Strut

  1. Insert New Strut: Position the new strut into the housing and align it properly.
  2. Tighten Steering Knuckle Bolts: Secure the new strut to the steering knuckle by tightening the bolts you removed earlier.
  3. Mount Strut to Body: Insert the strut into the top mount and hand-tighten the bolts.
  4. Reattach Sway Bar and Brake Line Support: If necessary, reconnect the sway bar attachments and brake line support bracket.
  5. Install Wheel: Put the wheel back on and tighten the lug nuts securely.
  6. Test Drive: After reassembling the components, take a short test drive to ensure everything is functioning as intended.

Tips:

  • Strut Spring Compression: To remove the old strut, you may need to compress the spring. If you're not confident in performing this step, consider purchasing a pre-assembled "quick-strut" unit that comes with the spring already compressed.
  • Replace in Pairs: It's recommended to replace both struts on the same axle (front or rear) at the same time, even if only one is causing issues. This ensures equal performance and handling on both sides of the vehicle.
  • Professional Assistance: If you're unsure about any steps in the process or feel uncomfortable working on your car, consult a qualified mechanic for assistance.

Conclusion:

By following these steps, you can effectively fix your car's struts and restore its performance. Remember to prioritize safety by using proper equipment and seeking professional assistance if needed. Regular strut maintenance is crucial for ensuring a smooth and comfortable driving experience, reducing tire wear, and enhancing overall vehicle handling. By investing a little time and effort into this DIY project, you can save money while ensuring your car remains in top condition.
