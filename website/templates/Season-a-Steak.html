Step 1: Prepare the Steak

Rest at Room Temperature:

Allow the steak to rest at room temperature for 30-60 minutes before cooking. This allows the steak to relax, promoting even cooking throughout. As the meat comes to room temperature, its muscles release tension, resulting in a more tender and juicy steak.

Pat Dry and Season with Salt:

Pat the steak dry with paper towels to remove any excess moisture. This moisture can hinder the seasoning from adhering to the steak and prevent proper searing. Generously season the steak with salt on both sides, using about 3/4-1 teaspoon of salt per pound. Salt draws out the steak's natural juices, enhancing its flavor and creating a flavorful crust.

Allow Salt to Penetrate:

Let the seasoned steak rest for at least 40 minutes before cooking. This allows the salt to penetrate deeply into the steak, enhancing its flavor throughout. During this resting period, the salt dissolves, creating a brine that helps break down the meat's proteins, resulting in a more tender texture.

Apply a Brushed Coat of Olive Oil:

Brush a small amount of olive oil over the seasoned steak. Olive oil helps to prevent the steak from sticking to the grill or pan and ensures an evenly cooked surface. Additionally, it imparts a subtle richness and helps the steak achieve a beautiful golden-brown crust.

Step 2: Choose Seasonings (Optional)

While seasoning with salt is essential, you can further enhance the steak's flavor with a variety of seasonings:

Montreal Steak Seasoning:

  • 1 tablespoon crushed black pepper
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon coriander seed
  • 1/2 teaspoon dill weed
  • 1/4 teaspoon crushed red pepper flakes

This classic blend combines bold flavors and spices to create a savory and aromatic crust.

Turmeric-Based Blend:

  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon paprika
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon onion powder
  • 1 tablespoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric

Turmeric adds a vibrant yellow hue and an earthy, slightly spicy flavor to the steak. This blend is well-suited for those who prefer a warm and exotic taste profile.

Coffee Rub:

  • 2 tablespoons kosher salt
  • 2 tablespoons black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon coriander
  • 1 tablespoon mustard powder
  • 1 tablespoon coarsely ground coffee
  • 1 tablespoon cocoa powder
  • 1 tablespoon ancho chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon cloves
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper

This unique blend incorporates the bold flavor of coffee and a hint of spice. It creates a rich and flavorful crust that is sure to impress.

Step 3: Cooking Methods

Choose the cooking method that best suits your preferences and the desired level of doneness:

Grilling:

  • Use charcoal if possible, as it imparts a smoky flavor to the steak.
  • Create different heat zones on the grill for optimal results. This allows you to sear the steak over high heat and move it to a lower heat zone to finish cooking to the desired doneness.
  • Sear the steak for a few minutes on each side to create a flavorful crust, then move it to a cooler zone to continue cooking.

Pan-frying:

  • Heat a heavy-bottomed pan or skillet over high heat.
  • Sear the steak for 2-3 minutes on each side to create a crispy exterior.
  • Reduce heat to medium and continue cooking for the desired doneness, flipping the steak occasionally.

Broiling:

  • Preheat the oven's broiler to the highest setting.
  • Place the steak on a broiler pan and place it on the top rack of the oven.
  • Broil for 5-7 minutes on each side, or until cooked to the desired doneness.
  • Keep a close eye on the steak to prevent burning.

Oven and Pan:

  • Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C).
  • Sear the steak in a pan over high heat for 2-3 minutes on each side.
  • Transfer the steak to the preheated oven and cook for 10-12 minutes, or until cooked to the desired doneness.
  • This method combines the crisp exterior created by pan-frying with the even cooking of a roasted steak.
