What is a Magic Square?

A magic square is a grid of numbers arranged in such a way that the sum of each row, column, and diagonal is equal to the same constant value, known as the "magic constant." This enchanting arrangement of numbers has fascinated mathematicians and puzzle enthusiasts for centuries.

Solving a Magic Square

Solving a magic square is an intriguing mathematical challenge that requires a specific set of rules depending on whether the square has an odd or even number of rows and columns.

Odd-Numbered Squares:

To solve odd-numbered squares, follow these steps:

  1. Calculate the Magic Constant: Multiply the number of rows (or columns) by half the sum of the square of that number and 1. This formula can be expressed as S = n[(n^2+1)/2].
  2. Start in the Center: Place the number 1 in the center square of the top row.
  3. Up-One, Right-One Pattern: Continue filling the squares in an up-one, right-one pattern. If you hit a square that's already filled, move down one row and continue right.

Doubly Even Magic Squares:

For doubly even magic squares, follow these steps:

  1. Divide by 4: Divide the square into four equal parts.
  2. Calculate the Magic Constant: Multiply the number of rows (or columns) by half the sum of that number and 1. Divide this value by 2 to get the magic constant: S = [n(n^2+1)]/2.
  3. Create Highlights: Mark the four corners of the square and the center square.
  4. Fill in Highlighted Areas: Starting from the top left highlighted corner, fill in the highlighted areas using the numbers 1 to n^2 in increasing order.
  5. Count Backward: To fill in the remaining squares, count backward from n^2 to 1.

Singly Even Magic Squares:

Solving singly even magic squares involves a combination of steps:

  1. Divide by 2: Divide the square into two equal parts.
  2. Calculate the Magic Constant: Multiply the number of rows (or columns) by half the sum of that number and 1. Divide this value by 2 to get the magic constant: S = [n(n^2+1)]/2.
  3. Divide into Quadrants: Divide the square into four quadrants.
  4. Assign Number Ranges: Assign a range of numbers to each quadrant.
  5. Solve Quadrants: Solve each quadrant using the up-one, right-one pattern.
  6. Swap Highlights: Create highlights in quadrants A and D and swap their positions. For squares larger than 6x6, also swap the rightmost columns in quadrants B and C.
