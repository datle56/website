Bleaching Black Hair to Blonde: A Comprehensive Guide

Bleaching black hair to blonde is an exciting transformation that requires meticulous preparation, careful execution, and proper aftercare. Embarking on this journey requires patience and an understanding of the process to achieve the desired results while minimizing damage.

Prepping Your Hair

Before subjecting your black locks to the bleaching process, it's crucial to prepare your hair to withstand the chemical treatment. Here are some preparatory steps to follow:

  • Deep Conditioning: Regularly deep condition your hair with nourishing hair masks to restore moisture and strengthen it from within. This will help prevent breakage and dryness during the bleaching process.
  • Clarifying Shampoo: A few days before bleaching, use a clarifying shampoo to remove any existing product buildup, color treatments, or styling residue. This ensures that the bleach will interact directly with the natural hair pigments.
  • Strand Test: To determine the appropriate bleach strength for your hair type and color, perform a strand test on an inconspicuous area. This will help you avoid over-processing and potential damage.

Bleaching: Round One

With your hair well-prepared, you can now proceed with the first round of bleaching:

  • Sectioning: Divide your hair into four distinct sections using clips to simplify the application process.
  • Bleach Mixture: In a non-metallic bowl, mix the bleach powder with the developer according to the manufacturer's guidelines. The developer's volume determines the hair's lifting power, so choose a lower volume (e.g., 20-volume) for dark hair to minimize damage.
  • Application: Using a tint brush, apply the bleach to the ends of your hair first. Work your way gradually upwards, avoiding direct application to the roots. This will create a natural-looking gradient.
  • Processing Time: Leave the bleach on for 30-40 minutes. Monitor the hair closely during processing to check its progress and avoid over-processing, which can lead to breakage.
  • Rinsing and Post-Treatment: Once the desired level of lift is achieved, thoroughly rinse the bleach from your hair. Use a bleach-specific shampoo and conditioner to gently cleanse and nourish the hair after the chemical treatment.

Bleaching: Round Two (Optional)

Depending on the starting darkness of your hair, you may need multiple rounds of bleaching to achieve the desired blonde shade. However, it's crucial to allow a resting period of 2-4 weeks between bleaching sessions to give your hair time to recover.

  • Second Round Bleaching: Repeat the bleaching process as described in Round One, using a lower-volume developer (e.g., 10-volume) to minimize further damage.
  • Toning: After the second round of bleaching, apply a toner to neutralize any unwanted brassy tones and achieve the desired blonde shade.

  • Heat Styling: Avoid excessive heat styling, such as blow-drying or straightening, as it can damage bleached hair. Opt for air-drying or use heat protectants when heat styling is necessary.
  • Root Touch-Ups: As your hair grows, dark roots will become visible. Touch up your roots every 4-6 weeks to maintain a seamless blonde look.
  • Hydrating Hair Masks: Incorporate a hydrating hair mask into your weekly hair care routine to replenish moisture and prevent hair breakage.
