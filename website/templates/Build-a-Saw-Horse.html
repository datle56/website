How to Build Your Own Saw Horses: A Comprehensive Guide

Introduction:

Saw horses are invaluable tools for woodworking projects, providing a sturdy and adjustable platform for cutting and shaping lumber. Building your own saw horses is a relatively simple task that can save you money and give you the satisfaction of building a useful tool with your own hands. This detailed guide will provide you with all the steps and information you need to create your own durable and functional saw horses.

Materials:

  • 2 or 4 lumber boards (2x6 pine or 2x4 lodgepole pine is recommended)
  • Circular saw
  • Tape measure
  • Angle square
  • Hammer
  • Nails

Choosing the Right Lumber:

The type of lumber you choose will determine the strength and durability of your saw horses. 2x6 pine or lodgepole pine is a good option for strength and ease of handling.

Step-by-Step Instructions:

1. Cutting the Legs:

  • Wear safety glasses and earplugs before operating any power tools.
  • Measure and mark the desired height of the saw horse legs on the lumber boards. A height of 34-36 inches is recommended for a 6-foot person.
  • Using a circular saw, cut the lumber into 4 equal-length legs.
  • Mark and cut a 65-degree bevel on the bottom of each leg using an angle square and a circular saw. The bevel will ensure that the saw horses are stable on uneven surfaces.

2. Creating the Top Board:

  • Measure and cut a board for the top of the saw horses that is about 42-48 inches long.
  • Mark and cut square lines about an inch from each end of the top board to serve as attachment points for the legs.

3. Assembling the Legs:

  • Place two of the legs on a flat surface, bevel side up.
  • Align the legs with the marked attachment points on the top board.
  • Using a hammer and nails, secure the legs to the top board.
  • Flip the assembly over and repeat the process with the remaining two legs, ensuring that they are opposite the first pair.

4. Creating the Spreader Braces:

  • Measure and cut two boards that are approximately 8 inches long.
  • Scribe the boards to fit snugly between the legs, forming a horizontal brace.
  • Secure the braces to the legs using nails.

5. Adding the Diagonal Braces:

  • Measure and cut four more boards that are about 18 inches long.
  • Using an angle square, mark and cut a 45-degree angle on one end of each board.
  • Scribe the other end of each board to fit the top board.
  • Align the diagonal braces with the attachment points on the top board and secure them with nails. The braces should form a triangular shape.

6. Trimming the Legs:

  • Place the saw horses on a level surface.
  • Check if the legs are equal in length and fit evenly on the ground. If any legs are uneven, trim them accordingly.

Customization:

  • You can customize the size, height, and weight capacity of your saw horses to suit your specific needs.
  • For larger and heavier workpieces, use thicker lumber and increase the number of diagonal braces.
  • To create adjustable saw horses, cut the legs longer and use a bolt and wing nut system to adjust the height.

Conclusion:

Remember to prioritize safety by wearing appropriate protective gear and operating power tools responsibly.
