How to Seal Granite Countertops: A Comprehensive Guide

Granite countertops are a popular choice for kitchens and bathrooms due to their durability and aesthetic appeal. However, like all natural stone surfaces, granite is porous and susceptible to staining and damage from water and other liquids. Sealing your granite countertops is essential to maintain their beauty and protect them from these potential hazards.

Step 1: Check if Sealing is Needed

Before applying a sealer, it's important to determine if your granite countertops actually need it. Here's a simple test:

  • Place a few drops of water on the granite surface.
  • Wait for 15 minutes.

If the water beads up and remains on the surface, your countertops do not need sealing. However, if the water absorbs into the stone and leaves a dark spot, it's time to seal.

Step 2: Choose a Sealer

When choosing a sealer for your granite countertops, it's crucial to select a product specifically designed for natural stone, especially granite. There are two main types of sealers:

  • Penetrating Sealers: These sealers soak into the pores of the granite, creating a protective barrier without altering the appearance or finish of the stone.
  • Surface Sealers: These sealers form a thin film on the surface of the granite, providing protection but potentially affecting the look and feel of the stone.

Penetrating sealers are generally preferred for granite countertops as they provide long-lasting protection without changing the aesthetic qualities of the stone.

Step 3: Prepare the Granite

Before applying the sealer, the granite surface must be thoroughly cleaned to remove any dirt, grease, or other contaminants that could interfere with the bonding process. Follow these steps:

  • Wash the granite with a mild dish soap and water solution.
  • Use a degreasing cleaner to eliminate any oily residues.
  • Rinse the granite thoroughly with clean water and allow it to dry completely for at least 24 hours.

Step 4: Apply the Sealer

Once the granite is dry, it's time to apply the sealer. Wear gloves and ensure proper ventilation in the area where you're working.

  • Spread a thin, even layer of sealer over the granite surface using a spray bottle or a soft cloth.
  • Allow the sealer to penetrate the stone for approximately 20 minutes.

Step 5: Apply a Second Coat (Optional)

For optimal protection, especially in high-traffic areas or for heavily used countertops, you may consider applying a second coat of sealer.

  • Allow the first coat to dry almost completely, but not fully.
  • Apply a second coat in the same manner as the first, ensuring even coverage.

Step 6: Remove Excess Sealer

After the specified time has elapsed, wipe up any excess sealer from the surface of the granite using a clean cloth.

Step 7: Let it Cure

Allow the sealer to cure undisturbed for 48 hours before touching or using the countertops. During this curing period, avoid placing anything heavy or sharp on the surface.

Maintenance

To maintain the effectiveness of the sealer and protect your granite countertops, follow these maintenance tips:

  • Clean the countertops regularly using a mild cleaner designed for natural stone.
  • Use ioSeal Protectant products to enhance the protection provided by the sealer.
  • Re-seal the countertops as needed, depending on the type of granite and the level of usage. A general rule of thumb is to re-seal every 6 months to 2 years.

By following these comprehensive steps, you can effectively seal your granite countertops, protect them from stains and damage, and ensure their beauty and durability for years to come.
