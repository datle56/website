Unlocking the Secrets of Completing the Square

In the realm of algebra, quadratic equations often pose a challenge. But fear not, for there's a secret technique that can tame these equations and unveil their hidden roots: completing the square.

Imagine a square, its sides perfectly aligned, a symbol of harmony and balance. Completing the square in algebra is much like perfecting this geometrical shape, transforming a disjointed quadratic equation into a well-balanced and elegant form. By adding or subtracting specific terms, we can reshape the equation into a perfect square that can be easily solved.

Prepare yourself for a mathematical adventure as we embark on the steps of completing the square, revealing the hidden treasures of quadratic equations.


Completing the Square: A Comprehensive Guide

Introduction

Completing the square is an algebraic technique that allows us to simplify quadratic equations and find their roots. It involves transforming a quadratic equation into a form that reveals its vertex and facilitates the determination of its solutions.

Step-by-Step Procedure

Step 1: Rewrite the Equation in Standard Form

Write the quadratic equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are real numbers and a ≠ 0.

Step 2: Move the Constant Term

Subtract the constant term (c) from both sides of the equation. This isolates the quadratic terms on one side and the zero on the other side.

Step 3: Halve and Square the Coefficient of x

Take half of the coefficient of x (b/2) and square it. This is a key step in the process of completing the square.

Step 4: Add and Subtract the Square

Add the squared term (b²/4a) to both sides of the equation. This term represents the square of half the coefficient of x.

Then, subtract the same term from inside the parentheses on the left-hand side. This ensures that the equation remains balanced.

Step 5: Factor the Square

The terms inside the parentheses on the left-hand side can now be factored as (x ± b/2a)². This is a perfect square trinomial, which is a trinomial that can be written as a square of a binomial.

Step 6: Solve for x

Take the square root of both sides of the equation. This introduces the square root of the constant term (c/a) and produces two solutions: x = (b/2a) ± √(b²/4a - c/a).

Example

Let's complete the square for the equation x² + 6x - 7 = 0.

Step 1: Rewrite: x² + 6x = 7 Step 2: Move the Constant: x² + 6x - 7 = 0 Step 3: Halve and Square: (3)² = 9 Step 4: Add and Subtract: x² + 6x + 9 - 9 = 7 Step 5: Factor Square: (x + 3)² - 9 = 7 Step 6: Solve: x + 3 = ±4; x = -3 ± 4

Therefore, the solutions to the equation x² + 6x - 7 = 0 are x = -7 and x = 1.

Significance

Completing the square is a valuable technique in algebra for the following reasons:

  • It simplifies quadratic equations, making them easier to solve.
  • It reveals the vertex of the associated parabola, which is useful in graphing and optimization problems.
  • It allows for the determination of the roots of any quadratic equation, regardless of whether they are real or complex.
  • It is a basis for other techniques, such as the quadratic formula and solving quadratic inequalities.

Conclusion

Completing the square is a valuable technique for solving quadratic equations. By manipulating the equation algebraically, it allows us to transform a complex quadratic expression into a simpler form, revealing its solutions. This process is particularly useful for equations that cannot be easily factored or solved by other methods. Understanding and applying the steps of completing the square empowers you to tackle a wide range of algebraic problems, including finding the roots of parabolas and determining the maximum or minimum points of quadratic functions.
