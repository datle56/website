Simplify Your TV Setup: A Comprehensive Guide to Concealing Cables with Wall Mounts and In-Wall Power Extenders

Enhance your viewing experience and minimize clutter by mounting your TV on the wall. However, managing the unsightly cables that accompany it can be daunting. This comprehensive guide provides step-by-step instructions, tips, and warnings to help you conceal TV cables effectively using wall mounts and in-wall power extenders.

Part 1: Installing the Wall Mount

  1. Selecting the Ideal Mount: Choose a wall mount that is compatible with your TV's size, weight, and intended placement. Consider a full-motion mount for maximum adjustability.

  2. Determining the Mounting Spot: Find an eye-level position for your TV and mark the center of the screen on the wall. Ensure the location provides a clear line of sight to your preferred viewing spot.

  3. Locating Studs: Use a stud finder to identify the locations of studs behind the wall. Studs provide secure support for the mount.

  4. Pre-Drilling Holes: Create pilot holes for the mount screws where the studs are located. This will make it easier to insert the screws securely.

  5. Utilizing Toggle Bolts: If no studs are present, use toggle bolts to anchor the mount securely to the drywall. Toggle bolts expand behind the wall, providing a stable hold.

  6. Mounting the Bracket: Align the mount with the pre-drilled holes or toggle bolts and tighten the screws by hand. Secure them firmly with a screwdriver.

Part 2: Wiring the In-Wall Power Extender

  1. Purchasing the Extender: Obtain an in-wall power extender that provides an additional outlet behind the TV, eliminating the need for bulky power cords running down the wall.

  2. Marking the Receptacle Locations: Draw an outline around the existing receptacle on the wall where the extender will be installed. This will ensure a precise cut for the drywall.

  3. Cutting the Drywall: Use a drywall saw or utility knife to carefully cut along the outline. Exercise caution while cutting to minimize damage to the surrounding drywall.

  4. Passing the Cables: Feed the power connector cable and any necessary AV cords through the hole in the drywall. Ensure there is ample slack in the cables to allow for adjustments and future servicing.

  5. Connecting the Power Connectors: Snap the power connectors from the receptacles together to extend the circuit. Ensure the connections are secure to prevent power interruptions.

  6. Inserting the Receptacle: Push the in-wall receptacle into the drywall hole and screw it in place. This will provide a flush and secure fit.

Part 3: Connecting the TV and Devices

  1. Mounting the TV Brackets: Align the vertical brackets with the screw holes on the back of the TV and fasten them securely. These brackets will support the TV's weight on the wall mount.

  2. Securing the TV to the Mount: Lift the TV and carefully set the brackets on the wall mount. Align the screws and tighten them to support the TV securely.

  3. Connecting the TV's Power Cord: Plug the power cord into the upper receptacle outlet on the in-wall extender. This will power the TV.

  4. Connecting the Extension Cable: Use the extension cable to connect the lower receptacle outlet on the extender to a nearby wall outlet. This will provide additional power to the in-wall receptacle.

  5. Plugging in the AV Cables: Connect the AV cords from the upper receptacle outlet to the matching ports on the back of the TV. Then, plug the lower ends into your devices, such as the DVD player, streaming box, or gaming console.

Tips:

  • Consider using a cord cover to conceal the cables that cannot be routed through the wall. This can be especially useful for vertical cables running down from the TV's power cord to the floor.

  • Place a small shelf or stand under the TV to hide the lower receptacle and provide storage space for devices like DVDs and remote controls.

Warnings:

  • Never route the TV's power cable behind the walls due to potential fire hazards. Keep the power cord visible and accessible for safety reasons.

  • Avoid mounting your TV over a fireplace, as smoke and soot from the fire can damage the TV and its components.


Conclusion

With the cables hidden, your viewing experience will be enhanced, and your room will exude an air of sophistication and organization. Embrace the art of cable concealment and let the pristine display of your TV take center stage.
