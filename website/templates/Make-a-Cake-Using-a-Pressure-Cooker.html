Method 1: Prepare the Batter and Pan

Baking the Perfect Cake in a Pressure Cooker

Step 1: Prepare the Cake Batter

To begin, meticulously follow the instructions outlined in your preferred cake recipe or prepare a box mix according to its specified guidelines. Ensure that the batter achieves the desired consistency, whether it's thick and decadent or light and airy.

Step 2: Select and Grease the Baking Pan

Choose a baking pan made of metal, Pyrex glass, or silicone that fits snugly within your pressure cooker. The pan should be of an appropriate size to accommodate the batter without causing spillage.

Step 3: Grease the Baking Pan

Thoroughly grease the interior of the baking pan using cooking spray or butter. This will prevent the cake from sticking and facilitate easy removal after baking. To ensure even cooking, lightly sprinkle the greased pan with flour. Gently shake the pan to distribute the flour evenly over the bottom and sides.

Step 4: Pour and Smooth the Batter

Gently pour the prepared cake batter into the greased and floured baking pan. Use a spatula or the back of a spoon to smooth out the batter, ensuring a level and even surface. This will promote uniform baking and a visually appealing finished cake.

Method 2: Stove-Top Pressure Cooker

Step 1: Remove Pressure Cooker Components

Before using a stove-top pressure cooker, carefully remove the gasket and whistle. The gasket is a rubber seal that sits around the lid, and the whistle is a safety feature that indicates when the desired pressure has been reached. Removing these components is essential for this method.

Step 2: Insulate the Pressure Cooker

Sprinkle approximately 1 1/2 cups of coarse salt evenly over the bottom of the pressure cooker. This layer of salt serves as insulation, protecting the cake from direct heat and preventing it from burning.

Step 3: Position the Cake Pan

Place a sturdy stand or trivet inside the pressure cooker, ensuring that it can elevate the cake pan about 2-3 inches above the bottom. This step is crucial to allow for proper steam circulation and even baking.

Step 4: Preheat the Pressure Cooker

Cover the empty pressure cooker and heat it over high heat for 2 minutes. This preheating step creates the initial steam pressure necessary for the baking process.

Step 5: Place the Cake Pan and Lid

Carefully place the prepared cake pan on the trivet inside the preheated pressure cooker. Immediately secure the pressure cooker lid by locking it into place. Ensure the lid fits tightly to prevent steam from escaping.

Step 6: Adjust Heat and Cooking Time

Reduce the heat to medium and cook the cake on high pressure for 5 minutes. This initial burst of high pressure will help the cake rise quickly. After 5 minutes, lower the heat to low and continue baking for the time specified in your cake recipe.

Step 7: Check for Doneness

Once the baking time is complete, turn off the heat and let the pressure cooker cool naturally for 10 minutes. This allows the pressure to release gradually, preventing the cake from deflating. After 10 minutes, release any remaining pressure by using the pressure release valve.

Step 8: Test and Remove the Cake

Carefully open the pressure cooker lid and insert a toothpick into the center of the cake. If the toothpick comes out clean, the cake is done. If it comes out with batter or crumbs attached, continue baking in increments of 2-3 minutes until the toothpick test is successful.

Method 3: Electric Pressure Cooker

Step 1: Prepare the Insert and Trivet

Pour 1 cup of water into the insert of the electric pressure cooker. Place a trivet or steamer basket in the bottom of the insert, ensuring that it is elevated above the water level. This will prevent the cake from becoming soggy.

Step 2: Position the Cake Pan

Place the prepared cake pan on the trivet inside the electric pressure cooker. Close the lid and ensure it is securely sealed.

Step 3: Set the Cooking Mode and Time

Set the electric pressure cooker to the "Cake" or "High" setting. Adjust the temperature setting according to the type of cake being baked:

  • Lighter cakes, such as angel food or sponge cake, require a lower temperature setting.
  • Standard cakes, such as pound cake or chocolate cake, require a normal temperature setting.
  • Denser cakes, such as cheesecake or carrot cake, require a higher temperature setting.

Set the baking time to half the normal time specified in your cake recipe. For example, if the recipe calls for baking at 350°F for 30 minutes in a conventional oven, set the electric pressure cooker for 15 minutes.

Step 4: Natural Pressure Release

After the cooking time is complete, allow the electric pressure cooker to naturally release pressure for 10 minutes. This gradual release prevents the cake from deflating. After 10 minutes, manually release any remaining pressure using the pressure release valve.

Step 5: Check for Doneness

Open the lid and insert a toothpick into the center of the cake. If the toothpick comes out clean, the cake is done. If it comes out with batter or crumbs attached, continue baking in increments of 2-3 minutes until the toothpick test is successful.
