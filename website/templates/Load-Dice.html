Methods of Weighted Dice Creation

Crafting weighted dice requires a keen understanding of the physical principles that govern their behavior. Several techniques have emerged over time, each offering unique advantages and challenges.

Drilling Method: Precise Placement, Lasting Results

The drilling method involves creating a precisely positioned cavity on the opposite side of the desired outcome number. This cavity is then filled with a heavier material, such as a nail or lead pellets, effectively shifting the center of gravity towards the favored outcome. To conceal the modification, the hole is meticulously glued shut and painted to match the original die's appearance.

Melting Method: Heat Manipulation for Subtle Alterations

The melting method harnesses the transformative power of heat to adjust the center of gravity within the die. A die is placed with the desired outcome number facing upwards and heated in an oven at 250°F (121°C) for 10 minutes. The die is then rapidly cooled in icy water, causing the plastic to contract and redistribute its mass. This process subtly enhances the probability of the desired outcome while maintaining the die's overall appearance.

Variable-Load Method: Customizing Weight Distribution

The variable-load method offers greater control over the die's weight distribution. This method involves drilling out multiple dots on one side of the die to create a hollow interior. A cubic-shaped cavity is then carved out within this interior, providing a space for weighted material. Lead pellets are gradually added to the die, with their placement carefully manipulated to influence the outcome. The process is completed by sealing all but one of the drilled holes and filling the remaining space with a moldable mixture of paraffin and coconut oil.
