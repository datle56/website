Understanding Half-Life

Half-life is a fundamental concept in the fields of radioactive decay, chemistry, and other disciplines. It refers to the time it takes for half of a substance to undergo a transformation or decay into a different substance or form. Half-life plays a vital role in determining the stability and safety of radioactive materials, as well as in archaeological dating techniques.

Key Features of Half-Life

  • Independence from External Factors: The half-life of a substance is determined solely by its intrinsic properties and is independent of temperature, concentration, or other external factors. This makes half-life a reliable and predictable measure of decay rates.
  • Exponential Decay: Half-life follows an exponential decay pattern, meaning that the amount of a substance remaining after a given time period decreases exponentially over time.
  • Carbon Dating Application: Half-life is extensively used in carbon dating, a technique for determining the age of organic materials based on the measurement of their carbon-14 content. The half-life of carbon-14 is approximately 5,730 years, allowing scientists to estimate the age of artifacts and fossils up to several tens of thousands of years old.

Calculating Half-Life

Calculating the half-life of a substance is straightforward using the following formula:

N(t) = N0(1/2)^t/t1/2

where:

  • N(t) is the amount of the substance remaining at time "t"
  • N0 is the initial amount of the substance
  • t is the time elapsed since the initial measurement
  • t1/2 is the half-life of the substance

Example:

Suppose a substance initially has an amount of 250 grams. After 100 days, the amount remaining is 62.5 grams. To calculate the half-life:

N(t) = N0(1/2)^t/t1/2 62.5 = 250(1/2)^100/t1/2 t1/2 = 50 days

Therefore, the half-life of this substance is 50 days.

Using a Graph to Determine Half-Life

A graphical representation of radioactive decay can be used to determine the half-life of a substance. To do this, follow these steps:

  • Plot the count rate of the substance (the decay rate) on the y-axis against time on the x-axis.
  • Divide the initial count rate by 2 to find the halfway point.
  • Draw a horizontal line at the halfway point.
  • Draw a vertical line from the halfway point to the x-axis.
  • The point where the vertical line intersects the x-axis represents the half-life of the substance.

Example Problems

Problem:

A sample of radioactive element A has a half-life of 20 hours. If there are initially 1000 atoms of element A, how many atoms will remain after 40 hours?

Answer:

Using the half-life formula:

N(t) = N0(1/2)^t/t1/2 N(40) = 1000(1/2)^40/20 N(40) = 1000(1/1024) N(40) = 0.977 atoms

Problem:

The half-life of carbon-14 is approximately 5,730 years. If a fossil contains only one-eighth of the original carbon-14 present in living organisms, how old is the fossil?

Answer:

One-eighth of the original carbon-14 implies that three half-lives have passed:

t = 5730 years * 3 t = 17,190 years

Therefore, the fossil is approximately 17,190 years old.
