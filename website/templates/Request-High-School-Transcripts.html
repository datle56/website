Getting Free Copies of Your High School Transcript: A Comprehensive Guide

Public Schools (United States)

  • Online:

Explore your school's official website. Navigate to the alumni or student services section. Many public schools allow graduates to download unofficial transcripts for free. These transcripts may suffice for general purposes, such as employment applications.

  • Third-Party Services:

Services like Parchment and Need My Transcript facilitate transcript requests. These platforms charge a nominal processing fee (usually between $3 and $10). They can send transcripts electronically or by mail, depending on your preference.

  • In Person or by Mail:

Contact your local school district or registrar's office. Provide proof of identity (e.g., driver's license, passport) and indicate your graduation year. Transcript requests can be made in person or via mail.

Private Schools (United States)

  • Contact the School:

Locate your school's website or contact information through online search engines. Reach out directly to the school office to request a transcript. They may have specific procedures or fees associated with transcript distribution.

  • Third-Party Services:

Consider using online transcript services like Parchment. These platforms often offer processing options for private school transcripts. Pay the applicable fees for electronic or paper copies.

  • State Department of Education:

If your private school has closed or no longer operates, contact your state department of education. They may maintain records or provide guidance on how to obtain your transcript.

Schools in Canada

  • Online:

Several Canadian provinces offer online transcript request systems for public schools. Visit your province's Ministry of Education website for more information.

  • Contact the School:

If your school is still operating, contact the school directly. Provide your full name, graduation year, and the years you attended. They may have specific instructions or fees associated with transcript distribution.

  • School Board:

In the event that your school has closed, the local school board may have inherited your records. Contact the school board to inquire about the availability of your transcript.
