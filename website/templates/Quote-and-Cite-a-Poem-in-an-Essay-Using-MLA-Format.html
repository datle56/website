Unlocking the Secrets of MLA Poetry Citation: A Concise Guide

When it comes to incorporating poetry into your academic work, the MLA format serves as a beacon, guiding you through the intricacies of quoting and citing. Let's embark on a poetic journey as we navigate the essential elements of MLA poetry citation, ensuring that your words dance harmoniously with the voices of great poets.

This guide will illuminate the intricacies of citing short and long quotations, guiding you through the use of parentheses and line numbers. Additionally, it will delve into the nuances of in-text citations and the proper construction of the Works Cited page. By understanding these MLA conventions, you'll be equipped to weave poetry into your academic prose with precision and grace.


Summarizing the MLA Format for Quoting and Citing Poems

Quoting Poems

  • Titles of poems: Use quotation marks for the titles of poems, short stories, essays, and other short works that appear within a larger publication (e.g., "The Raven").
  • Short quotes (3 lines or less): Integrate short quotes directly into your text and separate lines with slash marks. (e.g., "And the silken, sad, uncertain rustling of each purple curtain / Thrilled me—filled me with fantastic terrors never felt before" (24-25)).

  • Long quotes (4 or more lines): Indent long quotes ten spaces from the left margin and omit quotation marks. Maintain double spacing and do not alter the line breaks in the original text. (e.g.,

    In the chambers of my palace, weeping, weeping, evermore I behold the ghostly legions That troop out of his portals, evermore

    Upon the floors of crumbling palaces, I mourn with fervor that the lips implore

    His eyes, radiant and enticing, that weeps in vain his loss immense

    For the love of whom I ever weeping stand in tears and wait and still adore

    (8-14))

  • Line numbers: Always include line numbers after quotes in parentheses.

Citing Poems

In-Text Citations:

  • Short quotes: Include line numbers in parentheses after short quotes. (e.g., "And the silken, sad, uncertain rustling of each purple curtain / Thrilled me—filled me with fantastic terrors never felt before" (Poe 24-25)).

  • Long quotes: Place the punctuation after the quoted material, not after the citation. (e.g.,

    In the chambers of my palace, weeping, weeping, evermore I behold the ghostly legions That troop out of his portals, evermore

    Upon the floors of crumbling palaces, I mourn with fervor that the lips implore

    His eyes, radiant and enticing, that weeps in vain his loss immense

    For the love of whom I ever weeping stand in tears and wait and still adore

    (Poe 8-14))

  • Author's name in introduction: If you mention the author's name in the introduction, do not repeat it in the parentheses. (e.g., "In his famous poem 'The Raven,' Poe vividly depicts the narrator's descent into madness." (Poe 24-25))

  • Individual words or phrases: Include line numbers after quoting individual words or phrases. (e.g., "The repetition of 'weeping' in lines 8 and 14 emphasizes the narrator's overwhelming grief" (Poe 8-14)).

Works Cited Page:

  • Poems in books:

    • Author's last name, first name. "Title of Poem." Title of Book, City of Publication: Publisher, Year of Publication, Page Range.
    • e.g., Poe, Edgar Allan. "The Raven." The Tell-Tale Heart and Other Stories, New York: Modern Library, 2002, pp. 78-80.

  • Poems online:

    • Author's last name, first name. "Title of Poem." Website Name, Date Posted, Medium (Web), Date Accessed.
    • e.g., Dickinson, Emily. "Hope" is the Thing with Feathers." Poetry Foundation, 1861, Web, 15 April 2023.

  • Poems in anthologies:

    • Author's last name, first name. "Title of Poem." Title of Anthology, edited by Editor's name. City of Publication: Publisher, Year of Publication, Page Range.
    • e.g., Whitman, Walt. "O Captain! My Captain!" The Norton Anthology of American Literature, edited by Nina Baym. New York: W.W. Norton & Company, 2013, pp. 1183-1185.

  • Multiple poems by the same author: Use three dashes (-) instead of the author's name for subsequent entries.

    • e.g.,
      • Poe, Edgar Allan. "The Raven." The Tell-Tale Heart and Other Stories, New York: Modern Library, 2002, pp. 78-80.
      • ---. "Sonnet—To Science." The Works of Edgar Allan Poe, New York: W.J. Widdleton, 1856, pp. 149-150.

Conclusion:

Mastering the MLA format for quoting and citing poems is crucial for academic writing. By adhering to these guidelines, you ensure accuracy, consistency, and clarity in your research and assignments. The detailed instructions provided in this summary empower you to seamlessly integrate poetic excerpts into your text and properly acknowledge the original author's work. Remember, proper citation not only demonstrates intellectual integrity but also enhances the credibility and reliability of your written content.
