Part 1: Changing Mixed Numbers to Improper Fractions

Converting mixed numbers, which are a combination of a whole number and a fraction, into improper fractions allows for easier manipulation and calculation. Here are the steps involved:

  1. Multiply the whole number by the denominator of the fraction: Take the whole number and multiply it by the denominator of the fraction. For example, if the mixed number is 2 1/3, you would multiply 2 by 3, resulting in 6.
  2. Add the numerator to the product: Next, add the numerator of the fraction to the product obtained in step 1. In the example of 2 1/3, you would add 1 to 6, resulting in 7.
  3. Divide the sum by the denominator: Finally, divide the sum obtained in step 2 by the denominator of the fraction. In the example, you would divide 7 by 3, resulting in an improper fraction of 7/3.

Part 2: Dividing Improper Fractions

Dividing improper fractions, which are fractions with a numerator greater than or equal to the denominator, involves flipping the second fraction to its reciprocal and multiplying.

  1. Flip the second fraction to make it its reciprocal: In division, the divisor is flipped so its numerator and denominator are swapped. For example, if you are dividing 5/6 by 3/4, you would flip 3/4 to become 4/3.
  2. Multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal: After flipping the second fraction, you multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal. In the example, you would multiply 5/6 by 4/3.
  3. Multiply the numerators and denominators together: The multiplication process involves multiplying the numerator of the first fraction by the numerator of the second fraction and the denominator of the first fraction by the denominator of the second fraction. In the example, this would be (5 x 4) for the numerators and (6 x 3) for the denominators.
  4. Simplify the answer, if possible: The final result is an improper fraction. If possible, simplify the fraction by finding the greatest common factor (GCF) of the numerator and denominator and dividing both by the GCF. In the example, the GCF is 1, so the answer remains 20/18.

Part 3: Changing Improper Fractions Back to Mixed Numbers

To convert improper fractions back into mixed numbers, follow these steps:

  1. Divide the numerator by the denominator: Divide the numerator of the improper fraction by its denominator. For example, if you have an improper fraction of 13/4, you would divide 13 by 4.
  2. The whole number is the quotient: The division will result in a quotient and a remainder. The quotient becomes the whole number portion of the mixed number. In the example, the quotient is 3.
  3. The fraction is the remainder over the original denominator: The remainder from the division becomes the numerator of the fractional portion of the mixed number. The denominator remains the same as the original improper fraction. In the example, the remainder is 1, so the fractional portion is 1/4.
  4. Combine the whole number and the fraction: The mixed number is created by combining the whole number and the fractional portion. In the example, the mixed number is 3 1/4.
