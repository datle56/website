Understanding Your Property Survey

When you acquire a piece of property, one of the most important documents you should obtain is a property survey. This legal document provides a detailed and accurate representation of your property's boundaries, features, and other pertinent information. Understanding your property survey is crucial for a variety of reasons, including:

  • Planning for construction projects
  • Buying or selling land
  • Resolving boundary disputes
  • Protecting your legal rights

A property survey consists of two main components: an illustration and a written report.

1. Illustration

The illustration resembles a map of your property. It typically includes the following:

  • Property boundaries: These are marked with angles measured in degrees, minutes, and seconds. For example, a boundary might be described as "N 38° 03' 09" E," indicating that the line runs north at an angle of 38 degrees, 3 minutes, and 9 seconds from true east.
  • Important features: The survey will indicate the location of any structures, water bodies, shared boundaries, roads, and other notable features on or adjacent to your property.
  • Scale and scope: The illustration will specify the scale of the drawing, which indicates the ratio of the distance represented on the map to the actual distance on the ground. It may also include a vicinity map showing the general location of your property in relation to neighboring areas.
  • Legend: The illustration will include a legend explaining the symbols used to represent various features on the map. For example, blue lines may indicate water, while red lines may represent property boundaries.

2. Written Report

The written report accompanying the illustration provides additional legal information and details about your property. It typically includes:

  • Property description: A legal description of the property's boundaries, typically based on a metes and bounds survey.
  • Easements: Any easements (rights-of-way or other permissions) granted to third parties over your property.
  • Improvements: A list of any improvements or structures located on the property, such as buildings, fences, or driveways.
  • Other notes: Additional information that may be relevant to the property, such as its ownership history or the presence of nearby properties with potential impacts.

Key Elements to Look For

When examining your property survey, pay attention to the following key elements:

  • Property boundaries: Verify that the boundaries are clearly marked and consistent with the legal description provided in the written report.
  • Easements: Be aware of any easements that may exist on your property. These could potentially restrict your use or development of the land.
  • Improvements: Ensure that all existing structures and improvements are accurately represented on the survey. This can help you avoid disputes with neighbors or potential buyers.
  • Notes: Read any notes included on the survey, as they may provide valuable information about your property or its history.

Using Your Survey

Your property survey is a valuable tool that can assist you in a variety of ways:

  • Understanding your property's boundaries: A survey provides an accurate representation of where your property ends and your neighbor's begins. This can prevent boundary disputes and help you plan for construction projects and additions.
  • Planning for building or buying land: A survey can help you identify the best location for a new building or addition, ensuring that you comply with setback requirements and other building codes.
  • Buying or selling land: When you are buying or selling land, a property survey can provide potential buyers or lenders with assurance that the boundaries and features of the property are accurately represented.
  • Legal protection: In the event of a boundary dispute, your property survey can serve as legal evidence of the location of your boundaries. This can help protect your property rights.

Tips for Understanding Your Survey

To fully understand your property survey:

  • Familiarize yourself with the legend: The legend will explain the symbols used on the illustration.
  • Locate the surveyor's certificate: This typically appears on the illustration and indicates the survey's validity and the surveyor's qualifications.
  • Contact your surveyor for any clarifications: If you have any questions or concerns about your survey, don't hesitate to contact the surveyor who conducted it.
  • Get a second opinion (optional): If you need additional assurance or clarification, you may consider getting a second opinion from another surveyor or a trusted friend or family member with survey expertise.

Conclusion:

Understanding your property survey is crucial for safeguarding your legal rights and making informed decisions about your property. By carefully examining the illustration and written report, you can gain valuable insights into your property's boundaries, easements, and other important features. Remember to consult with the surveyor or a qualified expert if you encounter any complexities or have questions. By comprehending and utilizing your property survey, you can ensure the accurate demarcation, proper utilization, and legal protection of your valuable asset.
