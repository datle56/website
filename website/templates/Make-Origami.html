Basic Shapes:

  • Heart: A timeless symbol of love, this shape is perfect for creating romantic decorations, homemade cards, or gift tags. Its simple folds make it accessible for all skill levels.

  • Fortune Teller: A playful and interactive origami creation, this shape is known for its ability to reveal hidden messages. Folding it step-by-step, you can create a miniature fortune teller that will delight friends and family.

  • Waterbomb Base: This versatile shape serves as the foundation for countless origami designs. It resembles a six-pointed star with alternating folds, providing a stable base for more complex models.

  • Airplane: A classic boredom-busting toy, this origami airplane flies with grace and agility. With a few simple folds and adjustments, you can create a miniature plane that soars through the air.

  • Star: This five-pointed shape adds a festive and whimsical touch to any occasion. Its sharp angles and geometric precision create a visually striking decoration that can be used as a centerpiece or ornament.

Flowers and Animals:

  • Lily: This elegant flower is renowned for its beauty and simplicity. With delicate petals and a graceful form, this origami lily makes a stunning addition to paper bouquets or floral arrangements.

  • Lotus Flower: A symbol of purity and enlightenment, this origami lotus flower captures the essence of the Japanese tradition. Its intricate folds and layered petals create a unique and authentic blossom.

  • Jumping Frog: This playful and interactive origami creation brings a touch of whimsy to any desk or playroom. With a few clever folds, you can create a jumping frog that moves with ease and delight.

  • Paper Crane: This elegant and graceful bird shape symbolizes peace, longevity, and good fortune. Its intricate folds and sweeping wings make it a popular choice for origami enthusiasts of all skill levels.

  • Origami Dragon: A more advanced origami design, this mythical creature combines elements of bird and serpent. Its serpentine body and majestic wings create a captivating model that showcases the art of origami.

Reading an Origami Diagram:

Understanding origami diagrams is crucial for successful folding. Here are the key elements to pay attention to:

  • Colored Side: Diagrams usually indicate which side of the paper should face up. This is typically done by shading or coloring one side of the diagram.

  • Fold Type: Diagrams use different line types to indicate different fold types. Dotted lines represent mountain folds, where the paper is folded upwards, and dashed lines represent valley folds, where the paper is folded downwards.

  • Folding Direction: Arrows guide the direction in which the paper should be folded. Single arrows indicate a single fold, while double arrows indicate a double fold or tuck.

  • Rotation/Flip: Arrows with loops or circles indicate that the paper should be rotated or flipped. This is done to change the orientation of the folds.

  • Repetitions: Lines with perpendicular lines indicate how many times a fold should be repeated. This is typically used for repeating creases or tucking edges.
