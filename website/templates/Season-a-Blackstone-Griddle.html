Discover the Complete Step-by-Step Guide to Seasoning Your Blackstone Griddle for Enhanced Performance and Delicious Cooking


Prepare to elevate your grilling game with this comprehensive guide to cleaning, seasoning, and maintaining your griddle! Whether you're prepping a brand-new griddle or bringing a seasoned workhorse back to life, we'll walk you through every step. Get ready to unlock the secrets of a nonstick, flavor-enhancing surface that will revolutionize your outdoor cooking!


Method 1: Cleaning and Seasoning Your Griddle

Step 1: Cleaning the Griddle

  • New Griddle: For a brand-new griddle, use hot, soapy water to remove any factory oils or contaminants. Rinse the griddle thoroughly and dry it completely with a lint-free cloth to prevent rust.

  • Old Griddle: Skip this step if your griddle has been seasoned previously.

Step 2: Choosing an Oil

  • Select an oil that is high in fatty acids. These oils will polymerize and create a durable, protective coating on the griddle.
  • Good options include: vegetable shortening, olive oil, coconut oil, grapeseed oil, or avocado oil.

Step 3: Heating the Griddle

  • Turn the burners on to their maximum heat setting.
  • Allow the griddle to heat for 10-15 minutes, or until it is smoking. This will help to remove any moisture and prepare the surface for seasoning.

Step 4: Spreading Oil on the Griddle

  • Apply 2-3 tablespoons of your chosen oil to the center of the griddle.
  • Using a spatula or tongs, carefully spread the oil evenly over the entire surface, including the sides and corners.

Step 5: Wiping the Sides

  • Pay special attention to the edges and corners of the griddle, as these areas tend to accumulate food and grease.
  • Use a paper towel or clean cloth to wipe away any excess oil from the edges.

Step 6: Heating the Oil

  • Allow the oil to heat on the griddle for 15-30 minutes, or until it turns slightly darker and starts to smoke.
  • This process will help to polymerize the oil, creating a protective layer on the surface.

Step 7: Cooling the Griddle

  • After the oil has heated, turn off the burners and allow the griddle to cool for 10 minutes.
  • This will allow the oil to penetrate the pores of the griddle and fully polymerize.

Step 8: Repeating Seasoning

  • Repeat steps 3-7 1-4 times, or as many times as needed to build up a sufficient layer of seasoning.
  • The griddle should gradually turn a dark brown color as the seasoning layers accumulate.

Step 9: Wiping with Oil

  • Once the seasoning process is complete, wipe down the griddle with a thin layer of cooking oil using a paper towel or clean cloth.
  • This will help to prevent rust and keep the seasoning intact.

Method 2: Storing and Maintaining Your Seasoned Griddle

Step 1: Storing the Griddle

  • Store the griddle in a dry, cool place with a cover or lid to protect it from dust and moisture.

Step 2: Cleaning After Each Use

  • After each use, clean the griddle with a paper towel and hot water.
  • Avoid using soap, as it can remove the seasoning.
  • If there is any stubborn food residue, gently scrape it off with a wooden or plastic spatula.

Step 3: Removing Rust

  • If rust develops on the griddle, remove it by scrubbing the affected area with steel wool or fine-grit sandpaper.
  • Once the rust is removed, re-season the griddle as described in Method 1.

Step 4: Maintaining Seasoning

  • To maintain the seasoning on your griddle, apply a thin layer of cooking oil after each cleaning.
  • This will help to keep the surface protected and prevent rust from forming.

Conclusion

Seasoning your griddle is a crucial step that enhances its performance and longevity. By following the detailed steps in this guide, you can effectively clean, season, store, and maintain your griddle. Remember to use high-quality oil, heat the griddle thoroughly, and apply multiple layers of seasoning. With proper care, your seasoned griddle will become a valuable addition to your kitchen, providing you with delicious and evenly cooked meals for years to come.
