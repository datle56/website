Selecting and Caring for a Homing Pigeon

Determining Your Budget:

Establish a realistic budget that considers the cost of acquiring high-quality pigeons, constructing or modifying a loft, and ongoing care expenses. Reputable pigeon breeders often charge a premium for birds with desirable traits and proven lineage.

Choosing High-Quality Pigeons:

  • Consider specific breeds known for their homing abilities, such as the Racing Homer, Trentonian, or Tippler.
  • Look for healthy pigeons with strong physiques, alert eyes, and glossy feathers.
  • Ensure the birds have been vaccinated and dewormed according to veterinary recommendations.

Building a Loft:

  • Provide ample space per pigeon (approximately 2 cubic feet).
  • Construct the loft with adequate ventilation, protection from the elements, and natural light.
  • Include perches, nest boxes, and a trapdoor for training.
  • Consider installing a loft management system for automated feeding and cleaning.

Acquiring Pigeons:

  • Find reputable breeders or sellers through online forums, pigeon clubs, or local shows.
  • Verify that the pigeons are banded for identification and have been properly cared for.
  • Arrange for the safe transportation of the pigeons to your loft.

Introducing New Pigeons:

  • Quarantine new pigeons for a period to prevent the spread of diseases.
  • Gradually introduce them to the loft by placing them in a separate enclosure initially.
  • Monitor the pigeons for signs of aggression or sickness.

Providing Regular Care:

  • Ensure constant access to fresh water and a nutritious diet of pigeon feed supplemented with grains and vegetables.
  • Provide grit for digestion and mineral supplementation.
  • Clean the loft regularly to maintain hygiene and prevent disease.
  • Inspect the pigeons routinely for any injuries or signs of illness.

Training a Homing Pigeon

Initiating Training:

  • Begin training at approximately 6 weeks of age.
  • Teach the pigeon to use the trapdoor by placing it in the opening and rewarding the bird when it enters.

Gradual Distance Training:

  • Gradually increase the release distance in increments of 5 miles per week.
  • Provide the pigeons with incentives to return home, such as food, treats, or a familiar environment.

Bird Loss and Adjustment:

  • Accept that bird loss is inevitable during training.
  • Adjust the training distance and intensity based on the performance of the pigeons and weather conditions.

Two-Location Training:

  • Train pigeons to fly between two locations for long-distance flights.
  • Establish a base loft and a release point at increasing distances.
  • Use a beacon system to attract the pigeons to the destination.

Racing a Homing Pigeon

Joining a Pigeon Racing Club:

  • Join a local pigeon racing club to connect with experienced racers and participate in events.
  • Attend races to observe the sport and learn from others.

Seeking Mentorship:

  • Reach out to experienced racers for advice and guidance.
  • Consider participating in mentorship programs to accelerate your learning process.

Entering Pigeons in Races:

  • Enter your pigeons in races once they are well-trained and have exhibited a strong homing instinct.
  • Ensure that your pigeons are properly conditioned, vaccinated, and acclimated to the race distance.

Factors for Race Success:

  • The distance and speed of the pigeon play a crucial role in its success in races.
  • Consider the weather conditions, wind direction, and topography of the race course when preparing your pigeons.
