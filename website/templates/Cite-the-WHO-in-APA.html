Step-by-Step Guide to Properly Citing the World Health Organization Using APA Format


Unlocking the Wealth of Knowledge: A Guide to Citing the World Health Organization

As a hub of global health expertise, the World Health Organization (WHO) provides an invaluable source of information for professionals and enthusiasts alike. However, navigating the intricate world of citing this authoritative organization can be a daunting task. Fear not, for this comprehensive guide will empower you with the knowledge and confidence to accurately and effectively reference WHO publications.

Whether you're working on academic papers, research proposals, or simply seeking to disseminate credible health information, this guide has you covered. Step by step, we'll unravel the mysteries of WHO citations, ensuring that you can confidently credit this esteemed organization for its invaluable contributions to the world of health and well-being.


Citing the World Health Organization (WHO)

For Reference Page (APA Style):

  1. WHO. (2023). World Health Statistics 2023. Retrieved from https://www.who.int/data/sets/world-health-statistics
  2. WHO. (n.d.). Global Health Observatory (GHO) data repository. Retrieved from https://www.who.int/data/gho

For In-Text Citations (APA Style):

  1. First mention: (World Health Organization [WHO], 2023, p. 10)
  2. Subsequent mentions: (WHO, 2023)

Additional Details:

Formatting:

  • The title of the report is italicized in the reference page.
  • The publication date is always enclosed in parentheses, even if it is "n.d." (no date).
  • The website address or publication location should be the last element of the reference.

Quotations:

  • If quoting directly from a WHO report, include the page or paragraph number after the publication date in the in-text citation. For example: (WHO, 2023, p. 15)

Paraphrasing:

  • When paraphrasing information from a WHO report, include the publication date in the in-text citation. For example: (WHO, 2023)

Other Resources:

  • WHO Citation Guide: https://www.who.int/about/ethics/citation-guide

Conclusion

In conclusion, citing the World Health Organization (WHO) is crucial for supporting the credibility and accuracy of your research and writing. By adhering to the established guidelines for proper citation, you not only demonstrate scholarly rigor but also ensure that readers have access to reputable sources for further exploration. Remember, referencing the WHO's authoritative publications strengthens your arguments, enhances the validity of your claims, and facilitates further knowledge dissemination.
