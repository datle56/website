Myths and Old Wives' Tales about Baby Gender

For centuries, people have been attempting to predict the sex of unborn babies through a variety of methods, ranging from observing the mother's physical appearance to consulting ancient calendars. However, many of these so-called "myths" and "old wives' tales" have no scientific basis and should be taken with a grain of salt.

Skull Shape

One common myth is that the shape of the baby's skull can indicate the gender. It is believed that male babies have a more pronounced ridge at the top of the skull while female babies have a more rounded shape. However, scientific studies have shown no evidence to support this claim.

Baby Heart Rate

Another persistent belief is that the baby's heart rate can indicate the sex. Specifically, it is said that boys' heart rates are lower than girls' heart rates in the womb. While it is true that boys' heart rates tend to be slightly lower than girls' heart rates after birth, this difference is not significant enough to be a reliable predictor of gender during pregnancy.

Baby Bump Shape

The size and shape of the pregnant belly is often used to predict the baby's gender. Some believe that a high, pointed belly indicates a boy while a low, round belly indicates a girl. However, there is no scientific evidence to support this theory. The shape of the belly is determined by a variety of factors, including the mother's weight, posture, muscle tone, and the position of the baby.

Linea Nigra Position

The linea nigra, a dark line that often appears on the abdomen during pregnancy, is also said to indicate the baby's gender. Some believe that a straight linea nigra indicates a boy while a zigzag linea nigra indicates a girl. However, there is no scientific evidence to support this claim. The length and direction of the linea nigra is determined by the mother's hormones and the stretching of the skin on her abdomen.

Nipple Color

Another myth is that the color of the mother's nipples can indicate the baby's gender. It is believed that if the nipples darken during pregnancy, it indicates a girl. However, this is simply a result of hormonal changes during pregnancy and has nothing to do with the baby's sex.

Breast Size

Another long-standing belief is that the mother's breast size can indicate the baby's gender. Some believe that a larger left breast indicates a girl while a larger right breast indicates a boy. However, there is no scientific evidence to support this claim. Breast size is determined by a variety of factors, including the mother's age, weight, and hormones.

Complexion Changes

Some people also believe that changes in the mother's complexion during pregnancy can indicate the baby's gender. It is said that a clear complexion indicates a boy while a breakout of acne indicates a girl. However, these changes in complexion are simply a result of hormonal fluctuations during pregnancy and have nothing to do with the baby's sex.

Dry Hands or Food Cravings

Other common myths include the belief that dry hands indicate a boy while soft, moisturized hands indicate a girl. Similarly, some believe that craving salty foods indicates a boy while craving sweet foods indicates a girl. However, there is no scientific evidence to support any of these claims.

Moodiness

Another myth is that the mother's moodiness during pregnancy can indicate the baby's gender. It is believed that a more emotional and moody mother is carrying a girl. However, mood swings are a normal part of pregnancy and have nothing to do with the baby's sex.

Ancient Calendars or Baking Soda Test

Some people also believe that ancient calendars or the baking soda test can predict the baby's gender. The baking soda test involves mixing a sample of the mother's urine with baking soda and observing whether it fizzes. A fizz is said to indicate a boy while no fizz is said to indicate a girl. However, there is no scientific evidence to support any of these methods for predicting the baby's sex.

  • Fetal DNA Test: Another method for determining the baby's gender is through a fetal DNA test. This test involves a blood draw from the mother and can detect the presence of a Y-chromosome, which is only present in male babies. This test can be performed as early as 10 weeks of pregnancy.

  • Nub Theory: The nub theory is a method that can be used to estimate the baby's gender based on the angle of the developing genitalia. This method is not as accurate as the ultrasound scan or fetal DNA test, but it can be performed as early as 12 weeks of pregnancy.
