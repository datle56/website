Expanded Guide to Essential Italian Greetings

Daytime Greetings:

  • Buongiorno (boh-n-jor-noh): A formal and polite greeting used during the day, typically from sunrise to sunset. It translates to "Good morning" or "Good day."

Evening Greetings:

  • Buona sera (boh-na seh-rah): A formal and respectful greeting used in the evening, typically from sunset onwards. It translates to "Good evening."

Asking About Well-being:

  • Come sta? (koh-meh stah): A formal way to ask someone, particularly strangers or older individuals, "How are you?"
  • Come stai? (koh-meh stah-yee): A more informal and friendly way to ask someone your age or younger, or a friend, "How are you?"

Introducing Yourself:

  • Mi chiamo (mee-kee-ah-moh): Begin by indicating your name. For example, "Mi chiamo Maria."
  • Piacere (pee-ah-shehr-reh): Use this phrase to express pleasure in meeting someone. It translates to "Nice to meet you."
  • Vengo da (veen-goh dah) or Sono di (soh-noh dee): Provide your origin by stating where you are from. For instance, "Vengo da Torino" or "Sono di Napoli."

Slang and Casual Greetings:

  • Ciao (chow): A casual and versatile greeting used among friends, family, and acquaintances. It can mean both "Hello" and "Goodbye."
  • Salve (sahl-veh): A slightly more formal but still casual greeting suitable for most situations. It is a traditional greeting that means "Hello" or "Greetings."
  • Bella (beh-lah): This informal and youthful greeting, meaning "beautiful" or "nice," is commonly used among young people as a casual way to say hello.
  • Come butta? (koh-meh boot-tah): A laid-back and informal way to ask "What's up?" It is less formal than "Come sta?" and is often used in casual conversations.
