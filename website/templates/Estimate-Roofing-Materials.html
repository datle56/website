DIY Roofing: A Step-by-Step Guide for the Handy Homeowner

Attention all house enthusiasts and aspiring roofers! Are you ready to embark on the rewarding journey of roofing your abode? Look no further, for we've meticulously crafted a comprehensive guide that will empower you to tackle this project with confidence.

In this three-part series, we'll walk you through the intricacies of measuring your roof, selecting the right materials, and installing them with precision. Whether you're a seasoned DIYer or a novice eager to save some hard-earned cash, this guide will guide you every step of the way.

So, grab your tools, strap on your safety gear, and let's dive into Part 1: Shingles!


Part 1: Shingles

Step 1: Measure Roof Dimensions

  • Using a measuring tape, determine the length and width of each distinct roof section.
  • Record these measurements in feet or inches.

Step 2: Calculate Square Footage

  • Convert the length and width measurements to feet if they are not already in that unit.
  • Multiply the length by the width of each roof section to obtain its square footage.
  • Total the square footage of all roof sections to arrive at the total square footage of the roof.

Step 3: Determine Number of Squares

  • Divide the total square footage by 100 to determine the number of "squares" of roof.
  • A "square" is 100 square feet of roofing area, the industry standard unit for measuring roofing materials.

Step 4: Calculate Number of Shingle Bundles

  • For 3-tab shingles, multiply the number of squares by 3 to estimate the number of bundles required.
  • This assumes an average coverage of 33.3 square feet per bundle of 3-tab shingles.

Step 5: Adjust for Waste

  • Add 10% to the number of bundles calculated in Step 4 for waste.
  • If the roof has hips and valleys, increase the waste factor to 15% to account for additional cutting and fitting.

Part 2: Felt Underlayment

Step 1: Measure Roof Dimensions and Calculate Square Footage

  • Follow the same steps as outlined in Part 1 for shingles to determine the total square footage of the roof.

Step 2: Determine Number of Squares

  • Divide the total square footage by 100 to get the number of squares.

Step 3: Calculate Number of Underlayment Rolls for #15 Felt

  • For slopes greater than 4:12, use 1 roll of #15 felt underlayment for every 4 squares of roof.
  • For slopes between 3:12 and 4:12, use 2 rolls of #15 felt underlayment for every 4 squares of roof.

Part 3: Other Materials

Step 1: Measure Lengths for Drip Edge

  • Measure the total length of the roof's rakes (sides) and eaves (bottom edge).
  • Convert the measurements to feet if they are not already in that unit.

Step 2: Estimate Number of Roofing Nails

  • For field shingles, estimate around 4 nails per shingle.
  • For starter shingles, estimate 5 nails per shingle.
  • For areas with high winds, consider using 6 nails per shingle for both field and starter shingles.

Step 3: Calculate Hip and Ridge Cap Shingles

  • Determine the coverage of the hip and ridge cap shingles (typically in feet per bundle).
  • Measure the length of all hips and ridges on the roof in feet.
  • Divide the bundle coverage by the total length of hips and ridges to estimate the number of bundles needed.

Step 4: Consider Additional Costs and Materials

  • Venting: Estimate the number of roof vents required based on the square footage of the roof.
  • Flashing: Determine the approximate amount of metal flashing needed for chimneys, valleys, and other roof penetrations.
  • Ice/Water Shield: Consider using ice/water shield in areas prone to ice dams or cold weather.
  • Boots: Calculate the number of boots required for plumbing vent pipes or other roof protrusions.

Conclusion

Once you have estimated the quantities of roofing materials needed, it's crucial to consider other factors that may influence your choice and expenses. Factors such as the complexity of the roof design, the pitch, local building codes, and the availability and cost of materials in your area can impact the overall cost.

Remember, these calculations are estimates, and the actual quantities may vary depending on the specifics of your project. Consulting with a licensed roofing contractor is highly recommended to ensure accurate measurements, proper installation, and adherence to safety regulations. By following these steps and considering the additional factors, you'll have a better understanding of the materials and quantities required for your roofing project. This will empower you to make informed decisions and prepare a realistic budget for your home improvement undertaking.
