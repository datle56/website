Finding Fenton Glass Markings

Fenton Art Glass, a renowned American glass manufacturer, has been producing stunning and collectible glassware for over a century. Identifying Fenton glass is crucial for collectors, antique enthusiasts, and those seeking to preserve its legacy. Here's a comprehensive guide to finding Fenton glass markings:

Oval Stickers

Prior to 1970, Fenton glass often featured oval foil stickers affixed to the bottom of the piece. These stickers varied in color and design, but typically displayed the Fenton logo or the words "Fenton Art Glass Co." in a distinctive font.

Oval Logo

From 1970 onwards, Fenton carnival glass often bore an oval logo embossed on the bottom. This oval logo featured the word "Fenton" inscribed within a leaf-like design. The presence of this logo indicates that the piece was manufactured during or after 1970.

Number in the Logo

In the 1980s, Fenton introduced an additional element to its oval logo: a number. This number represented the decade in which the piece was produced. For instance, a piece with the number "8" in the logo was made in the 1980s, a piece with the number "9" in the logo was made in the 1990s, and so on.

Cursive "F" in an Oval

Some Fenton glass pieces feature a cursive "F" inside an oval on the bottom. This marking indicates that the glass mold used to create the piece was originally owned by another company. Fenton often acquired molds from other glassmakers, which resulted in the presence of this distinctive marking.

Flame or Star

Occasionally, Fenton glass may have a small flame or star symbol etched or embossed on the bottom. This symbol indicates that the piece is considered a "second" or imperfect piece. Fenton glass is renowned for its high quality, and pieces that do not meet the company's exacting standards are often marked with a flame or star to denote their imperfections.

Identifying Unmarked Fenton Glass

Not all Fenton glass pieces bear visible markings. However, there are several telltale characteristics that can help you identify unmarked Fenton glass:

No Pontil Mark

Unlike many other types of glass, Fenton glass typically does not have a pontil mark. This is a small, raised mark on the bottom of the glass that is left by the metal rod used during the manufacturing process. Fenton glass is often made using a press mold, which eliminates the need for a pontil rod.

Study Fenton Catalogs or Books

Familiarizing yourself with Fenton's extensive catalog of patterns, styles, and colors is invaluable for identifying unmarked pieces. Study Fenton catalogs, books, and online resources to gain a comprehensive understanding of their design aesthetic.

Examine the Base and Edges

Fenton glass often features a flat, collared base or ball feet. The edges of Fenton glass pieces are typically smooth, bracketed, crimped, or ruffled. These distinctive features can help you differentiate Fenton glass from other types of glassware.

Check for Bubbles or Flaws

Fenton glass is known for its high quality and meticulous craftsmanship. Authentic Fenton glass should be free of air bubbles or imperfections. However, some unmarked pieces may have slight irregularities that are not visible to the naked eye.

Consult a Fenton Dealer or Antique Expert

If you're unsure about the authenticity of an unmarked Fenton glass piece, it's advisable to consult a knowledgeable Fenton dealer or antique expert. They can provide expert guidance and help you determine the origin and value of your piece.
