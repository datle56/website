Easy Guide to Cooking Meat Mince

Stovetop Method

  1. Prepare the skillet and heat: Heat a large skillet or frying pan over medium heat. Add 2 tablespoons of vegetable oil and let it shimmer.

  2. Add the mince: Gently add the ground meat mince to the skillet. Use a wooden spoon or spatula to break up the mince into large chunks.

  3. Brown the mince: Gradually stir the mince, breaking it into smaller crumbles as it browns. Browning the mince imparts flavor and develops a crispy exterior.

  4. Season to taste: Once the mince has browned, season it with salt to taste. You can also add other herbs and spices, such as black pepper, garlic powder, or ground cumin.

  5. Ensure even cooking: Continue stirring the mince frequently to prevent scorching and ensure that it cooks evenly throughout.

  6. Check for doneness: To check for doneness, cut into a large crumble of mince. There should be no pink or red areas visible, indicating that the meat is cooked through.

Microwave Method

  1. Prepare the microwave-safe container: Place the ground meat mince in a microwave-safe container. Add 2-3 tablespoons of water and 1 tablespoon of Worcestershire sauce (optional).

  2. Cover and microwave: Cover the container loosely with a lid or plastic wrap and microwave on high for 2 minutes.

  3. Break up the mince: Remove the container from the microwave and use a fork to break up the mince.

  4. Microwave in intervals: Continue to microwave the mince in 30-second intervals, stirring in between, until it is thoroughly browned and no longer pink.

Seasoned Mince

  1. Sauté aromatics: Heat 1 tablespoon of oil in a skillet. Add chopped onion and garlic and sauté until softened.

  2. Brown the mince: Add the ground meat mince to the skillet and cook until browned.

  3. Add canned tomatoes: Pour in 1 undrained 14.5-ounce can of diced tomatoes.

  4. Season and simmer: Sprinkle oregano, black pepper, and 1 teaspoon of beef granules dissolved in 1/4 cup of hot water. Bring to a simmer and cook for 20 minutes, or until the sauce has thickened and the flavors have blended.
