Calculating the Distance Between Two Points on a Line

Step 1: Note Down the Coordinates

The first step in calculating the distance between two points on a line is to locate their coordinates. A coordinate system, typically a Cartesian coordinate system, provides a grid to plot points in a two-dimensional plane. Coordinates are usually represented as (x, y) pairs, where the x-coordinate represents the point's horizontal position, and the y-coordinate represents its vertical position.

To calculate the distance between two points, you need to determine the coordinates of both the starting and ending points of the line segment. These coordinates will be denoted as (x1, y1) for the starting point and (x2, y2) for the ending point.

Step 2: Plug the Coordinates into the Formula

Once you have identified the coordinates of the two points, you can use the Euclidean distance formula to calculate the distance between them. The Euclidean distance formula, which is attributed to the ancient Greek mathematician Euclid, is expressed as:

d = √((x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)²)

  • d: represents the distance between the two points.
  • (x2 - x1): represents the difference between the x-coordinates of the two points.
  • (y2 - y1): represents the difference between the y-coordinates of the two points.

Step 3: Calculate the Distance

To calculate the distance (d), you need to perform a series of mathematical operations:

  • Subtract the Coordinates: Subtract the x-coordinates and the y-coordinates of the two points to find the differences, (x2 - x1) and (y2 - y1), respectively.
  • Square the Results: Square each of the differences, yielding (x2 - x1)² and (y2 - y1)².
  • Add the Squares: Add these squared differences together to get the expression: (x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)².
  • Find the Square Root: Take the square root of the sum from the previous step to obtain the distance (d) between the two points. In the final step, the square root represents the straight-line distance along the hypothetical line connecting the two points. The distance is expressed in the same units as the coordinates.

By following these steps, you can accurately calculate the distance between two points on a line, whether it's a horizontal line, a vertical line, or a line at any other angle. This procedure is fundamental in various applications, including geometry, surveying, navigation, and engineering.
