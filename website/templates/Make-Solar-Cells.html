Unleash the Power of Nature: A Step-by-Step Guide to Creating Your Own Solar Cell

Part 1: Creating a Titan Electrode

  1. Turn berries into a paste:

    • Select fresh, ripe berries of your choice (such as blueberries, blackberries, or raspberries).
    • Mash the berries thoroughly using a mortar and pestle or a spoon until a smooth paste forms. Ensure there are no large chunks or skins remaining.

  2. Mix the paste with water:

    • Transfer the berry paste to a shallow dish.
    • Gradually add distilled or deionized water to the paste while stirring constantly. The ratio of water to paste should be approximately 1:1.
    • Continue stirring until a homogeneous, liquid mixture is obtained.

  3. Place a glass slide coated with titanium oxide in the dye:

    • Obtain a glass microscope slide and clean it thoroughly with soap and water. Dry the slide using a lint-free cloth.
    • Purchase a bottle of titanium oxide powder and prepare a suspension by mixing it with water.
    • Dip one side of the clean glass slide into the titanium oxide suspension. Ensure that the slide is fully coated with a thin layer of titanium oxide.
    • Allow the slide to dry completely.

  4. Rinse the slide with water and alcohol:

    • After the titanium oxide has dried, gently rinse the slide with distilled water to remove any remaining powder.
    • Rinse the slide further with isopropyl alcohol to ensure that the surface is completely clean.

  5. Dry the slide with a tissue:

    • Use a clean, lint-free tissue to carefully dry the glass slide. Avoid touching the coated surface with your fingers.

Part 2: Making a Counter Electrode

  1. Find the conductive side of a glass slide:

    • Hold a glass microscope slide by the corners and gently scrape one side of it with your fingernail.
    • Observe the surface closely. The side that produces a fine, metallic dust is the conductive side.

  2. Cover half of the conductive side with a strip of tape:

    • Cut a strip of masking tape to a length that is slightly shorter than the conductive side of the glass slide.
    • Align the tape against the edge of the conductive side and press it down firmly.
    • Ensure that approximately half of the conductive surface is covered by the tape.

  3. Rub a graphite pencil over the exposed conductive surface to coat it with carbon:

    • Hold a graphite pencil at a 45-degree angle to the exposed conductive surface.
    • Apply firm pressure and rub the pencil back and forth along the length of the exposed area.
    • Continue rubbing until a thin, uniform layer of graphite completely covers the surface.

  4. Remove the tape without touching the carbon coating:

    • Grip both ends of the masking tape and carefully peel it away from the glass slide.
    • Avoid touching the carbon coating with your fingers or any other objects.

Part 3: Assembling the Solar Cell

  1. Drop some electrolyte solution onto the dyed slide:

    • Prepare an electrolyte solution by dissolving a suitable compound (such as iodine or methylene blue) in a solvent (such as water or ethanol).
    • Use a dropper to carefully place a few drops of the electrolyte solution onto the surface of the dyed glass slide.
    • The electrolyte solution should spread evenly across the surface, creating a thin layer.

  2. Place the carbon-coated slide on top, offsetting it slightly:

    • Align the carbon-coated glass slide with the dyed slide.
    • Slide the carbon-coated slide onto the dyed slide, starting from one edge.
    • Offset the slides slightly by about 1-2 centimeters, leaving a small gap on one side.

  3. Clip the slides together at the non-exposed edges:

    • Use binder clips or clothespins to securely hold the slides together at the edges that are not exposed to the electrolyte solution.
    • Ensure that the clips do not touch the exposed surfaces of the slides.

Part 4: Testing the Solar Cell

  1. Connect a multimeter to the exposed edges of the slides:

    • Set a multimeter to the voltage measurement setting (usually denoted by V or mV).
    • Connect one lead of the multimeter to the exposed titanium oxide edge of the dyed slide and the other lead to the exposed carbon coating of the counter electrode.
    • Ensure that the leads make good contact with the surfaces.

  2. Place the cell in sunlight and read the voltage on the multimeter:

    • Take the assembled solar cell outdoors and position it in direct sunlight.
    • Observe the voltage reading on the multimeter. If the cell is functioning correctly, you should see a positive voltage reading (typically in the range of 0.1-0.5 volts).
