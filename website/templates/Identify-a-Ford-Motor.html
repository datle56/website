Identifying Your Ford Engine

Accurate identification of your Ford engine is crucial for proper maintenance, repairs, and performance enhancements. Two primary methods can help you decipher the engine code: the identification tag and the casting tag.

Using the Identification Tag

  1. Locate the Identification Tag: Find the metal tag with various numbers and letters bolted to the engine. It's usually located on the front or side of the engine block.
  2. Decode the Information: Read the information from left to right:
    • Cubic Inch Displacement (CID): The first three numbers represent the engine's displacement in cubic inches. For example, "351" indicates a 351 cubic inch engine.
    • Manufacturing Plant: The following letter indicates the manufacturing plant where the engine was assembled. For example, "C" stands for the Cleveland plant.
    • Year: The next two numbers represent the year of manufacture. For instance, "70" signifies the engine was produced in 1970.
    • Month Built: The hyphenated number and letter denote the month and year of production. For example, "5-C" indicates the engine was assembled in March 1975.
    • Engine Code Number: The last three digits form the engine code number, which provides additional information about the specific engine configuration and features. For example, "123" could represent a specific camshaft profile or intake manifold design.

Using the Casting Tag

  1. Locate the Casting Tag: Find the 9-digit code etched into the engine block, typically on the intake manifold surface.
  2. Decipher the Casting Tag: The 9-digit code contains the following information:
    • Year: The first two digits are a letter that corresponds to the year of production. For example, "C" represents 1969.
    • Engine Design: The third digit is a letter that indicates the engine type. For example, "A" signifies a generic engine design.
    • Part Type: The fourth digit is always "E" for engines.
    • Engine Assembly: The next four digits represent the engine assembly number. For example, "6500" indicates the 6500th engine assembled in that configuration.
    • Part Version: The last digit is a letter that denotes the part version. For example, "A" indicates the original design version, while a subsequent letter (e.g., "B") indicates a revised version.

By carefully following these steps and referencing the provided examples, you can accurately identify your Ford engine and access the correct information for maintenance, repairs, and performance upgrades.


Conclusion:

Identifying your Ford engine is crucial for proper maintenance, repairs, and modifications. By following these steps, you can accurately determine the displacement, plant of origin, manufacturing date, and engine code. With this knowledge, you can ensure that your Ford runs smoothly and effectively for years to come.
