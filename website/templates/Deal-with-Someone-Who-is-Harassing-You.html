Confronting Harassment: A Guide to Standing Up and Taking Back Control

Harassment is a pervasive issue that can have a profound impact on our lives. Whether it occurs in the workplace, at school, or online, it's essential to know how to respond effectively. This comprehensive guide will empower you to stand up for yourself, access help when needed, and protect your safety.

In Part 1, we'll delve into the steps you can take to confront harassment head-on. We'll explore how to clearly communicate your boundaries, assert your discomfort, and firmly demand that the harassment stop. By understanding these techniques, you can effectively shut down unwelcome advances and make it clear to the offender that their behavior is unacceptable.


Part 1: Standing Up for Yourself

Clearly Tell the Harasser What They're Doing Wrong:

  • Avoid using vague language or beating around the bush. Directly state that their behavior is unwelcome and unacceptable.
  • Use specific, non-accusatory language: "That comment about my appearance is considered sexual harassment."
  • Maintain a calm and assertive tone, even if the harasser becomes defensive.

Use "I" Statements to Focus on Their Actions:

  • Express your emotions and discomfort using "I" statements. This shifts the focus from blaming the person to their actions.
  • Examples: "I'm uncomfortable when you touch me without my permission" or "I feel harassed when you continuously ask me out despite my repeated refusals."

Avoid Blaming the Person or Making Excuses:

  • Blaming or accusing the harasser can escalate the situation and make it more difficult to resolve.
  • Instead, focus on the specific behaviors that are causing harm.
  • Avoid making excuses or downplaying the situation, as this may diminish the seriousness of the harassment.

If They Continue, Tell Them to Stop All Contact and That You Won't Respond:

  • If the harasser persists despite your clear communication, firmly tell them to stop all contact with you.
  • State that you will not respond to their attempts to communicate, whether it be by phone, email, texting, or in person.
  • Follow through with your boundary and do not engage with the harasser unless absolutely necessary.

Part 2: Help and Resources

Keep a Record of Every Incident of Harassment:

  • Document every incident in as much detail as possible, including the date, time, location, specific actions, and any witnesses present.
  • Keep a journal or log to record these incidents and maintain evidence for future reference.

Involve Your School, Workplace, or Authorities if Needed:

  • If the harassment occurs in a school or workplace setting, report it to the appropriate authorities.
  • They can investigate the situation and take disciplinary action against the harasser.
  • If the harassment poses an immediate threat to your safety, call 911 or the local police.

Consider Getting a Restraining Order to Protect Yourself:

  • A restraining order is a legal document that prohibits a person from contacting or approaching you.
  • If you feel threatened or unsafe, consider obtaining a restraining order to protect yourself from further harassment.

Report Violations of the Restraining Order Immediately:

  • If the harasser violates the restraining order, report it to the authorities immediately.
  • Violations of restraining orders are serious offenses and may result in arrest and prosecution.

Part 3: Safety Tips

Confide in Friends and Family and Keep Them Updated on Your Whereabouts:

  • Inform trusted individuals about the harassment and ask them to support you.
  • Keep them updated on your activities and whereabouts, especially if you feel unsafe.

Ask Someone to Stay with You if You Feel Unsafe:

  • If you are feeling threatened or uncomfortable, do not hesitate to ask someone to stay with you.
  • Their presence can provide a sense of security and deter the harasser from approaching.

Don't Share Your Location or Daily Habits on Social Media:

  • Be mindful of what personal information you share on social media platforms.
  • Avoid revealing your location, daily routine, or other details that could make it easy for the harasser to track you.

Increase the Security of Your Home with Locks and Motion Detectors:

  • Secure your home with sturdy locks on all doors and windows.
  • Install motion detectors outside your home to alert you to any suspicious activity.

Learn Self-Defense and Consider Carrying a Security Device:

  • Enrolling in self-defense classes can provide you with the skills and confidence to protect yourself in the event of an attack.
  • Consider carrying a personal security device, such as pepper spray or a whistle, to deter potential aggressors.

Protect Your Personal Information Online by Removing It from Websites and Monitoring Your Credit Report:

  • Remove any personal information from websites or social media platforms that could be used to identify or locate you.
  • Monitor your credit report regularly to ensure that no fraudulent activity is taking place.

Conclusion:

Standing up for yourself against harassment can be a daunting task, but it is crucial for your well-being and safety. By assertively confronting harassment, seeking assistance if necessary, and implementing safety measures, you can protect yourself from further harm and empower yourself to live a life free from fear and intimidation. Remember, you have the right to respect and autonomy, and you are not alone in this fight. By speaking up and taking action, you not only protect yourself but also send a message that harassment will not be tolerated.
