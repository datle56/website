Easy-to-Understand Guide to Long Division of Decimals

Step 1: Preparing the Division Problem

  1. Convert the whole number to a decimal: If the whole number has no decimal point, add a decimal point and as many zeros as the decimal part of the other number. For example, 5 becomes 5.00.
  2. Move the decimal points: Move the decimal points of both numbers to the right by the same number of places as the decimal part of the divisor. This effectively converts the division problem into one involving whole numbers.

Example:

``` 5.5 ÷ 0.25

Convert 5 to 5.00 and move the decimal points two places to the right:

5.00 ÷ 0.2500 ```

Step 2: Solve the Division Problem

  1. Divide: Using the long division algorithm, divide the dividend (larger number) by the divisor (smaller number).
  2. Write the answer digit: Place the answer digit above the line, directly above the last digit of the dividend used in the division.
  3. Multiply and subtract: Multiply the answer digit by the divisor and write the product below the line. Subtract the product from the dividend.
  4. Bring down: Bring down the next digit of the dividend.
  5. Repeat: Repeat steps 1-4 until you reach the end of the dividend or until the remainder is zero.

Step 3: Handling Remainders

If there is a remainder after all digits of the dividend have been brought down, you can either:

  • Add a decimal point and zeros: Add a decimal point and bring down a zero to the dividend. Continue dividing until the remainder is zero or you have reached the desired level of accuracy.
  • Estimate the remainder: Estimate the value of the remainder by rounding it to the nearest thousandth, hundredth, or tenth.

Additional Tips:

  • Don't add zeros to the left of the decimal point: This will change the value of the number.
  • Treat a whole number dividend: If the dividend is a whole number and the divisor is a decimal, follow the same steps but don't add any zeros to the divisor. Convert the whole number dividend to a decimal with a decimal point and as many trailing zeros as the decimal places in the divisor.
