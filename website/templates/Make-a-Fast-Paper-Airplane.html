Part 1: Folding the Airplane

  1. Initial Fold: Take a flat piece of paper and fold it in half lengthwise, creating a vertical crease down the center. Unfold it to return to the flat position.

  2. Corner Triangles: Fold the top left and right corners down to meet the central crease you just created. These triangles will form the plane's wings.

  3. Top Triangle Fold: Bring the top edge of the paper down to meet the bottom edge of the created triangles, forming a smaller triangle at the top.

  4. Small Triangle Formation: Bring the top corners of the small triangle together, folding them towards each other to create an even smaller, but prominent triangle.

  5. Body Formation: Fold the bottom edge of the paper upwards to form the plane's body. Ensure that the fold is parallel to the top edge of the wings.

  6. Wings: Fold the paper outward from the center crease, creating the plane's wings. The crease from the initial fold will serve as the hinge for the wings.

  7. Final Wing Fold: Fold the wings upward at the ends to create a gentle angle, providing lift and stability during flight.

Part 2: Making Adjustments for Enhanced Flight

  1. Nose Bluntness: To increase the plane's flight distance, fold the nose of the plane inward to make it blunt. This shape reduces air resistance and allows the plane to glide further.

  2. Wing Symmetry: Ensure that the wings are even and aligned at the same height. Uneven wings can cause the plane to curve or spiral during flight.

  3. Wing Elevation: To prevent nosedives, adjust the back edges of the wings slightly upwards. This creates an angle that generates lift, preventing the nose from drooping.

  4. Wing Curvature: To maintain loft and prevent the wings from flapping excessively, bend the back of the wings slightly downwards. This curvature stabilizes the flight and keeps the wings from dragging against the air.

Part 3: Choosing the Ideal Paper for Airplane Folding

  1. Paper Weight: Select paper with a weight that is not too thin or too heavy. Standard letter paper or A4 paper provides an appropriate balance for easy folding and flight performance.

  2. Paper Size: Stick to standard paper sizes for ease of folding and consistency. Sizes such as 8.5" x 11" or A4 are commonly used and offer ample space for creating a stable aircraft.

  3. Surface Quality: Choose paper with a smooth surface that accepts folds well. Rough or textured paper can make it difficult to create precise folds, affecting the flight characteristics of the plane.
