Homemade Pesticides for Organic Pest Control

1. Vegetable Pesticide: A Potent Repellent

  • Ingredients:

    • 1/2 cup hot peppers (such as cayenne or jalapeños)
    • 2 cloves garlic
    • 1/2 onion
    • 1 gallon warm water

  • Instructions:

    1. In a blender, combine the hot peppers, garlic, and onion until they form a smooth paste.
    2. Transfer the paste to a large container and add 1 gallon of warm water.
    3. Stir thoroughly and let the mixture sit covered for 24 hours.
    4. Strain the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray directly on plants, focusing on areas where insects are visible or where you anticipate pest infestations.

2. Oil Pesticide: A Smothering Solution

  • Ingredients:

    • 1/4 cup mild dish soap
    • 1/2 cup cooking oil
    • 1 gallon water

  • Instructions:

    1. In a large bowl or bucket, combine the dish soap and cooking oil.
    2. Gradually whisk in 1 gallon of water until the mixture is well-combined and emulsified.
    3. Pour the solution into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray the oil mixture directly on insects or plants where pests are present. The mixture will smother and kill insects, and can also deter future infestations.

3. Soap Pesticide: A Simple and Effective Insecticide

  • Ingredients:

    • 1/4 cup mild dish soap
    • 1 gallon water

  • Instructions:

    1. In a large container or bucket, combine the dish soap and water.
    2. Stir until the soap is fully dissolved.
    3. Pour the solution into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray the soap mixture directly on plants, focusing on the undersides of leaves and areas where insects are likely to gather. The soap solution will kill insects by disrupting their outer membranes.

4. Tobacco Pesticide: A Natural Insecticide for Caterpillars and Aphids

  • Ingredients:

    • 1 cup loose tobacco
    • 1 gallon water
    • 1/4 cup mild dish soap (optional)

  • Instructions:

    1. In a large container or bucket, combine the tobacco and water.
    2. Allow the mixture to steep for 24 hours.
    3. Strain the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve and add the dish soap, if desired.
    4. Pour the solution into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray the tobacco solution directly on plants affected by caterpillars, aphids, or other pests. The nicotine in the tobacco will paralyze and kill the insects.

5. Orange Pesticide: A Natural Repellent for Slugs and Mealy Bugs

  • Ingredients:

    • 1 cup orange peels
    • 1 gallon water
    • 1/4 cup castile soap

  • Instructions:

    1. In a large pot, bring the orange peels and water to a boil.
    2. Reduce heat to low and simmer for 30 minutes.
    3. Strain the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve and allow it to cool.
    4. Add the castile soap and pour the solution into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray the orange solution directly on plants to repel soft-bodied pests such as slugs, mealy bugs, and scale. The citric acid and limonene in the orange peels will deter pests from feeding and laying eggs.

6. Chrysanthemum Pesticide: A Paralyzing Insect Killer

  • Ingredients:

    • 1/2 cup dried chrysanthemums
    • 1 gallon water

  • Instructions:

    1. In a large pot or bucket, combine the chrysanthemums and water.
    2. Bring the mixture to a boil, then reduce heat to low and simmer for 30 minutes.
    3. Strain the mixture through a fine-mesh sieve and pour it into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray the chrysanthemum solution directly on plants to paralyze insects. Pyrethrum, an insecticidal compound found in chrysanthemums, will kill insects on contact.

7. Neem Pesticide: A Broad-Spectrum Insect Repellent

  • Ingredients:

    • 1/2 cup neem oil
    • 1 gallon water
    • 1/4 cup mild soap

  • Instructions:

    1. In a large container or bucket, combine the neem oil, water, and soap.
    2. Stir until the mixture is well-combined.
    3. Pour the solution into a spray bottle.

  • Usage: Spray the neem solution directly on plants, focusing on areas where pests are present or where you anticipate infestations. Neem oil is effective against a wide range of pests, including aphids, mealy bugs, mites, and leafhoppers.
