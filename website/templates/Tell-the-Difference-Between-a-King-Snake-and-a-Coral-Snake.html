Identifying Coral Snakes: A Comprehensive Guide

Coral snakes are venomous reptiles that can be found in various regions of the world. While their bites can be dangerous, understanding their distinct characteristics can help you avoid potential encounters and stay safe.

Color Patterns

  • Coral Snakes: Coral snakes typically exhibit a distinctive pattern of bright red, yellow, and black rings. These rings are arranged in a specific sequence: red, yellow, black, yellow, red.
  • Scarlet King Snakes: Scarlet king snakes, which are non-venomous, also have red, black, and yellow rings. However, their pattern differs from that of coral snakes. The rings on scarlet king snakes are arranged as follows: red, black, yellow, black, red (or blue).

Tail Coloring

  • Coral Snakes: The tails of coral snakes are always black and yellow, with no other colors present.
  • Scarlet King Snakes: Scarlet king snakes, on the other hand, have the same band pattern throughout their entire body, including their tails.

Head Shape and Color

  • Coral Snakes: Coral snakes have black heads with short, rounded snouts.
  • Scarlet King Snakes: Scarlet king snakes have red heads with longer, more pointed snouts.

Rhymes for Distinguishing Snakes in the US

To help you remember the key differences between coral snakes and scarlet king snakes, the following rhymes have been developed:

  • Red touches yellow, it's dangerous: If you see red touching yellow bands on a snake, it is a coral snake and should be avoided.
  • Red touches black, it's a friend: If you see red touching black bands on a snake, it is a scarlet king snake, which is non-venomous.

Behavior

  • Hiding Habits: Coral snakes are typically found in hiding places such as under logs, leaves, and rocks.
  • Climbing Ability: Scarlet king snakes are more agile climbers than coral snakes and may be found in trees or shrubs.
  • Defensive Reactions: When threatened, coral snakes typically move their heads and tails quickly in an erratic manner.
  • Biting Behavior: Coral snakes are not aggressive and will rarely bite unless they feel threatened or are being handled.

Additional Considerations

It is important to note that these rules only apply to certain types of coral snakes found in the United States. There are other species of coral snakes in other parts of the world that may have different characteristics.

If you encounter a snake that you cannot identify, it is best to err on the side of caution and assume that it is venomous. Do not attempt to handle the snake or get close to it. Instead, move away slowly and calmly and contact a professional for assistance.
