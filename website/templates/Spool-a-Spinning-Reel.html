Choosing the Right Fishing Line:

Selecting the appropriate fishing line is crucial for a successful fishing expedition, as it affects the bait's presentation, visibility, and overall fishing experience. Here's a comprehensive guide to choosing the ideal line:

  • Floaty Baits: For buoyant baits that require staying suspended in the water column, monofilament line is an excellent choice. Its stretchy nature allows the bait to move naturally with the water flow, mimicking the behavior of small fish or insects. Monofilament line also helps absorb shock, reducing the risk of breaking off the hook during a strike.

  • Clear Waters: When fishing in clear waters, fluorocarbon line is a highly effective option. Its near-invisibility makes it less noticeable to wary fish, enabling anglers to cast their lines closer to their targets. Fluorocarbon line is also highly sensitive, allowing anglers to feel the slightest nibbles and provides a more direct connection to the lure.

  • Bottom Fishing: For fishing near the bottom, braided line is the preferred choice. Its tight weave construction makes it strong and abrasion-resistant, ensuring durability against rough terrain. Braided line also has low stretch, which allows for more precise casting and better control over the bait's movement.

Loading the Reel:

Once you have chosen the appropriate fishing line, it's time to load it onto the reel. Follow these steps to ensure proper loading:

  • Determine Reel Direction: Start by inspecting the reel to determine its direction of rotation. Reels either turn clockwise or counterclockwise. This information is usually indicated on the reel's body or spool.

  • Open the Bail: Flip up the small handle on the reel, located near the spool, to open the bail. This releases the spool and allows the line to be fed through the reel.

  • Feed the Line: Pass the line through the line guides located on the reel's body. These guides help direct the line onto the spool in a smooth and orderly fashion.

  • Secure the Line: Once the line is threaded through the guides, tie it securely to the spool using an arbor knot. This knot creates a strong and reliable connection between the line and the spool.

  • Leave Extra Line: Leave a short length of extra line at the end of the spool. This extra line will be used for tying on lures or other terminal tackle.

Spooling the Reel:

  • Position the Spool: Place the spool on the floor with the label side facing up. This ensures the line is spooled in the correct direction.

  • Pinch and Crank: Pinch the end of the fishing line between your thumb and forefinger, and slowly begin cranking the reel to wind the line onto the spool.

  • Check for Twists: As you spool the line, periodically check for any twists or tangles. If you notice any, stop winding and gently remove the twist before continuing.

  • Fill the Spool: Fill the reel spool with line until it is about 1/8 inch from the rim. This leaves sufficient room for tying on a lure without overfilling the spool.

  • Secure the Line: Once the spool is full, cut the line and secure it to the spool using a lure or other appropriate rigging. This prevents the line from unwinding when the reel is not in use.


In conclusion, understanding the nuances of fishing line and its proper loading onto your reel is crucial for a successful fishing experience. By selecting the appropriate line type based on your bait and fishing conditions, you optimize bait visibility, sensitivity, and durability. Additionally, proper loading techniques ensure smooth operation and prevent tangles, maximizing your chances of making that perfect catch. Whether you're a seasoned angler or just starting out, these tips will equip you with the knowledge and techniques to level up your fishing skills and enhance your time on the water.
