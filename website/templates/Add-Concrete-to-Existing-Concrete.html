Discover the Step-by-Step Guide to Adding Concrete to Existing Concrete, with Visual Aids


Transform Your Old Concrete into a Pristine Canvas!

Do you have dull, dingy concrete that's an eyesore to your home? It's not too late to restore it to its former glory! Join us on a step-by-step journey to clean and refresh your old concrete, leaving it looking brand new.

From removing stubborn debris and scrubbing out unsightly stains to setting up a sturdy slab perimeter and pouring a rejuvenating primer coat, we'll guide you through every crucial step. We'll even show you how to add a fresh top layer that will breathe new life into your concrete, making it a vibrant addition to your home's aesthetic.

Get ready to unveil a pristine concrete canvas that will elevate your outdoor space and turn heads. Let's dive right in and give your concrete the makeover it deserves!


Cleaning Old Concrete

Step 1: Remove Debris

  • Sweep or brush away loose dirt, leaves, sand, and any other debris from the concrete surface. Use a hand broom or a push broom for larger areas.
  • Remove any weeds or grass that may have sprouted in the cracks or joints. Use a stiff-bristled brush or a weeding tool to loosen the roots and pull them out.
  • If there are any heavy stains or spills, such as oil or grease, apply a degreasing agent and let it sit for the recommended time before removing it.

Step 2: Power Wash

  • Use a pressure washer with a wide nozzle to remove any remaining debris, especially from the cracks and crevices.
  • Hold the nozzle approximately 6-12 inches from the surface and use a side-to-side motion to avoid damaging the concrete.
  • If there are any stubborn stains, increase the pressure or try using a stronger cleaning solution.

Step 3: Scrub Stains

  • For tough stains that cannot be removed by power washing, use a commercial concrete cleaner or trisodium phosphate (TSP).
  • Dilute the cleaner according to the manufacturer's instructions and apply it to the stained area.
  • Use a stiff-bristled brush or a scrub pad to work the cleaner into the stain.
  • Allow the cleaner to sit for the recommended time before rinsing it off thoroughly with water.

Step 4: Saturate Surface

  • Before mixing and pouring new concrete, thoroughly wet the old concrete surface with a hose.
  • This will help to prevent the new concrete from absorbing too much water from the old concrete, which can lead to cracks and other problems.
  • Allow the surface to soak for 15-30 minutes before proceeding.

Setting Up a Slab Perimeter

Step 1: Measure Area

  • Determine the length and width of the area you want to fill with concrete. Use a measuring tape or a laser level to ensure accuracy.
  • Mark the perimeter of the area with stakes or string.

Step 2: Mark Height

  • Measure and mark the desired height of the new slab. Use a level to ensure that the slab will be level when it is finished.
  • Mark the desired height on the stakes or string.

Step 3: Calculate Concrete

  • Multiply the length, width, and depth of the area to estimate the amount of concrete you need.
  • Add an additional 10-15% to account for waste and unevenness in the surface.

Step 4: Install Braces

  • Create a mold using wood braces or metal forms. The braces should reach the desired height of the slab.
  • Install the braces around the perimeter of the area, making sure that they are level and securely fastened.

Step 5: Support Braces

  • Dig out soil around the braces and insert wooden stakes or metal pins to hold them upright.
  • Tamp or drive the stakes into the ground to ensure that the braces are securely supported.

Step 6: Check Level

  • Use a bubble level to check that the braces are level and that the perimeter of the slab is square.
  • Make any necessary adjustments to the braces or stakes.

Step 7: Lay Wire Mesh

  • Place wire mesh between the braces to strengthen the concrete. The wire mesh should be made of galvanized steel or another corrosion-resistant material.
  • Cut the wire mesh to size and bend it to fit around the perimeter of the slab.
  • Secure the wire mesh to the braces using wire ties or nails.

Pouring a Primer Coat

Step 1: Choose Fine Aggregate Concrete

  • Use concrete with smaller stones or aggregates for thin coats, such as a self-leveling or patching concrete.
  • These types of concrete will flow more easily and create a smoother finish.

Step 2: Mix Scratch Coat

  • Combine concrete with water in a 1:7 ratio to create a liquid mix. The consistency should be similar to pancake batter.
  • Mix thoroughly until there are no lumps or clumps.

Step 3: Spread Scratch Coat

  • Pour and spread the liquid mix over the old concrete to a thickness of about 0.32 cm (1/8 inch).
  • Use a trowel or screed to level and smooth the surface.
  • Allow the scratch coat to dry for at least 24 hours before proceeding.

Adding the Top Layer

Step 1: Mix Concrete

  • Follow the manufacturer's instructions to mix concrete. Use a ratio of 1 part cement to 2 parts sand to 3 parts gravel.
  • Add water gradually until the concrete reaches a workable consistency. It should be thick enough to hold its shape but flowable enough to spread easily.
  • If using regular concrete, add a bonding adhesive to help it adhere to the old concrete.

Step 2: Pour Concrete

  • Pour the concrete onto the old slab to the desired level. Use a trowel or screed to spread the concrete evenly.
  • Avoid overworking the concrete, as this can weaken it.

Step 3: Smooth Concrete

  • Use a trowel to level and smooth the concrete surface. Use a back-and-forth motion to eliminate any bumps or ridges.
  • Finish the surface by using a floating tool or brush to create a smooth, even finish.

Step 4: Protect Concrete

  • Spray a curing compound or cover the concrete with plastic to prevent it from drying out prematurely.
  • Curing time will vary depending on the type of concrete used. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for proper curing procedures.

Conclusion:

By following these comprehensive steps, you can effectively clean old concrete and prepare it for a fresh overlay. Remember, thorough preparation is crucial for a successful renovation project. With a bit of effort and care, you can restore your old concrete to its former glory or even give it a completely new look. Whether you choose to add a thin primer coat for improved bonding or pour a full top layer, these techniques will ensure a durable and aesthetically pleasing result. So, gather your materials, get to work, and transform your old concrete surfaces into something remarkable.
