Part 1: Pruning Your Poinsettia

Pruning your poinsettia promotes healthy growth, prevents legginess, and enhances its appearance. Follow these steps to ensure proper pruning:

  • Step 1: Protect Your Hands: Poinsettia sap can irritate skin, so don sharp gloves to safeguard your hands.
  • Step 2: Remove Dead and Discolored Leaves: Identify any dried, wilted, or discolored leaves. Cut them off using sharp shears at a 45-degree angle to promote new growth.
  • Step 3: Trim Stems: To maintain a compact shape, trim stems to about 6 inches long. This will encourage bushier and fuller growth.
  • Step 4: Prune Regularly: Prune your poinsettia throughout the year to keep it at your desired size and shape. Remove any spindly stems, crossing branches, or yellowing leaves.
  • Step 5: Stop Trimming in Early November: Once early November arrives, stop trimming your poinsettia to allow it to prepare for flowering.

Part 2: Caring for Your Poinsettia

Providing proper care for your poinsettia ensures its health and longevity. Follow these guidelines to keep your plant thriving:

  • Watering: Water your poinsettia only when the soil feels dry to the touch. Overwatering can lead to root rot. Use lukewarm water and avoid getting the leaves wet.
  • Sunlight: Provide ample sunlight but avoid placing your poinsettia in direct sunlight, as this can scorch the leaves. East- or north-facing windows are ideal.
  • Temperature: Keep your poinsettia in a temperature range of at least 55°F (13°C) to prevent cold damage. Avoid extreme temperature fluctuations.
  • Darkness for Red Leaves: To achieve vibrant red leaves, your poinsettia requires complete darkness for 13 hours each day for 8-10 weeks. Starting in mid-September, cover your plant with a black cloth or place it in a dark room from 6 pm to 7 am daily.
