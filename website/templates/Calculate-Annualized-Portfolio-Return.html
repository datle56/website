Discover the Step-by-Step Guide to Accurately Calculating the Annualized Return on Your Investment Portfolio


Unveiling the Mystery: Time-Weighted vs. Dollar-Weighted Rates of Return

Measuring portfolio performance can be a complex task, but two key metrics stand out: time-weighted rate of return (TWRR) and dollar-weighted rate of return (IRR). In this blog, we delve into the intricacies of these two calculations, exploring their differences and providing a step-by-step guide to their analysis. Join us as we uncover the secrets behind these financial performance indicators, empowering you to make informed investment decisions.


Time-Weighted Rate of Return (TWRR)

The Time-Weighted Rate of Return (TWRR) is a measure of portfolio performance that excludes the impact of deposits and withdrawals. It provides a standardized metric for comparing portfolio returns over time, as it solely focuses on the price appreciation of the underlying investments.

Calculating the TWRR:

  1. Determine the Annual Rates of Return: For each year, calculate the difference between the beginning and ending portfolio values. Divide the difference by the beginning portfolio value to obtain the annual rate of return.
  2. Cumulative Product: Add 1 to each annual rate of return. Multiply these values together to obtain a cumulative product.
  3. Geometric Average: Divide the cumulative product by the number of years (n). Then, subtract 1 and multiply by 100 to express the result as an annualized rate of return percentage.

Formula:

TWRR = [(1 + r1) * (1 + r2) * ... * (1 + rn)]^(1/n) - 1 * 100%

Where:

  • r1, r2, ..., rn are the annual rates of return
  • n is the number of years

Dollar-Weighted Rate of Return (IRR)

The Dollar-Weighted Rate of Return (IRR) considers the impact of deposits and withdrawals on portfolio performance. It measures the compound annual growth rate of the actual cash flows invested and withdrawn from the portfolio over time.

Calculating the IRR:

To calculate the IRR, you can use a spreadsheet or a financial calculator that supports the XIRR function.

  1. List Transactions: Enter the dates and amounts of all contributions and withdrawals in a spreadsheet.
  2. XIRR Formula: Enter the formula: =XIRR(values, dates, [guess]). The "values" are the cash flows, the "dates" are the corresponding transaction dates, and the "guess" is an optional initial estimate of the IRR.
  3. Result: The program will calculate the annualized dollar-weighted IRR.

Importance of TWRR and IRR

Both TWRR and IRR are valuable performance metrics, each serving a specific purpose:

  • TWRR: Provides a measure of the underlying investment performance without the influence of cash inflows and outflows.
  • IRR: Assesses the impact of deposits and withdrawals on overall portfolio returns.

By using both TWRR and IRR, investors can gain a comprehensive view of their portfolio's performance, considering both the price appreciation and the impact of cash flow activities.


Conclusion

Understanding the difference between time-weighted and dollar-weighted rate of return is crucial for accurate portfolio performance measurement. Time-weighted return provides a simple average of annual returns, while dollar-weighted return accounts for the impact of cash flows (deposits and withdrawals). Investors should choose the appropriate metric based on their specific portfolio objectives and whether they wish to emphasize the pure performance of the investments or include the effect of cash flows. Both measures are valuable tools for evaluating long-term investment returns and optimizing portfolio strategies.
