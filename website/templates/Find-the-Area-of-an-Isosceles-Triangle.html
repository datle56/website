Method 1: Using Side Lengths

Step 1: Label the Base

Identify the third side of the isosceles triangle as the base (b).

Step 2: Draw the Height

Draw a perpendicular line segment from the midpoint of the base to the opposite vertex. This line segment forms the height (h) of the triangle. It divides the isosceles triangle into two right triangles.

Step 3: Find the Height Using Pythagoras Theorem

Apply the Pythagorean theorem to one of the right triangles:

(h)^2 = (s)^2 - (b/2)^2

where:

  • h is the height
  • s is the length of one equal side
  • b is the length of the base

Step 4: Calculate the Area

Substitute the values of the base and height into the formula for the area of a triangle:

Area = (1/2) * b * h

Method 2: Using Trigonometry

Step 1: Identify Known Values

Determine the length of one equal side (s) and the angle between them (θ) in the isosceles triangle.

Step 2: Divide into Right Triangles

Mentally divide the isosceles triangle into two right triangles by imagining a vertical line drawn from the vertex opposite the known angle to the base.

Step 3: Calculate Height and Base Using Trigonometry

Use the cosine function to find the height (h):

cos(θ/2) = h/s

Use the sine function to find half the base (x):

sin(θ/2) = x/s

Step 4: Calculate the Area

Multiply the values of the base (2x) and height together and substitute them into the formula for the area of a triangle:

Area = (1/2) * (2x) * h


In conclusion, calculating the area of an isosceles triangle can be conveniently achieved through two methods. By employing either the side lengths or trigonometric functions, it's possible to accurately determine the triangle's area.
