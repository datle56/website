Get Hitched in the Lone Star State: A Comprehensive Guide

Embarking on the marital journey in the vibrant state of Texas is a relatively seamless and straightforward process. Here's a detailed guide to navigate the steps involved:

Step 1: Securing the Marriage License

  • Initiate the process by applying for a marriage license at least 72 hours prior to your wedding ceremony. This waiting period provides ample time for the required processing and review.
  • Conveniently complete the application online or visit the county clerk's office with your partner to fill out the necessary paperwork.
  • Present valid government-issued identification, such as your driver's license or passport, and provide your social security card for verification purposes.
  • Finalize the process by tendering the license fee, which typically ranges between $67 and $84.

Step 2: Celebrating the Ceremony

  • As the day of your union approaches, engage a qualified officiant who is legally authorized to perform marriage ceremonies in Texas. Options include priests, ministers, judges, and others who meet the relevant requirements.
  • Designate a meaningful setting for your ceremony, whether it's an intimate backyard gathering, a grand ballroom, or a serene park.
  • Craft your vows with care and eloquence, expressing the love and commitment you share with your partner.

Step 3: Finalizing Your Matrimonial Status

  • Immediately following the ceremony, request your officiant to complete and sign the marriage license.
  • Promptly return the license to the county clerk's office within 30 days. This crucial step officially registers your marriage and provides you with the legal documentation to support your new marital status.
  • Once the license has been processed and approved, you are legally recognized as a married couple in the state of Texas.

Important Notes:

  • The 72-hour waiting period after obtaining your license is mandatory, unless you or your partner are:
    • Active military personnel
    • Have completed a state-approved premarital education course
  • Same-sex marriages are fully recognized and legal in Texas.
  • Alternatively, Texas law permits common law marriages. This is established when a couple lives together as spouses and publicly presents themselves as married without a formal ceremony or license. However, it's essential to note that common law marriages may not be recognized in all jurisdictions or for all legal purposes.
