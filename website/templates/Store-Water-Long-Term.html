Prepare for the Unexpected: A Guide to Safe Water Storage

In the wake of natural disasters or other emergencies, access to clean water becomes paramount. Planning ahead can ensure your family has a safe supply when disaster strikes. In this comprehensive guide, we delve into the essential steps to prepare and store safe water containers for any contingency.

Part 1: Choosing and Preparing Containers

The foundation of safe water storage lies in selecting the right containers. Food-grade plastic containers, specifically HDPE #2, LDPE #4, or PP #5, or stainless steel containers ensure water quality. Avoid containers used for toxic substances, such as PVC (#3), polystyrene (#6), or polycarbonate (#7).

Before use, thoroughly clean containers with hot soapy water. To disinfect, submerge them in bleach or boil them. If your water supply is contaminated, boil it or use bleach to purify it. Filters can effectively remove impurities like lead and heavy metals.


Part 1: Preparing Safe Water Containers

Choosing Safe Containers:

  • Food-Grade Plastic: Opt for plastic containers made from HDPE #2, LDPE #4, or PP #5, as these are approved for food contact and do not leach harmful chemicals.
  • Stainless Steel: Stainless steel containers are durable, rust-resistant, and non-toxic, making them an excellent choice for storing drinking water.

Avoiding Toxic Substances:

  • PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride #3): Avoid containers made from PVC, as this material releases potentially harmful chemicals that can contaminate water.
  • Polystyrene (#6): Polystyrene is not suitable for storing water as it can leach styrene, a known carcinogen, into the liquid.
  • Polycarbonate (#7): Polycarbonate containers may release bisphenol A (BPA), an endocrine disruptor that can pose health risks.

Cleaning and Disinfecting Containers:

  • Thorough Cleaning: Use hot soapy water to thoroughly clean all containers before use. Remove any debris or residue left from the manufacturing process.
  • Bleach Disinfection: Mix one teaspoon of bleach per gallon of water and fill containers to the brim. Let stand for 30 minutes, then drain and rinse thoroughly.
  • Boiling Disinfection: Boil enough water to fill the containers and pour it in. Let stand for 15 minutes, then drain and rinse thoroughly.

Water Treatment if Necessary:

  • Boiling: If your water supply is unsafe, bring it to a rolling boil for at least one minute to kill harmful bacteria and viruses.
  • Bleach Treatment: Add eight drops of liquid bleach (5.25% concentration) per gallon of water. Mix thoroughly and let stand for 30 minutes before using.
  • Filtration: Use water filters designed to remove specific impurities, such as sediment, lead, or heavy metals.

Part 2: Storing Water Safely

Proper Sealing and Labeling:

  • Tight Sealing: Ensure containers are sealed tightly with lids or caps to prevent contamination from the environment.
  • Labeling: Clearly label containers with "drinking water" and the date of bottling. This ensures easy identification and helps track shelf life.

Storage Location:

  • Cool and Dark: Store water in a cool (below 70°F), dark place to minimize bacterial growth and maintain freshness.

Emergency Preparation:

  • Three-Day Supply: Keep accessible containers with a three-day supply of drinking water near an exit in case of an emergency.
  • Regular Check-Ups: Inspect your stored water supply every six months to ensure its quality and replace unopened, store-bought water as needed.

Plastic Container Maintenance:

  • Cloudiness or Damage: Replace plastic containers if they become cloudy or damaged, as this may indicate leaching of harmful chemicals.
  • Open Containers: Open one container at a time and store open water in a refrigerator for up to five days. Discard any unused water after this period.

Conclusion:

By following these comprehensive guidelines, you can effectively prepare and store safe water for emergency situations. Proper water storage safeguards your health and ensures that you have access to clean drinking water when it matters most. Remember to regularly check and replenish your water supply, and stay informed about local water quality reports to avoid any potential contamination risks. By taking these precautions, you can feel confident knowing that you are well-equipped to handle any water shortage or emergency that may arise.
