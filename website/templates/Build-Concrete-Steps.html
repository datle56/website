Part 1: Planning Your Concrete Steps

Key Terms:

  • Riser: The vertical component of each step, measuring the height between two treads.
  • Tread: The horizontal component of each step, which you step onto.
  • Pitch line: An imaginary line drawn from the bottom edge of the bottom riser to the top edge of the top riser, indicating the incline of the stairs.
  • Pitch: The angle of the stairs, determined by the ratio of the rise to the run.
  • Rise: The total height of the stairs, calculated by multiplying the number of risers by the riser height.
  • Run: The depth of the stairs, measured from the front edge of one tread to the front edge of the next.
  • Stringers: The side supports that provide structural integrity to the staircase, connecting the treads and risers.

Steps:

  1. Measure the dimensions: Determine the overall rise, run, and width of the stairs using a level, measuring tape, and stakes or string. The width typically ranges between 36 to 48 inches for residential stairs.
  2. Calculate the number of stairs: Divide the total rise by your desired riser height, which is typically between 6 and 8 inches. This will determine the number of risers and treads.
  3. Determine tread height and depth: The tread depth should be at least 11 inches to ensure comfortable stepping. The tread height is calculated by dividing the total rise by the number of risers.
  4. Find the length of the stringers: Use the Pythagorean theorem to calculate the length and angle of the stringers. The formula is: Length² = Rise² + Run², and Angle = arctan(Rise / Run).
  5. Plan for handrails if needed: Determine the location, height, and style of handrails for safety and support.

Part 2: Groundwork

Steps:

  1. Stake out the base: Mark the area where the stairs will be constructed using stakes, string, or spray paint.
  2. Excavate the area: Dig a trench below the ground level to the depth of the stringers and formwork, typically around 6 to 12 inches.
  3. Cut the pieces for the form: Prepare the lumber for the risers and stringers using a saw and ensuring precise measurements.
  4. Assemble the concrete form: Screw or nail the risers to the stringers, creating the framework for the concrete steps. Secure the form to the ground to prevent movement during the pour.
  5. Lay the subbase: Fill the excavated trench with compacted gravel or crushed stone to provide a stable base for the concrete.
  6. Place the form: Position the assembled form over the subbase and use a level to ensure that it is level both front to back and side to side.
  7. Build a rebar cage: Install rebar within the form to reinforce the concrete, improving its strength and durability. Tie the rebar together using wire or zip ties.

Part 3: Pouring and Finishing

Steps:

  1. Mix the concrete: Follow the instructions on the concrete mix bag or consult with a professional for the correct proportions and mixing method.
  2. Pour the concrete: Carefully fill the form with concrete, ensuring that it is evenly distributed and there are no voids or air pockets.
  3. Screed the surface: Use a screeding tool to level the concrete, removing any excess material and creating a smooth surface.
  4. Float the concrete: Use a float to smooth the concrete further and bring the "cream" to the surface, creating a denser and more durable finish.
  5. Brush the concrete: Immediately after floating, drag a stiff-bristled brush across the surface to create traction and prevent slipping. This process is known as "brooming" or "texturing."
  6. Allow the concrete to cure: Cover the concrete with plastic wrap or burlap and keep it moist for several days to allow it to cure properly. The curing process can take up to 28 days for the concrete to reach its full strength.
