Mange in Dogs: A Comprehensive Guide to Symptoms, Types, and Treatment

Mange is a skin condition in dogs that is caused by parasitic mites. It is highly contagious and can affect dogs of all ages and breeds. There are two main types of mange in dogs: sarcoptic mange and demodectic mange.

Symptoms of Mange

Regardless of the type of mange, common symptoms include:

  • Intense Itching: Dogs with mange experience constant and severe itching, which can be extremely uncomfortable and disruptive.
  • Hair Loss: Mange often causes hair loss in affected areas of the skin. The hair loss can be patchy or complete, depending on the severity of the infestation.
  • Swollen Feet: In some cases, especially with demodectic mange, the feet may become swollen and irritated. This condition is known as demodectic pododermatitis.

Types of Mange

1. Sarcoptic Mange:

  • Causative Agent: Sarcoptes scabiei var. canis
  • Transmission: Highly contagious, spread through direct contact with infested animals
  • Symptoms: Intense and widespread itching, crusty skin lesions, severe hair loss, skin inflammation, and bacterial infections
  • Diagnosis: Pinnal-pedal scratch test (a positive result indicates the presence of mites)

2. Demodectic Mange:

  • Causative Agent: Demodex canis
  • Transmission: Not highly contagious, often occurs in dogs with weakened immune systems
  • Symptoms: Localized or generalized hair loss, bald patches, skin irritation and thickening, demodectic pododermatitis
  • Diagnosis: Skin scraping or biopsy to confirm the presence of mites

Diagnosis of Mange

To properly diagnose mange in dogs, a veterinarian will conduct a thorough physical examination and collect relevant information about the dog's history and symptoms. They may also perform diagnostic tests, such as:

  • Pinnal-pedal Scratch Test: This test is used specifically to detect sarcoptic mange. A veterinarian will gently scratch the inside of the dog's ear flap and observe if the dog scratches its ear in response. A positive result indicates the presence of mites.
  • Skin Scraping or Biopsy: These tests involve collecting skin samples from affected areas to examine under a microscope to look for mites or other abnormalities.

Treatment for Mange

The treatment for mange depends on the type of mite infestation and the severity of the condition. Typically, treatment involves a combination of medicated shampoos, topical or oral medications, and environmental management.

  • Medicated Shampoos: Veterinarians prescribe medicated shampoos that contain ingredients such as permethrin or lime sulfur to kill mites and reduce inflammation.
  • Topical or Oral Medications: Oral or topical medications, such as ivermectin, milbemycin, or sarolaner, are used to systemically kill mites throughout the body.
  • Environmental Management: Cleaning and disinfection of the dog's bedding, toys, and other gear is essential to prevent re-infestation. Dogs with sarcoptic mange may need to be quarantined to prevent spreading the infection to other pets.

Prevention of Mange

Preventing mange in dogs is crucial for maintaining their health and well-being. Here are some preventive measures:

  • Avoid Exposure to Wild Animals: Avoid allowing dogs to come into contact with wild animals, such as infected foxes, coyotes, or raccoons, which can carry the mites that cause mange.
  • Keep the Dog's Environment Clean: Regularly clean the dog's bedding, toys, and other accessories to prevent the accumulation of mites.
  • Seek Prompt Veterinary Care: If you suspect that your dog may have mange, seek veterinary care immediately. Early diagnosis and treatment can prevent serious complications and discomfort.

Conclusion

Mange is a common yet highly manageable canine skin condition. Prompt diagnosis and treatment are crucial to alleviate the discomfort and prevent complications. With a combination of proper veterinary care, diligent home management, and preventive measures, dogs with mange can live happy and healthy lives. Remember, early detection is key, so don't hesitate to seek professional help if you suspect your dog may be affected by this condition.
