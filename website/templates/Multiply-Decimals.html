How to Multiply Decimals: A Comprehensive Guide

Multiplying decimals is an essential mathematical operation used in various scenarios. Whether it's calculating discounts, solving scientific problems, or measuring distances, understanding how to multiply decimals is crucial. Here's a detailed step-by-step guide to help you master this operation:

Step 1: Line Up the Numbers

  • Align the two decimal numbers vertically, with the decimal points positioned directly below each other.
  • Ensure that the digits of each number are aligned, creating a neat grid-like structure.
  • Example: To multiply 2.25 by 0.75, align them as follows:

2.25 x 0.75

Step 2: Ignore the Decimals and Multiply

  • Treat the numbers as whole numbers and perform the multiplication process as usual.
  • Multiply each digit of the first number by each digit of the second number, carrying any tens or hundreds as needed.
  • Example: Ignoring the decimals, we multiply 225 (2.25 treated as a whole number) by 75 (0.75 treated as a whole number), resulting in 16875.

Step 3: Count the Decimal Places

  • Count the number of digits to the right of the decimal point in each of the original numbers.
  • Example: In 2.25, there are two digits to the right of the decimal point, and in 0.75, there are two digits as well.

Step 4: Move the Decimal

  • From the product obtained in Step 2, move the decimal point to the left by the total number of digits counted in Step 3.
  • This will ensure that the product retains the correct number of decimal places.
  • Example: Since there were a total of four digits to the right of the decimal point (two from each number), we move the decimal in 16875 four places to the left, resulting in 0.016875.

Step 5: Add Zeroes if Needed

  • If necessary, add zeros to fill in any additional spaces that may appear after moving the decimal.
  • This ensures that the product has the correct number of decimal places.
  • Example: In our case, there are five digits to the right of the decimal point after moving it (instead of four as expected), so we add a zero to fill the extra space: 0.016875.
