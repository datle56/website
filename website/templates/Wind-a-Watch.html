Manual Movement Watches

Step 1: Locate the Stem

Orient the watch with the dial side facing upwards and place it on a flat surface. Identify the small knob or protrusion located at the side of the watch case, usually between the 2 and 4 o'clock positions. This is the stem.

Step 2: Wind the Watch

Gently pull out the stem until it reaches the second or third click-point. This disengages the stem from the hands-setting mechanism. Now, rotate the stem clockwise in steady, rhythmic motions. As you wind, you will feel increasing resistance. This resistance indicates that the mainspring inside the watch is being wound.

Step 3: Return the Stem

Once you feel significant resistance, stop winding and gently push the stem back into its original position. Ensure that the stem is fully engaged and flush with the case.

Automatic Movement Watches

Step 1: Locate the Crown

With the watch face facing upwards, inspect the side of the case for the crown. It is a larger, protruding knob typically found at the 3 o'clock position.

Step 2: Wind the Watch

Pull out the crown from its recessed position. Then, turn the crown clockwise with steady, even strokes. You will notice a slight resistance as the rotor inside the watch winds the mainspring.

Care for Your Watch

1. Regular Winding:

  • For manual watches, wind the watch daily to ensure it stays powered.
  • For automatic watches, wear the watch for at least 8 hours per day to keep it wound through natural arm movements.

2. Cleaning:

  • Use a soft toothbrush and warm water to gently clean the watch case, bracelet, and crystal. Avoid using harsh chemicals or abrasive cleaners.
  • If necessary, use a mild soap solution to remove stubborn dirt or grime.

3. Storage:

  • Store the watch in a cool, dry, and dust-free environment.
  • For prolonged storage, consider placing the watch in a watch box or case that provides protection from moisture and dust.
