Uninstalling app updates is a breeze on Android devices, with three simple methods to restore apps to their original versions.


Want to revert to an earlier version of an app but don't know how? You're not alone! Many users encounter situations where the latest app update causes issues or compromises their preferred functionality. In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk you through three proven methods to downgrade apps on Android devices, whether you're running Android 8.0 or later, or earlier versions like Android 7.0. Get ready to reclaim control over your apps and enjoy the best versions that suit your needs!


Method 1: Uninstalling Pre-Installed App Updates

Step 1: Access Device's Settings and App Management

  • Navigate to your device's Settings menu.
  • Locate and tap on the "Apps" or "Applications" option.

Step 2: Enable System Apps Visibility

  • Scroll down and find the "Show system apps" option.
  • Toggle this option to enable the display of pre-installed apps that are not typically visible.

Step 3: Select the App to Downgrade

  • From the list of apps, scroll through until you locate the specific app you wish to downgrade.

Step 4: Uninstall App Updates

  • Tap on the selected app.
  • Locate the "Uninstall updates" option from the menu.
  • Confirm the uninstallation to revert the app to its original, factory-installed version.

Method 2: Installing an Older Version of an App (Android 8.0 and Later)

Step 1: Acquire APK Installation Tool

  • Open the Google Play Store.
  • Search for and install the "APK Installer" app.

Step 2: Determine Device Specifications

  • Install the "Droid Hardware Info" app from the Play Store.
  • Launch the app and gather the following information:
    • Android OS version
    • Display density (DPI)
    • CPU architecture (ARM, ARM64, x86, x86_64)

Step 3: Uninstall Current App Version

  • Return to your device's Settings menu.
  • Navigate to the Apps section.
  • Select and uninstall the app you wish to install an older version of.

Step 4: Enable Unknown Source App Installation

  • Open the Settings menu on your device.
  • Locate the "Security" or "Privacy" section.
  • Enable the option "Install apps from unknown sources."

Step 5: Find Older Version of App

  • Visit the APK Mirror website using a browser.
  • Search for the app you are interested in.
  • Filter the search results based on your device's specifications:
    • Android OS version
    • DPI
    • CPU architecture

Step 6: Download and Install Older Version

  • Find the appropriate version of the app.
  • Tap on the download link.
  • Once downloaded, open the APK Installer app.
  • Navigate to the downloaded APK file and tap on it.
  • Follow the on-screen instructions to complete the installation.

Method 3: Installing an Older Version of an App (Android 7.0 and Earlier)

Step 1: Determine Device Specifications and Uninstall App

  • Follow steps 1-4 of Method 2.

Step 2: Enable App Installation from Unknown Sources

  • Refer to Step 4 of Method 2.

Step 3: Locate and Download Older Version

  • Visit the APK Mirror website and select the appropriate version of the app.
  • Tap on the download link.

Step 4: Manual Installation

  • Open the Downloads folder on your device.
  • Locate the downloaded APK file.
  • Tap on the APK file and follow the on-screen instructions to install the older version of the app.

Conclusion: Choose Your Downgrading Method Wisely

The choice of downgrading method depends on your device's operating system version. If you're using Android 8.0 or later, Method 2 offers a more straightforward approach by installing an older version through APK Installer. For devices running Android 7.0 and earlier, Method 3 requires installing the APK file manually.

Remember to exercise caution while installing apps from unknown sources. Ensure you obtain the APK files from reputable websites like APK Mirror. Always verify the permissions requested by the app before granting them access.

By following these methods, you can effectively downgrade apps to suit your preferences or restore older versions for compatibility purposes.
