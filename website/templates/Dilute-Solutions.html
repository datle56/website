Method 1: Accurate Dilutions Using a Formula

Step 1: Know Your Variables

  • Starting solution concentration (C1): The concentration of the solution you start with.
  • Final solution concentration (C2): The desired concentration of the final diluted solution.
  • Final solution volume (V2): The desired volume of the final diluted solution.
  • Starting solution volume (V1): The unknown volume of the starting solution needed to prepare the desired final solution.

Step 2: Use the Dilution Equation

The dilution equation is C1V1 = C2V2, where:

  • C1 = Initial solution concentration
  • V1 = Initial solution volume
  • C2 = Final solution concentration
  • V2 = Final solution volume

Step 3: Solve for the Unknown Volume (V1)

To find the unknown starting volume (V1), rearrange the dilution equation as follows:

V1 = (C2V2) / C1

Step 4: Practical Application

  • Calculate the starting volume (V1) using the formula above.
  • Measure out the calculated starting volume of the concentrated solution.
  • Add diluting liquid to the concentrated solution until the total volume reaches the desired final volume (V2).

Method 2: Simple Dilutions for Home Use

Step 1: Check the Packaging

Many household cleaning products and other liquids have dilution instructions printed on their packaging.

  • Check the packaging for the recommended dilution ratio.
  • Determine the desired amount of diluted solution.

Step 2: Add the Concentrate to the Diluting Liquid

  • Measure out the recommended amount of concentrated product using a measuring cup or spoon.
  • Gradually add the concentrate to the diluting liquid (usually water) while stirring gently.

Step 3: Ignore Powder Volume

When diluting small amounts of powder concentrates in liquids, such as laundry detergents or drink mixes, the volume of the powder can typically be ignored.

  • Add the recommended amount of powder to the diluting liquid.
  • Stir until dissolved or mixed thoroughly.
