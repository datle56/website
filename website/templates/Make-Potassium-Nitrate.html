Part 1: Making the Ammonium Nitrate Solution

  • Acquire a commercially available cold pack containing ammonium nitrate. Carefully extract approximately 80 milliliters of the solution using a syringe or pipette.
  • In a separate heat-resistant container, measure out 70 milliliters of boiling hot water.
  • Slowly pour the ammonium nitrate solution into the hot water while gently stirring to ensure complete dissolution.
  • To purify the solution, line a funnel with a coffee filter and carefully pour the mixture through it. This step removes impurities and particulates that may interfere with the crystallization process.

Part 2: Combining Ingredients

  • Measure out 56 grams of potassium hydroxide using a precise scale.
  • Gradually add a small amount of water to the potassium hydroxide, stirring continuously until a thick paste forms. This paste will be highly caustic and corrosive; exercise caution.
  • Donning a gas mask and protective gloves, carefully transport both the ammonium nitrate solution and the potassium hydroxide paste to an outdoor area with adequate ventilation.
  • Slowly and carefully pour the potassium hydroxide paste into the ammonium nitrate solution while stirring. A chemical reaction will occur, releasing ammonia gas. Take appropriate precautions to avoid inhalation of the gas.

Part 3: Crystallizing the Potassium Nitrate

  • Transfer the combined solution to a suitable vessel and place it over a heat source. Bring the solution to a boil and maintain a gentle simmer until crystals begin to form around the edges of the container.
  • Remove the vessel from the heat and allow it to cool to room temperature.
  • Transfer the solution to a shallow dish and place it in a well-ventilated outdoor area for 1-2 weeks. The solution will evaporate slowly, leaving behind white crystals of potassium nitrate.
  • To verify the purity of the crystals, dissolve a small amount in water and mix it with an equal amount of sugar. Carefully ignite the mixture. If it produces a purplish flame, it indicates the presence of pure potassium nitrate.

Conclusion

Congratulations! You have successfully created potassium nitrate crystals, an essential ingredient in many fireworks and explosives. It's crucial to remember that this process must be carried out with extreme caution and safety precautions. Always wear appropriate personal protective equipment, especially a gas mask, and conduct the experiment outdoors. The resulting potassium nitrate crystals should be handled cautiously and stored securely away from any potential ignition sources. Use them responsibly and enjoy the wonders of chemistry!
