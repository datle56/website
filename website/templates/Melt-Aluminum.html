Method 1: Melting Aluminum in a Small Foundry

Materials:

  • Small foundry
  • Crucible
  • Propane torch
  • Aluminum cans

Process:

  1. Set up the foundry and propane torch: Place the foundry on a stable surface and connect the propane torch to the fuel source. Ensure the torch is properly adjusted for a stable flame.
  2. Prepare the aluminum: Crush aluminum cans flat or into smaller pieces to fit into the crucible. Remove any impurities or debris that may contaminate the aluminum.
  3. Place aluminum in crucible and heat: Fill the crucible with the prepared aluminum. Use metal tongs to handle the crucible and place it inside the foundry. Turn on the propane torch and direct the flame towards the bottom of the crucible.
  4. Melt the aluminum: Gradually increase the heat as the aluminum begins to melt. Stir the molten aluminum occasionally to ensure even heating. Continue heating until all the aluminum has liquefied.
  5. Remove and pour the molten aluminum: When the aluminum is fully melted, use metal tongs to carefully remove the crucible from the foundry. Pour the molten aluminum into molds or other desired containers.

Method 2: Making a DIY Aluminum Foundry

Materials:

  • Steel bucket (outer body)
  • Plaster of Paris
  • Play sand
  • 2.5-quart bucket (center form)
  • Steel tubing (air supply port)
  • PVC pipe (blower tube)

Process:

  1. Create the outer body: Cut the bottom off the steel bucket to form a cylindrical shape. This will serve as the outer body of the foundry.
  2. Make the lining: Mix plaster of Paris with water to form a thick paste. Apply the paste to the inner surface of the outer body, smoothing it evenly. Allow the plaster to dry completely.
  3. Form the center: Place the 2.5-quart bucket inside the outer body. Fill the space between the two buckets with play sand, packing it tightly. Remove the smaller bucket to create a cavity for the molten aluminum.
  4. Drill air supply port: Drill a hole in the side of the foundry near the bottom. Insert steel tubing into the hole, which will serve as the air supply port.
  5. Create blower tube: Cut a length of PVC pipe and connect it to the air supply port. This will allow air to be blown into the foundry during the melting process.
  6. Make the lid: Cut a piece of metal or plywood for the lid. Drill a vent hole in the lid to allow gases to escape.

Method 3: Preparing to Melt Your Aluminum

Safety:

  • Wear protective gear, including heat-resistant gloves, apron, and safety goggles.
  • Use proper tools for handling hot materials, such as metal tongs and crucibles.
  • Work in a well-ventilated space to avoid exposure to fumes.

Materials:

  • Scrap aluminum (pure sources or pool of molten aluminum preferred)
  • Metal tongs
  • Strainer
  • Crucible
  • Foundry

Tips:

  • Minimize oxidation: Use pure aluminum sources or add a pool of molten aluminum to the crucible to prevent oxidation of the molten metal.
  • Seek assistance from an experienced person: If you are new to melting aluminum, consider working with someone who has experience in the process. They can provide guidance and help ensure your safety.
  • Prepare scrap aluminum: Clean and separate scrap aluminum from other materials. Remove any impurities or coatings that may contaminate the aluminum.
  • Strain the molten aluminum: Before pouring, use a strainer to remove any remaining impurities or dross from the molten aluminum.
