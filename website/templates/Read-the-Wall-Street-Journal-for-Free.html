Method 1: Using a Reader App or Extension

Step-by-Step Guide:

  1. Download Reader App or Extension:

    • For iOS users, download the Read Across The Aisle app from the App Store.
    • For Chrome users, install the Read Across The Aisle extension from the Chrome Web Store.

  2. Open Reader App/Extension:

    • Launch the Read Across The Aisle app on your iOS device or open the Chrome extension in your browser.

  3. Create Free Account:

    • Click on the "Create Account" button within the app/extension.
    • Provide your email address, create a password, and accept the terms of service.

  4. Access Wall Street Journal Articles:

    • Click on the "Wall Street Journal" link within the app/extension.
    • You will be redirected to the Wall Street Journal website.

  5. Read Articles:

    • Browse and select the articles you wish to read.
    • You will have free access to articles for a limited period.

  6. Renew Free Trial:

    • Every 7 days, the free trial will expire.
    • To continue reading, return to the app/extension and renew your free trial.

Important Note:

  • The free trial allows access to a limited number of articles per day.
  • After the free trial expires, you can continue reading articles by subscribing to the Wall Street Journal.

Method 2: Using a Library

Step-by-Step Guide:

  1. Check Library Partnership:

    • Contact your local library to determine if they have a partnership with the Wall Street Journal.

  2. Visit Library:

    • If there is a partnership, visit the library in person or access their online resources.

  3. Access Articles:

    • Use the library's computers or online resources to access the Wall Street Journal website.
    • You may need to log in with your library card number and PIN.

Note:

  • Some libraries may provide a limited number of free articles per month.
  • You may need to renew your access periodically.

Method 3: Finding Links on Social Media

Step-by-Step Guide:

  1. Follow Wall Street Journal Reporters:

    • Follow Wall Street Journal reporters on social media platforms such as Twitter.

  2. Look for Article Links:

    • Reporters often share links to their articles on social media.
    • These links may provide temporary free access to the articles.

  3. Search for "WSJ":

    • Search for "WSJ" or specific article titles on social media.
    • Some users may post article links on their profiles.

Note:

  • Not all articles shared on social media may be available for free reading.
  • The availability of free articles may vary depending on the reporter's settings and the user sharing the link.
