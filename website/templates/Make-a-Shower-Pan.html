Transforming Your Bathroom: A Comprehensive Guide to Building a Shower from Scratch

Step into the realm of home improvement and embark on a journey to create a luxurious shower sanctuary. This comprehensive guide will empower you with the step-by-step knowledge to construct a shower from scratch, ensuring both functionality and aesthetic appeal. Discover the techniques to frame the shower base, waterproof it, and skillfully tile it, turning your bathroom into a masterpiece. Prepare to take your shower experience to new heights of comfort and style!


Part 1: Framing and Waterproofing the Shower Base

Framing the Base

  • Begin by creating the frame that will provide structural support for the shower base. Utilize 2x6 inch lumber for the frame, ensuring a sturdy and reliable foundation.
  • Secure the frame to the subfloor using screws or nails, spacing them approximately 16 inches apart for optimal stability.
  • Construct a raised curb on one side of the frame to serve as a step into the shower. The height of the curb should be approximately 6 inches above the floor level.

Laying Tar Paper

  • Spread tar paper across the floor of the shower base to protect it from moisture. This waterproof barrier will prevent water from seeping into the subfloor and causing damage.
  • Ensure the tar paper extends beyond the perimeter of the frame and up the walls by a few inches to create an effective barrier.

Cutting the Drain Hole

  • Locate the drain hole and carefully cut an opening in the tar paper. The hole should be large enough to accommodate the drain flange while providing a snug fit.
  • Trim any excess tar paper around the drain hole to create a clean and secure seal.

Part 2: Applying Mortar Base

Mixing Mortar

  • Choose a quick-set mortar specifically formulated for shower applications, adhering strictly to the manufacturer's instructions for mixing. Typically, a 3:1 ratio of mortar to water is recommended.
  • Gradually add the water to the mortar, mixing thoroughly with a drill equipped with a paddle attachment. Continue stirring until the mortar achieves a uniform and workable consistency resembling thick peanut butter.

Creating the Mortar Border

  • Using a notched trowel, apply a generous bead of mortar around the perimeter of the framed base. This border will create a positive slope towards the drain, ensuring proper water drainage.
  • Smooth and shape the mortar to form a sloped surface, beginning at the drain and gradually increasing the thickness towards the walls.

Spreading and Sloping

  • Spread the remaining mortar over the entire base, ensuring a uniform thickness throughout. Use a flat trowel to smooth and level the surface, creating a continuous and even slope towards the drain.
  • Allow the mortar to cure for the recommended time, usually around 24 hours, before proceeding to the next step.

Part 3: Waterproofing the Shower

Placing the Shower Liner

  • Cut a piece of shower liner to fit the dimensions of the shower base. Smoothly lay the liner inside the base, tucking it into the mortar border around the edges.
  • Ensure the liner extends over the top of the curb and up the walls by at least 6 inches.

Sealing the Drain Flange

  • Apply a liberal amount of silicone sealant around the drain flange, where it meets the shower base. Smooth the sealant to create a watertight seal, preventing leaks.
  • Allow the sealant to set for the recommended time before proceeding.

Securing the Liner

  • Secure the shower liner to the wall studs using galvanized nails or staples, spacing them approximately 6 inches apart. Ensure the liner is taut and fits snugly against the walls.

Installing the Drain Flange

  • Fit the drain flange into the hole in the shower liner and secure it to the drain pipe using the provided bolts or screws.
  • Apply silicone sealant around the base of the drain flange to ensure a secure and leak-proof connection.

Part 4: Tiling the Shower

Dry-Fitting the Tiles

  • Before applying any adhesive or grout, dry-fit the tiles on the shower base to determine their placement and ensure a cohesive appearance.
  • Adjust the spacing and layout of the tiles as needed to achieve the desired aesthetic.

Applying Thinset Mortar

  • Mix a batch of thinset mortar according to the manufacturer's instructions. It should have a slightly lumpy consistency, similar to peanut butter.
  • Using a notched trowel, spread the thinset mortar over a small section of the shower base, beginning at the drain. Apply the mortar in a uniform thickness to create a strong bond between the tiles and the base.

Setting the Tiles

  • Press the tiles into the thinset mortar, ensuring they are level and secure. Use a rubber mallet or tile spacers to adjust the tiles as needed.
  • Continue the process of applying thinset mortar and setting tiles until the entire shower base is covered.

Grouting the Tiles

  • Once the tiles are securely set, mix a batch of grout according to the manufacturer's instructions. The consistency should be similar to thick peanut butter.
  • Apply the grout to the spaces between the tiles using a grout float or trowel, firmly pressing it into the joints.
  • Use a damp sponge to wipe away excess grout and smooth the surface.
  • Allow the grout to cure for the recommended time, usually around 24 hours, before using the shower.

Conclusion

With the shower base framed, waterproofed, and tiled, you've successfully completed the foundation for a safe and stylish shower. By following these step-by-step instructions, you've ensured a sturdy base that will withstand water and wear-and-tear. The waterproof lining and sloping design prevent moisture from seeping into your walls and subfloor, protecting your bathroom from costly water damage. Now, you're ready to proceed with installing the shower walls and fixtures, bringing your dream shower to life. Remember, attention to detail and proper waterproofing techniques are crucial for a long-lasting and aesthetically pleasing result.
