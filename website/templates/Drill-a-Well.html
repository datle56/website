Planning a Well: A Comprehensive Guide

Evaluating Costs and Benefits: Piping vs. Shipping Water

Before embarking on the well-planning process, it is crucial to assess the costs and benefits of alternative water sources, such as piping or shipping water.

  • Piping: Piping water from a distant source involves high initial costs for infrastructure, maintenance expenses, and potential water quality issues. However, it can provide a reliable and constant supply of water.
  • Shipping: Shipping water by truck is generally more expensive than piping, but it offers flexibility and can be a viable option in remote or underdeveloped areas.

Identifying Well Location

  • Land Records and Surveys: Review land records and property surveys to determine the legal boundaries, any easements or restrictions, and potential well placement options.
  • Previous Wells and Water Table Depth: Research existing wells in the vicinity to gather data on their depth, water levels, and yield. This information can provide insights into the local water table depth and potential well yields.

Researching Groundwater Conditions

  • Geologic and Topographic Maps: Examine geologic and topographic maps to identify areas with potential groundwater resources. Pay attention to rock formations, aquifers, and surface features that indicate groundwater presence.
  • Consulting with Experts: Consult with geological survey agencies, hydrologists, or local well drillers for professional guidance on potential well sites and water availability in the specific area.

Obtaining Permits

Before commencing drilling, it is imperative to obtain necessary permits from local authorities. Typically, these permits include:

  • Drilling Permits: These permits authorize the excavation of the well and specify drilling specifications.
  • Water Use Permits: These permits regulate the withdrawal and use of water from the well.

Drilling the Well

Selecting the Well Location

  • Distance from Contaminants: Choose a well location that is a safe distance away from potential sources of contamination, such as septic tanks, landfills, and agricultural fields.
  • Accessibility: Ensure that the well location is accessible for drilling equipment and maintenance purposes.

Choosing the Drilling Method

  • Auger Method: This method involves using a large auger to bore a hole into the ground. It is most suitable for shallow wells and soft soil conditions.
  • Rotary Method: This method employs a rotating drill bit to cut through rock or soil. It is commonly used for drilling deeper wells in harder formations.
  • Percussion Method: This method uses a heavy weight that is repeatedly dropped to penetrate the ground. It is mostly used for drilling in rocky terrains.
  • Jet Method: This method utilizes high-pressure water to break up soil or rock. It is effective in unstable or sandy formations.

Installing Casing and Finishing the Well

  • Casing: To prevent cave-ins and contamination, casing is installed inside the drilled hole. Casing materials can vary, including steel, plastic, or concrete.
  • Pump: A pump is installed to extract water from the well and bring it to the surface. Pump types can include submersible, jet, or hand pumps.
  • Wellhead: The wellhead is the above-ground portion of the well that provides access for maintenance and protects the well from surface contamination.

Conclusion:

By meticulously planning and executing the well's construction, you can ensure a reliable source of water for your property.
