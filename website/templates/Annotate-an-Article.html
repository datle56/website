Annotating an Article

Introduction

Annotating an article is a crucial academic skill that enhances comprehension, improves recall, and fosters critical thinking. By adding notes, highlights, and other annotations to a text, readers can engage deeply with the material, extracting insights and synthesizing their knowledge.

Benefits of Annotating

  • Enhanced Understanding: Annotating helps readers identify key concepts, main arguments, and supporting evidence. This process solidifies understanding and promotes deeper engagement with the text.
  • Improved Memory and Recall: Writing notes forces readers to actively process information, which strengthens their memory and makes it easier to recall key points later.
  • Critical Thinking: Annotating encourages readers to analyze the text, assess its strengths and weaknesses, and draw connections to their own knowledge and experiences.

General Procedures for Annotating

Step 1: Mark Source Information

  • Clearly record the article's title, author, and source (e.g., journal, website). This information will be essential for future reference and citation.

Step 2: Read and Annotate

  • Read the article carefully, highlighting and annotating important passages.
  • Use different colored highlighters or pens to distinguish between key points, supporting evidence, and personal insights.

Step 3: Ask Questions

  • Note any questions or areas of confusion that arise during your reading. These questions will guide your further research and enhance your comprehension.

Step 4: Highlight Themes

  • Connect the text to your class discussions, personal experiences, or other relevant concepts. This process helps you integrate the information into your existing knowledge framework.

Step 5: Identify Thesis and Topic Sentences

  • Underline the main argument (thesis) of the article and the key points that support it (topic sentences). This will help you structure your annotations and focus on the most important aspects of the text.

Step 6: Research the Author

  • Learn about the author's background, expertise, and motivations. This information can provide valuable context for understanding the article's content.

Step 7: Write Your Opinions

  • Share your thoughts, reactions, and critical insights in the margins or alongside the text. This will help you engage with the material on a personal level.

Step 8: Note Unclear Concepts

  • Circle or mark unfamiliar words or passages that you need to clarify through further research or discussion.

Annotating by Hand

Step 1: Make a Photocopy

  • Create a copy of the article with generous margin space to accommodate annotations.

Step 2: Choose a Writing Tool

  • Use a pencil, highlighter, or Post-it notes for annotations. Choose tools that allow for easy modification and expansion of your notes.

Step 3: Use Post-it Notes

  • For books or borrowed articles, attach Post-it notes with comments or questions. This method allows you to annotate without damaging the original text.

Step 4: Complete an Annotation Paragraph

  • After reading and annotating the article, summarize the main points and highlight important observations in a separate paragraph. This will serve as a concise summary and reflection on your reading.

Annotating a Webpage

Step 1: Download an Online Program

  • Install software or browser extensions like Diigo, Evernote, or Hypothesis to annotate web articles. These programs provide a range of annotation tools and allow you to save or share your notes.

Step 2: Use Annotation Toolbar

  • Highlight text, draw arrows, or make notes using the annotation toolbar. These annotations will be stored in your browser or the software program.

Step 3: Save or Share

  • Save your annotations locally or share them with others for collaboration and discussion.
