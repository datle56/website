Cross-Multiplying Fractions: A Comprehensive Guide

Introduction

Cross-multiplication is a technique used to solve equations involving fractions. It is a simple and effective method that simplifies the process of finding solutions.

Step-by-Step Guide

For Equations with a Single Variable:

  1. Multiply the numerator of the left fraction by the denominator of the right fraction.
    • This means multiplying the top number of the left fraction by the bottom number of the right fraction.
  2. Multiply the numerator of the right fraction by the denominator of the left fraction.
    • This means multiplying the top number of the right fraction by the bottom number of the left fraction.
  3. Set the two products equal to each other.
    • The products obtained in steps 1 and 2 should be set equal to each other.
  4. Solve for the unknown variable.
    • Use algebraic techniques to isolate the unknown variable and solve for its value.

For Equations with Variables in Both Fractions:

  1. Multiply the numerator of the left fraction by the denominator of the right fraction.
    • As in the previous case, multiply the top number of the left fraction by the bottom number of the right fraction.
  2. Multiply the numerator of the right fraction by the denominator of the left fraction.
    • Similarly, multiply the top number of the right fraction by the bottom number of the left fraction.
  3. Set the two products equal to each other and combine like terms.
    • Set the products obtained in steps 1 and 2 equal to each other and simplify by combining like terms on both sides of the equation.
  4. Solve for the unknown variable.
    • Use algebraic techniques to isolate the unknown variable and solve for its value.

Example:

Solve for x:

(2/3)x = 1/6

Solution:

  1. Multiply the numerator of the left fraction by the denominator of the right fraction: (2/3)x * 6 = (2/3) * 1 2x * 6 = 2/3
  2. Multiply the numerator of the right fraction by the denominator of the left fraction: (2/3)x * 1 = (2/3) * 6 2x * 1 = 12/3
  3. Set the two products equal to each other: 2x * 6 = 12/3
  4. Solve for x: 12x = 12 x = 1

Checking the Solution:

Substituting the value of x = 1 back into the original equation:

(2/3)(1) = 1/6 2/3 = 1/6

Since the left-hand side and the right-hand side are equal, the solution is correct.

Conclusion

Cross-multiplication is a valuable technique for solving fractional equations. By understanding the steps involved, you can apply it efficiently to find solutions both for single-variable equations and equations with variables in both fractions. Remember to check your solution by plugging it back into the original equation for confirmation.
