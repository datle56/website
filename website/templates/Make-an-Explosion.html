Method 1: Hydrogen Peroxide Explosion

Materials:

  • Hydrogen peroxide (3%)
  • Dish soap
  • Food coloring (optional)
  • Active dry yeast
  • Warm water
  • Plastic bottle

Instructions:

  1. Pour 1/2 cup of warm water into a plastic bottle.
  2. Add 1 tablespoon of hydrogen peroxide.
  3. Add 1 tablespoon of dish soap.
  4. Add a few drops of food coloring (optional).
  5. Sprinkle 1 teaspoon of active dry yeast into the bottle.
  6. Quickly shake the bottle to combine the ingredients.
  7. Observe the reaction as the yeast and hydrogen peroxide interact, creating a foamy explosion.

Caution: The reaction produces heat, so handle the bottle carefully. The foamy explosion can splatter, so it's best to conduct this experiment outdoors or over a sink.

Method 2: Exploding Ivory Soap

Materials:

  • Ivory soap bar
  • Microwave
  • Paper towels
  • Heat-resistant gloves

Instructions:

  1. Microwave a bar of Ivory soap on high for 80-90 seconds.
  2. Remove the soap from the microwave using heat-resistant gloves.
  3. Place the soap inside a few layers of paper towels.
  4. Carefully hold the soap bundle and observe as it expands.
  5. The soap will quickly expand and explode, releasing hot steam.

Caution: The soap will be very hot after microwaving. Allow it to cool thoroughly before handling it. Do not microwave the soap for longer than indicated, as it can cause fire.

Method 3: Diet Coke and Mentos

Materials:

  • Diet Coke (2-liter bottle)
  • Mentos candies (5-6 pieces)
  • Large tray or open area

Instructions:

  1. Place the Diet Coke bottle in a large tray or open area.
  2. Unwrap the Mentos candies.
  3. Hold the Mentos candies in your hand and quickly drop them into the Diet Coke bottle.
  4. Step back and observe the reaction.
  5. The Mentos will trigger a rapid release of carbon dioxide, creating a large geyser of soda that shoots high into the air.

Caution: The soda geyser can create a mess, so it's best to conduct this experiment outdoors or in a large sink filled with water.

Method 4: Ammonium Dichromate Volcano

Materials:

  • Ammonium dichromate powder
  • Tray of sand
  • Lighter fluid
  • Lighter or match
  • Open area with good ventilation

Instructions:

  1. Create a mound of ammonium dichromate powder in the center of a tray of sand.
  2. Pour a small amount of lighter fluid onto the pile.
  3. Light the pile using a lighter or match.
  4. Step back and observe the reaction.
  5. The ammonium dichromate will ignite and produce a crackling, explosive reaction, erupting like a miniature volcano.

Caution: Ammonium dichromate is a toxic substance. Conduct this experiment in a well-ventilated area, away from flammable materials. Wear gloves and eye protection.

Method 5: Dry Ice Bomb

Materials:

  • Dry ice
  • Plastic water bottle
  • Warm water
  • Safe, open area

Instructions:

  1. Fill a plastic water bottle with warm water.
  2. Break a chunk of dry ice into small pieces.
  3. Place the dry ice pieces inside the water bottle.
  4. Quickly cap the bottle tightly and place it in a safe, open area.
  5. The dry ice will react with the water, producing a loud boom.

Caution: Dry ice can burn your skin. Handle it with gloves. Do not seal the water bottle too tightly, as the pressure buildup could cause it to explode.

Method 6: Liquid Nitrogen Explosion

Materials:

  • Garbage can
  • Warm water
  • Liquid nitrogen
  • Water bottle
  • Packing peanuts or ping pong balls
  • Heat-resistant gloves
  • Safety glasses

Instructions:

  1. Fill a garbage can with warm water.
  2. Pour liquid nitrogen into a water bottle.
  3. Quickly place the bottle in the warm water.
  4. Add packing peanuts or ping pong balls to the water.
  5. The sudden temperature change will cause the liquid nitrogen to expand rapidly, creating a large explosion.

Caution: Liquid nitrogen can cause severe burns. Wear heat-resistant gloves and safety glasses when handling it. Do not touch the bottle after the explosion, as it will be extremely cold.
