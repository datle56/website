Growing Apple Trees from Seeds: A Step-by-Step Guide

Method 1: Extracting and Preparing Seeds

Step 1: Collecting Apple Seeds

  • Choose ripe, healthy apples from trees that bear edible fruit.
  • Cut the apples open and scoop out the seeds.
  • Remove any remaining pulp from the seeds.

Step 2: Drying the Seeds

  • Spread the seeds on a paper towel and allow them to dry for 3-4 weeks.
  • Flip the seeds regularly to prevent molding.

Step 3: Moist Chilling (Stratification)

  • Mix the dry seeds with equal parts peat moss and water.
  • Place the mixture in a plastic bag and seal it.
  • Store the bag in the refrigerator for three months.
  • This process helps soften the seed coat, improving germination rates.

Method 2: Planting the Seeds Outside

Step 1: Preparing the Soil

  • Choose a garden bed with well-drained soil in full sun or partial shade.
  • Remove any weeds, rocks, or clumps from the soil.
  • Spread a layer of compost over the soil and mix it in.

Step 2: Creating Furrows

  • Create shallow furrows in the soil, spacing them approximately 12 inches apart.
  • Make sure the furrows are deep enough to accommodate the seeds and cover them with soil.

Step 3: Planting the Seeds

  • Take the seeds out of the refrigerator and soak them in warm water for 24 hours.
  • Place the sprouted seeds in the furrows, 12 inches apart.
  • Cover the seeds with a thin layer of soil and sand.

Method 3: Potting the Seeds Indoors

Step 1: Preparing the Pots

  • Fill small pots with potting soil.
  • Poke two small holes in each pot, big enough to accommodate two seeds.

Step 2: Planting the Seeds

  • Place two seeds in each hole.
  • Water the soil and cover the seedlings.

Step 3: Germinating and Growing

  • Keep the pots in a warm, sunny location with temperatures between 65-75°F.
  • Water the soil twice a week.
  • When the seedlings have developed their first set of true leaves, transplant them outdoors in the fall, when they are dormant.

Tips:

  • Choose seeds from apples that you know produce edible fruit. Avoid seeds from ornamental or crabapple trees.
  • Plant multiple seeds to increase the chances of successful germination.
  • Apple trees grown from seed have a higher probability of being rootstock compatible with different apple varieties.
  • It typically takes approximately 10 years for an apple tree grown from seed to start producing fruit.
  • Be patient and enjoy the process of growing your own apple trees!
