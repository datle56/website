Room Painting Guide: A Comprehensive Step-by-Step Guide

Part 1: Preparation

Choosing Interior Paint

  • Water-Based Paint: Most widely used for interior applications. Easy to clean up with soap and water. Available in various sheen finishes (flat, eggshell, satin, semi-gloss, gloss).
  • Oil-Based Paint: Durable and resistant to scuffs and stains. Requires solvents for cleanup. Often used in high-traffic areas, such as kitchens and bathrooms.

Calculating Paint Quantity

  • Measure the length and width of the room in feet. Multiply these values to get the area in square feet (sq ft).
  • Divide the room area by 400 sq ft (average coverage of one gallon of paint). This will give you the approximate number of gallons of paint needed.

Preparing the Room

  • Remove all furniture, artwork, and rugs from the room.
  • Cover remaining furnishings and walls with plastic sheeting or drop cloths. Secure with tape.
  • Clean the walls thoroughly with a solution of trisodium phosphate (TSP) and water. Rinse with clean water.
  • Inspect walls for any cracks or holes. Fill with spackle or drywall compound and let dry. Sand smooth.
  • Apply painter's tape around all trim, outlets, casings, and other areas where clean lines are desired.
  • Ensure adequate ventilation by opening windows and doors.

Surface Preparation

  • For glossy walls that have not been painted before, lightly sand with fine-grit sandpaper to create a surface for better paint adhesion.

Part 2: Applying Primer

When to Prime

  • Apply primer to unpainted walls or when changing colors drastically. Primer helps seal and prime surfaces, providing a more even finish.

Applying Primer

  • Use an angled brush to "cut in" around edges, corners, and any intricate areas.
  • Pour primer into a paint tray. Dip the roller into the tray and roll over the excess paint on the angled tray to remove any drips.
  • Apply primer to the wall in smooth, even strokes. Use a horizontal or vertical pattern for each section.
  • Allow primer to dry completely. If necessary, apply a second coat and sand lightly before painting.

Part 3: Painting Walls

Stirring Paint

  • Stir the paint thoroughly with a stir stick to ensure even consistency and color.

Cutting In

  • Paint along all edges, corners, and around obstacles with an angled brush. This step gives a precise finish.

Rolling Walls

  • Fill the paint tray. Dip the roller into the tray and roll over the excess paint on the angled tray.
  • Apply paint to the wall using a "V" or "M" shape pattern. Overlap each stroke slightly.
  • Apply two coats of paint, allowing each coat to dry completely before applying the next.

Finishing Up

  • Once the paint is dry, carefully remove the painter's tape.
  • Clean up the room by removing drop cloths and cleaning any paint splatters.
  • Allow the room to air out completely before reintroducing furniture and accessories.
