Calculating Displacement

For Distance Units:

  • Formula: S = √(x² + y²)
  • Explanation:
    • Calculate the distance traveled along the horizontal axis (x-axis): dx = x₂ - x₁
    • Calculate the distance traveled along the vertical axis (y-axis): dy = y₂ - y₁
    • Substitute dx and dy into the formula to find the total displacement: S = √(dx² + dy²)

For Velocity and Time:

  • Formula: S = V * T
  • Explanation:
    • Calculate the average velocity: V = (U + V) / 2, where U is the initial velocity and V is the final velocity.
    • Determine the time taken to travel from the initial to the final position: T
    • Substitute V and T into the formula to find the displacement: S = (U + V) / 2 * T

For Initial Velocity, Acceleration, and Time:

  • Formula: S = Ut + 1/2 * A * T²
  • Explanation:
    • Determine the acceleration: A
    • Calculate the time taken to travel from the initial to the final position: T
    • Substitute U (initial velocity), A (acceleration), and T into the formula to find the displacement: S = Ut + 1/2 * A * T²

Angular Displacement:

  • Formula: θ = S / R
  • Explanation:
    • Calculate the linear displacement (distance traveled along a curved path): S
    • Determine the radius from the center of the arc to the point where the displacement occurred: R
    • Substitute S and R into the formula to find the angular displacement: θ = S / R, which is measured in radians.

Understanding Displacement:

  • Displacement vs. Distance: Displacement is different from distance, which measures the total length traveled regardless of the direction.
  • Magnitude and Direction: Displacement is a vector quantity, meaning it has both a magnitude (length) and a direction (angle).
  • Negative Displacement: Displacement can be negative if the final location is in the opposite direction from the initial location.
  • Equal Distance and Displacement: In some cases, such as when traveling in a straight line, the distance traveled and the displacement are equal.
