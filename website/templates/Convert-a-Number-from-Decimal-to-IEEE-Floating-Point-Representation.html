Converting Numbers to IEEE 754 Format: A Comprehensive Guide

Computers, unlike humans, represent numbers in a binary format, utilizing a system restricted to two values: 0 and 1. The IEEE 754 standard establishes a framework for this binary representation of numbers.

Step-by-Step Conversion Process

1. Separating the Whole and Decimal Parts

Begin by dividing the number into its integral component, known as the whole number, and its fractional component, represented by the decimal. For instance, if we have the number 85.125, the whole number is 85 and the decimal portion is 0.125.

2. Converting to Binary

The next step involves converting both the whole number (85) and the decimal (0.125) to their binary equivalents. In our example, 85 is represented as 1010101 in binary, while 0.125 translates to 0.001 in binary.

3. Combining the Binary Parts

Once we have the binary representations, we concatenate the whole number and the decimal, separated by a decimal point. In our case, we obtain 1010101.001.

4. Converting to Scientific Notation

The following step entails converting the combined binary number to scientific notation. This involves shifting the decimal point to the right until it resides after the initial digit, creating an exponent in base 2. In our example, this yields 01.010101001 * 2^-6.

5. Determining the Sign

If the original number is positive, the sign bit is set to 0; conversely, for negative numbers, the sign bit is set to 1. In our case, since 85.125 is positive, the sign bit is 0.

6. Extracting the Exponent

We utilize the exponent from the scientific notation (133) and convert it to its binary form, which is 10000101 in our example.

7. Identifying the Mantissa

The mantissa is obtained by removing the first digit of the scientific notation result and copying the remaining decimal portion. In our case, this yields 010101001.

8. Assembling the Components

Finally, we combine the sign, exponent, and mantissa in the following order:

  • Sign bit: 0
  • Exponent: 10000101
  • Mantissa: 010101001000000000000000

To meet the precision requirements for single-precision, we add leading zeros to the mantissa, resulting in a total of 32 bits:

0 10000101 01010100100000000000000

This binary representation constitutes the IEEE 754 single-precision format for the number 85.125.
