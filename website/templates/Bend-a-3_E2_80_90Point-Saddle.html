3-Point Saddle Conduit Bend: A Comprehensive Guide

Calculating and Marking Bend Points

Step 1: Measure Obstruction Height

Determine the height of the obstruction that the conduit will need to clear.

Step 2: Add Clearance Allowance

Add 1/8 inch to the obstruction height to account for clearance.

Step 3: Calculate Distance to Previous Conduit Piece

Measure the distance from the obstruction to the end of the previous conduit piece.

Step 4: Determine Shrink Amount

For each inch of obstruction height, calculate the shrink amount as follows: 3/16 inch

Step 5: Calculate Center Bend Point

Add the shrink amount to the distance to the previous conduit piece: Distance to Previous Conduit Piece + Shrink Amount = Center Bend Point

Step 6: Mark Center Line

Mark the center line on the conduit at the calculated center bend point.

Step 7: Calculate Outer Bend Mark Distance

Multiply the obstruction height by 2.5 to determine the distance between the center line and the outer bend points: Obstruction Height x 2.5 = Outer Bend Mark Distance

Step 8: Mark Outer Bend Points

Mark the two outer bend points on the conduit, equidistant from the center line and at the calculated outer bend mark distance.

Making the Bends

Step 1: Position Conduit in Bender

Align the conduit in the conduit bender with the center line mark positioned at the center of the bender.

Step 2: Create Center Bend

Push down on the end of the conduit to bend it at a 45-degree angle, creating the center bend.

Step 3: Rotate Conduit

Slide the conduit down the bender and rotate it 180 degrees.

Step 4: Align First Outer Bend Mark

Align the first outer bend mark with the arrow on the bender head.

Step 5: Bend to 22.5 Degrees

Bend the conduit at a 22.5-degree angle, using the bender's scale as a guide.

Step 6: Lift and Rotate Conduit

Lift the conduit out of the bender, rotate it, and align the other outer bend mark with the arrow.

Step 7: Bend to Final 22.5 Degrees

Bend the conduit at the final 22.5-degree angle, completing the saddle bend.

Additional Notes

  • Conduit bender manufacturers often provide reference tables that specify the necessary measurements for different bend radii and angles, eliminating manual calculations.
  • A 30-60-30 saddle bend has a center bend angle of 60 degrees and outside bend angles of 30 degrees.
