Part 1: Graphing a Parabola

What is a Parabola?

A parabola is a U-shaped curve that arises from the graphical representation of a quadratic equation. It consists of several key features:

  • Focus: A fixed point within the parabola's interior.
  • Directrix: A line perpendicular to the axis of symmetry, located equidistant from the focus.
  • Axis of Symmetry: A vertical line that bisects the parabola into two symmetrical halves.
  • Vertex: The point where the parabola changes direction, representing either its minimum or maximum value.

Equation of a Parabola

The standard equation for a parabola is:

y = ax² + bx + c

where:

  • a determines the parabola's shape and direction:
    • If a > 0, the parabola opens upward.
    • If a < 0, the parabola opens downward.
  • b controls the horizontal shift of the parabola.
  • c represents the vertical shift of the parabola.

Steps to Graph a Parabola

  1. Find the Axis of Symmetry:

Determine the axis of symmetry using the formula:

x = -b/2a

  1. Find the Vertex:

The vertex is the point where the axis of symmetry intersects the parabola. Its coordinates are:

(x, y) = (-b/2a, a(-b/2a)² + c)

  1. Create a Table of Values:

Construct a table of x and y values, including the vertex. This will provide sufficient points to plot the parabola accurately.

  1. Plot and Connect:

Plot the points from the table on a graph and connect them to form the parabola.

Part 2: Shifting the Graph of a Parabola

Shifting the graph of a parabola involves modifying the constant terms without re-plotting points. The following transformations apply:

Vertical Shifts:

  • Shift Up: Add a constant to y, e.g., y = x² + 1. This moves the parabola vertically upward.
  • Shift Down: Subtract a constant from y, e.g., y = x² - 1. This moves the parabola vertically downward.

Horizontal Shifts:

  • Shift Left: Shift the entire expression inside the parentheses to the right, e.g., y = (x + 1)². This moves the parabola leftward.
  • Shift Right: Shift the entire expression inside the parentheses to the left, e.g., y = (x - 1)². This moves the parabola rightward.
