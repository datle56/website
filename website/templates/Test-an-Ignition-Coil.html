Ignition Coil Spark Test

If your car is experiencing ignition problems, the ignition coil may be to blame. Follow these steps to perform a spark test:

Materials: - Spark plug socket - Ohmmeter (multimeter)

Procedure:

  1. Safety First: Ensure the engine is turned off and the parking brake is engaged.

  2. Locate the Ignition Coil: Typically found near the fender or distributor, the ignition coil is a small, cylindrical device connected to spark plug wires.

  3. Disconnect a Spark Plug Wire: Using a spark plug socket, gently disconnect one of the spark plug wires from its corresponding spark plug.

  4. Remove the Spark Plug: Using the same spark plug socket, carefully unscrew the spark plug from the engine.

  5. Reconnect the Spark Plug: Reconnect the spark plug to the spark plug wire you disconnected earlier. Do not tighten it yet.

  6. Ground the Spark Plug: Holding the spark plug by its insulated boot, ensure its metal threads make firm contact with a metal surface on the engine.

  7. Disable Fuel Pump: Locate the fuel pump relay, which is usually found in the engine bay's fuse box. Remove the relay to prevent the engine from starting while performing the test.

  8. Start and Observe: Ask an assistant to attempt starting the car while you observe the gap between the spark plug and the engine.

  9. Observe for Sparks: If the ignition coil is functioning correctly, you should witness a series of bright blue sparks jumping across the gap. Absence of sparks indicates a potential issue with the ignition coil or related components.

Ignition Coil Resistance Test

An alternative method to diagnose ignition coil issues is a resistance test using an ohmmeter.

Procedure:

  1. Remove the Ignition Coil: Locate the ignition coil as described earlier and disconnect it from the engine.

  2. Check Resistance Specifications: Consult your vehicle's repair manual or online resources to obtain the resistance specifications for your specific ignition coil model.

  3. Measure Resistance: Using an ohmmeter set to the proper resistance scale, measure the resistance between the two poles of the ignition coil.

  4. Compare Readings: Compare the measured resistance values to the specified values. A significant deviation from the specifications may indicate a faulty ignition coil.

  5. Replace if Necessary: If the ignition coil's resistance is significantly outside the specified range, it requires replacement to restore proper engine ignition.


Conclusion: Simple and Effective Car Troubleshooting

With these straightforward tests, you can easily determine if your ignition coil is the culprit behind your car's issues. Both the spark test and resistance test provide clear indicators of coil functionality. If the tests reveal a faulty coil, replacing it is a simple and relatively inexpensive solution to restore your vehicle's ignition system to peak performance.
