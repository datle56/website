Unlocking Audio Files: Three Simple Steps to Convert Protected Audio to MP3


Unlock the Freedom of Your Music: Convert Protected Purchases and Enjoy Unlimited Playback

Tired of being restricted by protected iTunes and Windows Media Player music? Say goodbye to DRM limitations and embrace the versatility of MP3s! With these comprehensive methods, you'll learn how to effortlessly convert your purchased music, unlocking a world of freedom and endless playback possibilities. Whether you want to download deleted songs, burn protected music to CDs, or seamlessly import it into other devices, these foolproof techniques will empower you to take full control of your music library.


Method 1: Converting iTunes Purchases

Step 1: Get iTunes Match

iTunes Match is a subscription service that allows you to store your entire music library in iCloud and access it from any Apple device. It also lets you re-download deleted songs, including those that were previously purchased.

Step 2: Convert Protected Content

  • Open iTunes and log in to your Apple ID.
  • Select the protected song or album you want to convert.
  • Click the "Delete from Library" button.
  • Go to the "Purchased" section in the iTunes Store.
  • Find the song or album you deleted and click the cloud icon to download the unprotected version.

Step 3: Convert to MP3

Once the unprotected version of the song is downloaded, you can convert it to MP3 if desired.

  • Select the song in iTunes.
  • Go to "File" > "Convert" > "Create MP3 Version."
  • Choose a destination folder and click "OK" to complete the conversion.

Method 2: Converting Protected Songs in iTunes (Limited)

Limitations:

  • This method only works for certain types of protected music (FairPlay protected AAC files).
  • You will need a blank CD-RW and a CD burner.

Steps:

  • Burn the protected song or album to a blank CD-RW using iTunes.
  • Insert the CD into your computer and rip the tracks back into iTunes as MP3 files.
  • Once the songs have been ripped, delete the protected version from your iTunes library.

Method 3: Converting Windows Media Player Purchases

Limitations:

  • This method requires a CD burner and a blank CD-RW.
  • It works for songs purchased from the Windows Media Store.

Steps:

  • Burn the protected song or album to a blank CD-RW using Windows Media Player.
  • Insert the CD into your computer and rip the tracks back into Windows Media Player as MP3 files.
  • Once the songs have been ripped, delete the protected version from your Windows Media Player library.

Conclusion:

Converting protected music files can be a hassle, but with the right methods at your disposal, you can easily bypass these restrictions and enjoy your music on any device you desire. By following the steps outlined in this guide, you can unlock your protected iTunes and Windows Media Player purchases and convert them into unprotected MP3 files. This will empower you to listen to your music without worrying about compatibility issues or DRM limitations, ensuring that your digital music collection is accessible anytime, anywhere.
