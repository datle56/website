Making Yogurt: A Comprehensive Step-by-Step Guide

Ingredients:

  • 1 quart milk (any kind): Whole, 2%, skim, or plant-based milk like almond, soy, or coconut milk can be used.
  • 1/4 to 1/2 cup non-fat dry milk (optional): This adds additional protein and calcium to the yogurt.
  • 1 tablespoon white sugar: Sugar provides food for the beneficial bacteria.
  • Pinch of salt (optional): A pinch of salt enhances the flavor of the yogurt.
  • 2 tablespoons existing yogurt with live cultures: This serves as the starter culture and contains the beneficial bacteria that will ferment the milk. It's important to use yogurt with live and active cultures.

Instructions:

Mixing the Milk and Starter:

  1. Heat the milk: Pour the milk into a large pot or saucepan and heat it over medium heat. Use a thermometer to monitor the temperature.
  2. Cool the milk: Once the milk reaches 185°F (85°C), remove it from the heat and allow it to cool to 110°F (43°C). This is the ideal temperature for the bacteria to thrive.
  3. Warm the starter yogurt: While the milk is cooling, place the starter yogurt in a small bowl and warm it slightly in the microwave or by setting it in warm water.
  4. Optional: Add nonfat dry milk: If desired, whisk the nonfat dry milk into the warm milk.
  5. Add the starter to the milk: Once the milk has cooled to 110°F (43°C), add the warmed starter yogurt and stir well to combine.

Incubating the Bacteria:

  1. Pour the mixture into containers: Divide the milk mixture into clean glass jars or small containers. Leave 1/2 to 1 inch of headspace at the top of each container.
  2. Incubate the mixture: Place the containers in a yogurt maker, an insulated cooler filled with warm water, or a warm oven (turned off) with the light on.
  3. Choose an incubation method: The ideal temperature for incubation is 100°F (38°C). A yogurt maker maintains this temperature automatically. For other methods, you may need to adjust the temperature occasionally.
  4. Maintain a temperature of 100°F during incubation: Check the temperature of the containers periodically and adjust the incubation environment as needed.
  5. Incubation time: Incubate the mixture for 7 hours or more, depending on the desired consistency. Thicker yogurt will require longer incubation.

Adding the Finishing Touches:

  1. Optional: Strain the yogurt: For a thicker, Greek-style yogurt, line a fine-mesh strainer with cheesecloth and pour the yogurt into it. Allow it to drain for several hours or overnight in the refrigerator.
  2. Refrigerate the yogurt: After incubation, refrigerate the yogurt for several hours or overnight. This helps develop its texture and flavor.
  3. Add optional flavorings: Before serving, you can stir in fruit, honey, granola, or other flavorings as desired.
  4. Reuse the starter: You can use a portion of the yogurt from this batch as a starter for future batches. This helps maintain a consistent culture and flavor.
