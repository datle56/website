How to Craft Black Tattoo Ink in the Shadows of Incarceration: A Detailed Guide

In the confines of correctional facilities, where artistic expression may be limited, inmates have ingeniously devised methods to create their own tattoo ink. One such method, steeped in secrecy and passed down through generations of incarcerated artists, involves utilizing soot and baby oil to produce a potent black ink.

Materials:

  • Small, airtight container (e.g., boot polish can, half of an aluminum can)
  • Cotton balls or absorbent paper
  • Baby oil

Step 1: Constructing the "Soot Collector"

  1. Locate a flat piece of metal, such as a discarded tray or sheet, and place it over the opening of your chosen container.
  2. Ensure that there is a small gap between the metal and the container's rim to allow air to circulate.
  3. This metal sheet will act as a soot collector, capturing the fine black powder that will become the cornerstone of your ink.

Step 2: Generating the Powder

  1. Fill the container with cotton balls or sheets of absorbent paper and saturate them with baby oil.
  2. Using a lighter or match, ignite the soaked cotton/paper.
  3. Carefully hold the soot collector above the burning flame, creating a receptacle for the black soot that will be released as the oil combusts.
  4. As the cotton/paper continues to burn, the soot will accumulate on the metal sheet, forming a fine, powdery substance.

Step 3: Preparing the Ink

  1. Once a sufficient amount of soot has been collected, carefully scrape it off the metal sheet and transfer it to a clean container.
  2. Add a single drop of clean water to the soot and mix thoroughly until it transforms into a thick, pen-like consistency.
  3. Continue adding water or soot as necessary to achieve the desired viscosity and opacity.

Additional Considerations:

  • The quality of the ink will be influenced by the purity of the soot. To obtain the darkest and most vibrant ink, ensure that the flame burns cleanly and avoid introducing any foreign materials into the soot collection process.
  • Sterilization is paramount when dealing with tattooing equipment in a prison environment. Before using the ink, sterilize the container and any instruments used to apply it to prevent infections.
  • It is important to note that this method of ink production should only be used in extreme circumstances where access to commercial tattoo ink is unavailable. Always prioritize safety and consult with medical professionals before engaging in any tattooing practices within correctional facilities.

Conclusion:

Making black tattoo ink in prison, while not recommended due to potential health risks, is a testament to the ingenuity and resourcefulness of those incarcerated. It's crucial to emphasize that this method is only intended for temporary tattoos and should not be used for permanent ones. Seeking professional tattoo artists for lasting body art remains the safest and most responsible approach.
