Buying and Preparing a Raw Brisket

A brisket is a large cut of beef from the lower chest of a cow. It has two main parts: the fatty "point" and the leaner "flat." When purchasing a brisket, it's important to choose one with a moist, deep red color. Aim for 3-4 ounces of meat per person.

Before preparing the brisket, begin by trimming the excess fat. Remove the thick fat cap on the point side of the brisket. This will allow seasonings to penetrate the meat better. Next, trim any small pieces of fat from the underside of the flat cut. These can act as flavor barriers during cooking.

If working with a whole brisket, you'll need to trim the fat vein separating the point and flat cuts. This will make it easier to slice the brisket after cooking.

Finding the Grain of the Cooked Brisket

To ensure a tender cut, it's crucial to identify the grain of the brisket and cut against it. The grain refers to the direction of the muscle fibers. By slicing against the grain, you break down the fibers and make the meat easier to chew.

To find the grain, examine the surface of the cooked brisket. You will see lines or streaks that indicate the direction of the muscle fibers.

  • For a flat cut: Locate the grain and cut in a direction perpendicular to it.
  • For a whole brisket: Cut against the grain along the flat cut, then separate the point cut. Alternatively, you can cut at a 45-degree angle against the grain along both cuts.

Slicing the Cooked Brisket

After cooking, let the brisket rest for 20 minutes to 24 hours before slicing. This will allow the juices to redistribute throughout the meat, resulting in more flavorful and juicy slices.

To slice the brisket, use a long serrated knife. Hold the knife parallel to the cutting board and at a slight angle against the grain. Use short strokes and work your way from top to bottom. Aim for slices that are about half the width of a pinkie fingernail.
