Part 1: Mastering the Kickflip

1. Foot Position:

  • Front Foot: Position your front foot behind the board's bolts, slightly angled forward towards the nose.
  • Back Foot: Rest your back foot on the tail, near the edge, with your toes hanging off.

2. Ollie:

  • Bend your front knee and use your back foot to pop the tail of the board towards the ground.
  • Simultaneously, jump as high as you can, extending your legs fully.

3. Kick the Board:

  • As you reach the peak of your jump, slide your front foot forward towards the board's nose.
  • Flick your front foot upwards with a quick and forceful motion using your baby toe.

4. Catch and Land:

  • Catch the board with your back foot as it flips in the air.
  • Quickly bring your front foot back onto the board, keeping your shoulders level and balanced.

5. Bend Your Knees:

  • As you land, bend your knees to absorb the impact and maintain control over the board.
  • Keep your body slightly crouched to lower your center of gravity.

6. Practice:

  • Kickflips are a challenging maneuver that requires repetition.
  • Practice consistently and focus on developing proper technique and muscle memory.

Part 2: Kickflip Variations

  • Double Kickflip:

  • Execute two consecutive kickflips in the air, requiring greater momentum and timing.

  • Flick the board faster and harder to achieve the double rotation.

  • Varial Kickflip:

  • Combine a kickflip with a 180-degree board spin.

  • As you kick the board, simultaneously rotate your body and the board 180 degrees in the air.

  • Kickflip Body Varial:

  • Perform a kickflip while spinning your body 180 degrees in the air.

  • Keep your body close to the board and rotate it with your shoulders.

  • Kickflip Indy:

  • After completing a kickflip, grab the board with your hand and hold it vertically.

  • Release the board and catch it with your back foot as it completes the rotation.

  • Kickflip Underflip:

  • Execute a kickflip in the opposite direction of the board's original spin.

  • Flick the board with your heel instead of your toe to achieve the reverse rotation.

Part 3: Preparation

1. Be Ready:

  • Ensure proficiency in ollies, balance, and skateboarding basics.
  • Familiarize yourself with skateboard parts, including the bolts, tail, and nose.

2. Practice Style:

  • Choose to practice kickflips either while moving (rolling) or stationary.
  • Rolling kickflips provide momentum but require more coordination.
  • Stationary kickflips allow for more focused repetition without having to balance on the board.
