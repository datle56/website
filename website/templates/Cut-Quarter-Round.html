Cutting Quarter Round for Joints and Corners

Step 1: Measure and Mark

  • Carefully place the quarter round pieces along the wall or floor where they need to be installed.
  • Use a pencil or marking tool to mark the exact point where the quarter round should be cut to join with another piece or create a corner.
  • Ensure that the marks are precise and align perfectly with the desired joint or corner.

Step 2: Determine the Cut Angle

  • Joint Pieces: For joint pieces, you will need to cut 45-degree angles in the same direction on both pieces. This will create a clean and flush joint when the pieces are assembled.
  • Corner Pieces: For corner pieces, you will need to cut 45-degree angles in opposite directions on each piece. This will form a 90-degree corner when the pieces are joined.
  • Other Angles: If you need to create a corner with an angle other than 90 degrees, simply measure the angle and divide it by two. Cut both corner pieces at this angle in opposite directions.

Step 3: Use a Miter Box and Hand Saw

  • Secure the quarter round piece into the miter box at the appropriate angle, aligning the marked line with the guide.
  • Use a hand saw to carefully make the cut at the marked angle, ensuring that the blade remains perpendicular to the quarter round.
  • Repeat this process for all joint and corner pieces.

Cutting Quarter Round Returns

Step 1: Mark the End

  • Place the quarter round against the wall and mark the point where it meets an obstruction, such as a doorway or window casing.

Step 2: Make a 45-Degree Miter Cut

  • Secure the quarter round into the miter box and cut a 45-degree angle away from the obstruction. This cut should extend past the marked line to allow for the end cap.

Step 3: Create an End Cap

  • Cut another small piece of quarter round at a 45-degree angle in the opposite direction.
  • Make a straight cut about 0.25 inches away from the angle cut to create an end cap.

Step 4: Assemble the Pieces

  • Align the longer quarter round against the wall and position the end cap against the cut piece, with the finished side facing out.
  • Carefully join the pieces together, creating a clean and mitered return around the obstruction.
