Part 1: Choosing Your Pair

Age and Maturity:

Ensure your cockatiels have reached a suitable age for breeding. The optimal age is at least 18 months old. At this stage, they have reached physical and emotional maturity, increasing the likelihood of successful breeding.

Avoiding Inbreeding:

Breeding closely related cockatiels is highly discouraged. Inbreeding can lead to genetic disorders and health issues in the offspring. It's crucial to select a pair that is unrelated to avoid potential problems.

Health Considerations:

Thoroughly assess the health of both cockatiels before attempting to breed. Look for signs of underlying illnesses or infections that could potentially harm the birds or their offspring. Ideal candidates should maintain a healthy weight, neither overweight nor undernourished.

Parental Suitability:

Not all cockatiels possess the natural instincts required to be effective parents. Some individuals may demonstrate apathy or outright rejection of their young. Be prepared for the possibility of needing to hand-raise the chicks if the parents prove unsuitable.

Part 2: Preparing for Breeding

Light Cycle:

Mimicking the natural breeding season is essential. Provide your cockatiels with 10-12 hours of bright light each day. This stimulates hormonal production and encourages breeding behavior.

Balanced Diet:

Offer a diverse and nutritious diet to support the increased demands of breeding. Include a variety of fresh fruits, vegetables, seeds, and pellets. Ensure your cockatiels have access to a cuttlebone for calcium supplementation.

Spacious Cage:

Provide a cage that is large enough to accommodate both cockatiels comfortably. A minimum size of 6x3x3 feet is recommended. Ample space allows the birds to move freely, socialize, and establish territories.

Nest Box:

Install a suitable nest box made of wood. Choose one that is approximately one foot by one foot in size with a rear access door for easy inspection. Line the box with soft, low-dust pine shavings or paper materials to provide a cozy and comfortable environment for the eggs and chicks.

Part 3: Breeding and Care

Initiating Breeding:

The male cockatiel will typically take the lead in preparing the nest and attracting the female. He may perform courtship displays, such as bowing and feeding the female.

Egg-Laying and Incubation:

The female cockatiel typically lays one egg every 48 hours until a clutch of 2-8 eggs is complete. Both parents will take turns brooding the eggs, with the female assuming the majority of the responsibility. Incubation takes approximately 21 days.

Hatching and Chick Care:

Once the chicks hatch, avoid disturbing the nest as much as possible. Allow the parents to provide care and nourishment. Chicks begin to feather within 2 weeks and are fully feathered by 6 weeks.

Weaning:

Weaning, the process of transition from parental feeding to independent eating, typically begins at 8-10 weeks. Provide a separate dish of soft foods to encourage consumption and gradually reduce hand-feeding.

Preventing Further Breeding:

To prevent unwanted or excessive breeding, gradually reduce the light cycle to 8 hours per day. Remove the nest box and eliminate soft foods from the diet. These measures will discourage hormonal activity and breeding behavior.
