Home Treatment for Bleeding Hemorrhoids

Hemorrhoids are swollen and inflamed veins in the rectum and anus. They can be painful, itchy, and may bleed. Home treatments can provide relief from symptoms and help reduce bleeding.

  • Warm Baths:

    • Soak in warm water for 15-20 minutes, several times a day.
    • Add sea salt or witch hazel to the water for additional healing benefits.
    • Warm baths help soothe pain, reduce irritation, and promote blood flow to the affected area.

  • Ice Packs:

    • Apply a cold pack to the affected area for short periods (10-15 minutes).
    • Ice packs help reduce swelling and stop bleeding.
    • Do not apply ice directly to the skin; wrap it in a thin towel or washcloth.

  • Topical Creams:

    • Use over-the-counter creams containing phenylephrine to narrow blood vessels and reduce bleeding.
    • Soothing creams, such as hydrocortisone and aloe, can ease pain and irritation.

Medical Treatment for Hemorrhoids

In some cases, home treatment may not be sufficient to relieve symptoms or stop bleeding. Medical treatment options include:

  • Hemorrhoidectomy: Surgical removal of external or internal hemorrhoids.
  • Rubber Band Ligation: Internal hemorrhoids are tied off with a special band, cutting off blood supply and causing them to shrink and fall off.
  • Sclerotherapy: Chemicals are injected into internal hemorrhoids to shrink them.
  • Laser or Radio Treatment: Hemorrhoids are coagulated using lasers or radio frequencies.
  • Cryotherapy: Internal hemorrhoids are frozen to destroy the tissue, causing them to fall off.
  • Stapling: Prolapsed internal hemorrhoids are stapled back into the anal canal, cutting off blood supply.

Understanding Hemorrhoids

Hemorrhoids are caused by increased pressure on the rectal and anal veins. This pressure can result from:

  • Straining during bowel movements
  • Constipation
  • Prolonged sitting on the toilet
  • Pregnancy
  • Being overweight

Hemorrhoids can be internal (inside the rectum) or external (around the anus). Common symptoms include:

  • Painless bleeding
  • Itching or discomfort
  • Swelling or a lump near the anus
  • Pain, especially during bowel movements

When to See a Doctor

It is important to see a doctor if:

  • Bleeding persists after home treatment.
  • You notice dark red or tarry stools, which may indicate internal bleeding.
  • Pain or symptoms worsen after a week of home treatment.
  • You have other underlying medical conditions, such as inflammatory bowel disease or colon cancer.

Conclusion:
