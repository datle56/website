Part 1: Understanding the Cards

Monster Cards:

  • Summoned from your hand to the field.
  • Represent creatures, characters, and mythical beings.
  • Possess attack and defense points that determine their offensive and defensive capabilities.
  • Some monsters have special abilities or effects that enhance their gameplay.
  • Monster types include dragons, warriors, zombies, spellscasters, and many more.

Spell Cards:

  • Greenish cards that trigger specific effects when activated.
  • Can manipulate the battlefield, provide support to your monsters, or hinder your opponent's strategy.
  • Types of Spell cards include:
    • NORMAL SPELLS: Used immediately and discarded after activation.
    • CONTINUOUS SPELLS: Remain on the field, providing ongoing effects.
    • EQUIP SPELLS: Equip monsters with enhancements or boosts.
    • FIELD SPELLS: Affect the entire field, altering rules or granting benefits.

Trap Cards:

  • Purple cards that are set face-down on the field and activated during your opponent's turn.
  • Aim to counter or disrupt your opponent's plays.
  • Types of Trap cards include:
    • NORMAL TRAPS: Once activated, they resolve and go to the Graveyard.
    • CONTINUOUS TRAPS: Remain on the field, providing ongoing effects.
    • COUNTER TRAPS: Chain to other cards and negate their effects.

Part 2: Understanding the Game Mechanics

Setting Spells and Traps:

  • Set Spell or Trap cards face-down on the field to conceal their effects.
  • They can only be activated during your turn or your opponent's turn (depending on the card's type).

Position Yourself for Battle:

  • Position your monsters either in Attack Position, in which they can engage in combat with your opponent's monsters, or in Defense Position, which reduces their attack power but increases their defense.

Link Cards:

  • Specialized monsters that require link materials (other monsters you control) to be summoned.
  • Link monsters have arrows pointing to different zones on the field.
  • By linking monsters together, you can create a chain that gives you access to the Extra Monster Zone (an additional summoning zone reserved for monsters summoned from the Extra Deck).
  • Link monsters often have powerful effects that boost your strategy or disrupt your opponent's plays.

Spell Speed:

  • Cards have different spell speeds, which determine when they can be chained to other cards.
  • Normal Spells and Normal Traps have the slowest spell speed and can only be chained during the turn they are activated.
  • Quick-Play Spells and Counter Traps have the highest spell speed and can be chained to other cards as the second or later effect in a chain, even if it's not your turn.

Part 3: Dueling

Building a Deck:

  • Construct a deck of 40-60 cards, balancing monsters, spells, and traps.
  • Consider the overall strategy and playstyle you want to adopt.
  • Experiment with different card combinations to find a deck that suits your preferences.

Starting a Duel:

  • Shuffle each other's decks.
  • Decide who goes first through a coin toss or dice roll.
  • Draw 5 cards from your deck to start.

Drawing a Card:

  • Draw a card from your deck at the beginning of each turn.
  • If you have no cards left in your deck, you lose the duel.

Main Phase:

  • Summon monsters, activate Spell or Trap cards, and change the battle positions of your monsters.
  • You can repeat these actions until you pass your turn.

Battle Phase:

  • Declare an attack with one of your monsters against a monster or directly against your opponent's life points.
  • Compare the attack points of the battling monsters to determine damage.

End Phase:

  • Certain effects activate during this phase, such as discarding cards if you have more than 6 in your hand.
  • If you have any unused Normal Summons available, you can summon additional monsters.
