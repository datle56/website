How to Craft Authentic Soy Sauce at Home: A Comprehensive Guide

Ingredients:

  • 4 cups dried soybeans, picked over and washed
  • 4 cups whole wheat flour
  • 1 gallon (3.8 liters) of filtered water
  • 31/2 cups of fine sea salt

Equipment:

  • Large, heavy-bottomed pot
  • Colander
  • Food processor or potato masher
  • Shallow tray (3 inches deep, 14x18 inches recommended)
  • 2-gallon glass jar
  • Strainer
  • Cheesecloth or cheesecloth-lined press
  • Thermometer

Instructions:

Step 1: Preparing the Soybean Base

  1. Soak the soybeans: Place the washed soybeans in a large bowl and cover with 5 quarts of cold water. Allow them to soak overnight, or for at least 12 hours.
  2. Boil the soybeans: Drain the soaked soybeans and rinse thoroughly. Transfer them to a large pot and cover with fresh water. Bring to a boil over high heat.
  3. Reduce heat and simmer: Once boiling, reduce heat to low and simmer for 4-5 hours, or until the soybeans are soft and tender. Mash a few beans to check their doneness.
  4. Mash into a paste: Using a food processor or potato masher, puree the cooked soybeans into a smooth paste.
  5. Mix in the flour: Gradually add the whole wheat flour to the soybean paste, mixing thoroughly until a dough forms. The dough should be pliable and slightly sticky but not too dry.

Step 2: Fermenting the Soybean Mixture

  1. Spread the dough in a tray: Line a shallow tray (3 inches deep) with parchment paper or plastic wrap. Spread the soybean-flour dough evenly over the surface, creating a layer that is approximately 2 inches thick.
  2. Create furrows: Use a fork or other tool to make shallow furrows in the dough, creating a grid-like pattern. This will help increase the surface area for optimal fermentation.
  3. Apply koji starter (optional): If desired, sprinkle koji spores over the surface of the dough. Koji is a beneficial mold that aids in the fermentation process.
  4. Rest for fermentation: Place the tray in a warm, humid place (ideally between 75-80°F/24-27°C). Cover it with a damp cloth or lid and allow it to rest for 2-3 days, or until the surface is covered with a white or gray mold.

Step 3: Creating the Moromi and Fermenting the Sauce

  1. Dissolve the salt: In a large bowl or container, dissolve the salt in the 1 gallon of filtered water.
  2. Combine the koji mixture: Break up the fermented soybean mixture and add it to the salt water solution. Stir thoroughly to combine, forming a thick paste known as "moromi."
  3. Monitor and stir regularly: During the first week of fermentation, stir the moromi once daily to prevent mold growth and ensure even fermentation.
  4. Continue fermentation: Continue to store the moromi in a warm, humid place. Stir it once a week for the next 6-12 months, or until it develops a rich, savory flavor. The fermentation time can vary depending on temperature and other factors.

Step 4: Straining and Pasteurizing the Soy Sauce

  1. Strain the mixture: Line a large strainer with cheesecloth or a cheesecloth-lined press. Pour the fermented moromi into the strainer and allow it to drain overnight. Discard the solids.
  2. Heat to pasteurize: Transfer the strained soy sauce to a pot and heat it over medium heat to 175°F (79°C). Maintain this temperature for 20 minutes to pasteurize the sauce, killing any remaining bacteria or mold.
  3. Bottle and store: Once pasteurized, let the soy sauce cool slightly before bottling it in sterilized jars or bottles. Label and refrigerate, where it can be stored for up to 3 years.

Tips:

  • Fermentation time can vary depending on temperature and humidity. If the mixture ferments too quickly, move it to a cooler location.
  • Use a thermometer to monitor the temperature during pasteurization to ensure proper heating.
  • Soy sauce adds a salty flavor to dishes. Use in moderation to avoid over-seasoning.
  • The solids from the straining process can be used as a flavorful seasoning in other dishes, such as stir-fries or marinades.

By following these steps meticulously, you can craft your own authentic soy sauce at home, adding a unique and savory touch to your culinary creations.
