Discover Three Effective Methods to Eliminate Gophers from Your Yard or Garden


Gophers: The Underground Terrorists of Your Lawn and Garden

Imagine a tiny, furry beast that can wreak havoc on your meticulously manicured lawn and precious garden. Enter the gopher, the subterranean saboteur with an uncanny ability to turn your outdoor oasis into a scarred battlefield. Their relentless tunneling and feeding habits leave mounds of soil, destroyed plants, and a trail of disappointment in their wake.

But fear not, brave homeowners! This article will arm you with an arsenal of strategies to deter, eliminate, and outsmart these persistent pests. From non-lethal methods to more drastic measures, we'll guide you through the maze of available options, ensuring that your green spaces are once again a haven of tranquility. Prepare to take back control and say farewell to the reign of terror inflicted by these underground troublemakers.


Understanding and Effectively Combating Gopher Problems

Gophers, known for their distinctive tunneling activities, can wreak havoc on gardens and lawns. Their subterranean networks damage roots, consume plants, and disrupt irrigation systems, leading to significant economic losses and aesthetic concerns. This comprehensive guide explores various strategies, both non-lethal and lethal, to effectively manage and eliminate gopher problems.

Non-Lethal Methods

  • Pet Droppings: Gophers have an aversion to certain scents, including pet feces. Placing pet droppings near tunnel entrances deters them from venturing further.
  • Unpleasant Scents: Strong odors can also act as a repellent. Fish scraps, castor oil, and mothballs emit pungent fumes that gophers find unappealing. These substances can be placed in tunnels or along gopher-prone borders.
  • Gopher Spurge: Euphorbia lathyris, commonly known as gopher spurge, is a plant that exudes a milky sap toxic to gophers. Planting gopher spurge near infested areas creates a natural chemical barrier.
  • Noise: Gophers are sensitive to sound. Introducing noise using portable radios, wind chimes, or vibration devices can disrupt their activities and drive them away.
  • Traps: Humane live traps can be set at tunnel entrances. Disguise human odor by placing rosemary cuttings inside the trap to attract gophers. Release captured gophers at a distance from the property.
  • Sewage-Based Fertilizer: Applying sewage-based fertilizer around vulnerable areas creates a chemical barrier that repels gophers. It effectively deters them from burrowing and feeding in treated areas.

Lethal Methods (Use with Caution)

Warning: Lethal methods should be employed as a last resort and only when non-lethal methods have proven ineffective. Always consult local ordinances and regulations before using any lethal control measures.

  • Gopher Burrow Blasters: These devices release a mixture of propane and oxygen into tunnels, generating carbon monoxide gas that suffocates gophers. They provide a quick and effective means of eliminating gophers but require proper safety precautions.
  • Car Exhaust: Directing a car's exhaust into a tunnel creates carbon monoxide poisoning. This method requires meticulous care to prevent explosions and avoid damage to the vehicle's catalytic converter.
  • Gopher Poisons: Specialized gopher poisons are available, but their use should be restricted to trained professionals. Avoid using anticoagulants, as they can lead to secondary poisoning in other animals.
  • Water Flooding: Filling tunnels with water for extended periods (up to 30 minutes) can drown gophers. However, this method is not always successful, and it may damage underground structures.
  • Predators: Encouraging natural predators, such as cats, owls, or non-venomous snakes, can help reduce gopher populations. Provide nesting boxes and other attractants to draw predators to the area.
  • Professional Services: Exterminators can employ targeted pest control methods like Aluminum Phosphide, a highly effective fumigant. They possess the expertise and equipment to safely and effectively control gopher problems.

Reducing Food Supply

  • Protect Plants: Install gopher baskets or wire mesh barriers around vulnerable plants to prevent burrowing and damage to roots.
  • Limit Garden Size: Maintaining a smaller garden area reduces the availability of food sources for gophers, discouraging their presence.
  • Underground Fencing: Constructing a wire mesh fence that extends below ground level creates a physical barrier that prevents gophers from accessing plants and establishing tunnels.
  • Raised Plant Beds: Building elevated planters with a wire mesh lining ensures that gophers cannot access plants from the ground, effectively protecting root systems.

Additional Tips

  • Monitor infested areas for signs of re-infestation after employing control measures.
  • Consider checking with local authorities or extension services for specific regulations regarding pest control methods in your area.
  • Prioritize non-toxic methods, especially if pets or children are present.
  • Handle poisons with extreme caution and store them securely out of reach of animals.
  • Gopher control can be an ongoing process. Employ a combination of strategies to effectively manage and eliminate these pests from your property.

Conclusion

Gophers can be a nuisance, but with a tailored approach, you can effectively manage them. By employing these strategies, you can protect your garden, lawn, and peace of mind. Remember, non-lethal methods should be prioritized to minimize harm to the environment and non-target species. If all else fails, consider consulting with a professional exterminator for reliable and targeted gopher control solutions. With patience and perseverance, you can successfully eliminate gopher problems and enjoy a thriving outdoor space.
