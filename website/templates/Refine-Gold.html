Part 1: Melting the Gold

  • Gather the gold jewelry or nuggets you wish to refine.
  • Select a specialized crucible specifically designed for melting gold. This crucible should be made of a refractory material that can withstand high temperatures without melting or disintegrating.
  • Utilizing a suitable torch, such as an oxy-acetylene torch or a natural gas torch, heat the crucible until it reaches a temperature sufficient to melt the gold. Typically, this temperature ranges between 1,064 and 1,312 degrees Celsius (1,948 and 2,394 degrees Fahrenheit).

Part 2: Adding the Acid

  • Determine the amount of gold you possess and acquire appropriate containers for the acid solution. Ensure that the containers are spacious enough to accommodate the gold and the acid mixture without overflowing.
  • Measure and add nitric acid to the containers using a ratio of 30 milliliters of nitric acid per ounce of gold. Nitric acid is a strong oxidizing agent that helps dissolve the gold.
  • Add hydrochloric acid to the containers using a ratio of 120 milliliters of hydrochloric acid per ounce of gold. Hydrochloric acid aids in the dissolution process and prevents the formation of gold oxides.
  • Allow the mixture to rest overnight in a well-ventilated area. The acids will react with the gold, gradually dissolving it.

Part 3: Adding Urea and Precipitant

  • Prepare a mixture of urea and water. Urea acts as a reducing agent, converting the dissolved gold into a more manageable form.
  • Add the urea solution to the acid mixture.
  • In a separate container, prepare a solution of a specific precipitant. A common precipitant used in gold refining is sodium metabisulfite.
  • Heat a quantity of water to boiling point and add the precipitant solution to the boiling water.
  • Carefully add the precipitant solution to the acid mixture. The precipitant will react with the dissolved gold, causing it to precipitate out of solution as a solid.

Part 4: Testing for Dissolved Gold

  • Stir the solution thoroughly.
  • Remove a small drop of the solution and place it on a piece of paper.
  • If the drop turns purple, it indicates the presence of dissolved gold in the solution.
  • Continue adding precipitant solution to the mixture until the solution no longer produces a purple reaction on paper.
  • Once all the gold has been precipitated out of the solution, transfer the mixture to a new container.

Part 5: Cleaning the Gold

  • Add a generous amount of water to the remaining gold mixture and stir thoroughly.
  • Allow the mixture to settle, then carefully pour off the water. This process helps remove any residual acids or impurities.
  • Repeat the water rinsing process several times until the solution is completely free of acids.
  • Finally, rinse the gold with ammonia to neutralize any remaining acids and then with distilled water to remove the ammonia.

Part 6: Reconstituting the Gold

  • Transfer the remaining gold mud to a heat-resistant crucible.
  • Apply heat to the crucible using a torch. As the mixture heats, the water will evaporate, leaving behind a solid gold powder.
  • Once all the water has evaporated, continue heating the powder until it melts and forms a molten gold nugget.
  • Remove the gold nugget from the crucible and wrap it in paper towels to absorb any remaining moisture.
  • Soak the wrapped gold nugget in alcohol to remove any remaining impurities.
  • Finally, place the gold nugget back into a crucible and melt it to obtain a pure, refined gold nugget.

Conclusion:

Through this meticulous process, you have successfully liberated the precious gold from its impurities. The final step of reconstituting the gold allows you to transform the powder into pristine, malleable gold.
