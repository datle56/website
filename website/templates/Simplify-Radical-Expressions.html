Simplifying Square Roots

Step 1: Factor the Number Under the Root

Break down the number under the square root into its prime factors. For example, to simplify √36, first factor 36:

36 = 6 x 6

Step 2: Group the Same Factors as Exponents

Identify any factors that appear multiple times and group them using exponents. In our example, since both 6s appear under the root, we can write:

√36 = √(6 x 6)

Step 3: Move Factors Raised to the Power of 2 Outside the Square Root

If any factor is raised to the power of 2, move it outside the square root. In our case, both 6s are squared, so we have:

√36 = 6√1

Step 4: Simplify Any Multiplication or Exponents

Simplify the remaining expression by performing any necessary multiplication or exponent operations. In our example, √1 = 1, so:

√36 = 6

Simplifying Cube Roots and Higher Roots

Step 1: Factor the Number Under the Root

Similar to square roots, factor the number under the cube root or higher root into its prime factors. For example, to simplify ∛27, factor 27:

27 = 3 x 3 x 3

Step 2: Rewrite Groups of the Same Factors as Exponents

Group factors that appear multiple times as exponents. In our example, all three 3s appear under the root, so we write:

∛27 = ∛(3 x 3 x 3)

Step 3: Cancel Out Matching Exponents and Roots

If the exponent of the root (in this case, 3) equals the exponent of any factor, cancel them out. In our case, we have:

∛27 = 3

Step 4: Simplify Any Multiplication or Exponents

Perform any necessary multiplication or exponent operations to simplify the remaining expression.

Simplifying Fractions inside Roots

Step 2: Rewrite the Fraction as Two Radical Expressions or Cancel Out Roots in the Denominator

There are two options to simplify the square root of a fraction:

  • Rewrite the fraction as two radical expressions:

√(4/9) = √(2) / √(3)

  • Cancel out the root in the denominator:

√(4/9) = (2 / 3) / (1 / √3) = 2√3 / 3

Combining Roots of Different Kinds

Step 1: Convert Roots to Fractional Exponents

Convert the roots to fractional exponents using the following rule:

√[n]x = x^(1/n)

For example, to combine √2 and ∛3, convert them to fractional exponents:

√2 = 2^(1/2) ∛3 = 3^(1/3)

Step 2: Combine the Terms Using Exponent Rules

Combine the terms using the following exponent rules:

  • Multiplication: (a^m) * (a^n) = a^(m+n)
  • Division: (a^m) / (a^n) = a^(m-n)
  • Power: (a^m)^n = a^(mn)

Step 3: Convert Back to Radical Form

Convert the expression back to radical form if desired.

Simplifying Radical Expressions with Variables

Step 1: Cancel Out Exponents and Roots as with Integers

Apply the same principles of canceling out exponents and roots as with integers.

Step 2: Give Positive Solutions to Even Roots

For even roots of variables (√x or ∛x), the solutions will be both positive and negative. However, by convention, we generally give positive solutions unless otherwise specified.

Step 3: Use Absolute Value Symbols to Make Variable Solutions Positive

If necessary, use absolute value symbols to ensure that solutions for even roots of variables are positive. For example, we can write:

√x = |x|

Where |x| represents the absolute value of x.
