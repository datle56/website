Building a DIY Foundry for Melting Copper

Embarking on the artisanal journey of casting copper requires a dedicated workspace and specialized equipment. By constructing a DIY foundry, you can delve into the captivating world of metalworking and bring your creative visions to life. Here's a comprehensive guide to assist you in building your own foundry:

Materials:

  • Metal Can or Crockpot: A sturdy metal can or a large crockpot will serve as the body of your furnace.
  • Kaowool Insulation: A high-temperature insulation material used to line the interior walls and floor of the furnace, protecting it from the intense heat.
  • ITC-100 or Satanite Coating: A refractory coating applied over the kaowool to enhance its durability and resistance to thermal shock.
  • Propane Burner: A powerful burner capable of generating sufficient heat to melt copper.
  • Crucible: A high-temperature container, typically made of graphite or ceramic, used to hold the copper during melting.
  • Protective Gear: Essential safety equipment including heat-resistant gloves, a face shield, and lifting tongs.

Steps:

  1. Constructing the Furnace Body:

Begin by selecting a suitable metal can or crockpot as the foundation of your furnace. Ensure it has sufficient volume to accommodate both the crucible and the copper scraps you intend to melt.

  1. Lining the Furnace:

To insulate the furnace and protect it from extreme temperatures, line the interior walls and floor with kaowool insulation. Carefully cut and fit the kaowool to create a snug fit.

  1. Applying a Protective Coating:

Coat the kaowool with a layer of ITC-100 or Satanite. This refractory coating will enhance the insulation's durability, prevent thermal shock, and extend the furnace's lifespan. Allow the coating to dry and cure completely.

  1. Installing the Burner:

Drill a hole in the side of the furnace body large enough to fit the propane burner. Secure the burner firmly in place, ensuring it is properly connected to the propane tank.

  1. Creating a Lid:

Cut the top portion of the furnace body to create a lid. Insulate the lid with kaowool and drill a small vent hole to allow for exhaust gases to escape.

  1. Inserting the Crucible:

Place the crucible, made of a high-temperature material such as graphite or ceramic, inside the furnace. Center the crucible within the furnace and secure it as necessary.

  1. Obtaining Copper Scraps:

To obtain copper for melting, gather scrap copper from various sources such as discarded appliances, wiring, or plumbing pipes. Break or torch-cut the copper into smaller pieces for easy fitting into the crucible.

  1. Preparing the Scraps:

Clean the copper scraps thoroughly to remove any dirt, grease, or impurities that could affect the molten copper's quality. Remove any non-copper materials such as insulation or plastic from the scraps.

  1. Melting the Copper:

Position the crucible in the furnace and ignite the propane burner. Gradually increase the temperature until it reaches approximately 1981 degrees Fahrenheit (1083 degrees Celsius), the melting point of copper. Monitor the temperature using a thermocouple probe.

  1. Pouring the Liquid Copper:

Once the copper has melted, carefully remove the crucible from the furnace using lifting tongs. Wear heat-resistant gloves and a protective face shield for safety. Pour the liquid copper into a pre-prepared mold or cast to create your desired object.


Conclusion:
