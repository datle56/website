Finding the Sides of a Right Triangle:

Step 1: Identify the Right Triangle

A right triangle exhibits a 90-degree angle, often denoted by a small square in the corner. This angle is pivotal to calculating the triangle's side lengths.

Step 2: Label the Sides

Assign labels to the triangle's sides:

  • Non-hypotenuse sides: "a" and "b"
  • Hypotenuse: "c" (the side opposite the right angle)

Step 3: Employ the Pythagorean Theorem

The Pythagorean Theorem, a fundamental concept in trigonometry, provides a crucial relationship between the sides of a right triangle:

a² + b² = c²

Depending on which side(s) are unknown, substitute the known side lengths into this equation.

Step 4: Square the Known Side Lengths

Before isolating the unknown side, square the known side lengths. This step ensures proper arithmetic operations within the equation.

Step 5: Isolate the Unknown Side

Rearrange the equation to isolate the unknown side on one side of the equation:

  • For side "a": a² = c² - b²
  • For side "b": b² = c² - a²

Step 6: Find the Unknown Side's Length

To determine the length of the unknown side, take the square root of both sides of the isolated equation:

  • For side "a": a = √(c² - b²)
  • For side "b": b = √(c² - a²)

Calculating Distance in the X-Y Plane:

Step 1: Plot the Points

Visualize two distinct points, denoted as (x1, y1) and (x2, y2), plotted on a Cartesian coordinate plane.

Step 2: Determine the Horizontal Distance

Calculate the horizontal distance between the two points by subtracting their x-coordinates:

|x1 - x2|

Step 3: Determine the Vertical Distance

Similarly, calculate the vertical distance between the two points by subtracting their y-coordinates:

|y1 - y2|

Step 4: Apply the Pythagorean Theorem

Utilize the Pythagorean Theorem to compute the distance between the two points, designated as "d":

d² = |x1 - x2|² + |y1 - y2|²

Step 5: Find the Distance

To determine the actual distance, take the square root of both sides of the equation:

d = √(d²) = √(|x1 - x2|² + |y1 - y2|²)
