Shanghai Rummy: A Comprehensive Guide

Objective:

Shanghai Rummy is a card game where the goal is to be the player with the lowest score after 10 rounds. Players form melds, which are combinations of cards with the same value (sets) or in sequence (runs), and try to discard all their cards before the other players.

Key Terms:

  • Melds:
    • Sets: Three or more cards of the same value (e.g., three Queens)
    • Runs: Three or more cards in sequence in the same suit (e.g., 3, 4, 5 of Hearts)
  • Laying Off: Adding cards to existing melds on the table (e.g., adding a 4 to a 3-of-a-kind of Queens)
  • Buying: Taking the top card of the discard pile instead of drawing from the deck
  • Going Out: Discarding the last card in your hand

Setup:

  • Use 2-3 decks of cards, depending on the number of players (3-8).
  • Deal 11 cards to each player.
  • Flip the top card of the deck to form the discard pile.

Gameplay:

The game proceeds in a clockwise direction. On each turn, a player takes the following steps:

  1. Draw a card: Draw a card from the top of the deck or the discard pile.
  2. Meld cards: If you have the required melds for the round, play them. A player must have at least one set and one run to play cards. The minimum number of cards in a set or run depends on the round:
    • Round 1: 3 cards
    • Round 2: 4 cards
    • Round 3: 5 cards
    • Round 4: 6 cards
    • Round 5: 7 cards
    • Round 6: 8 cards
    • Round 7: 9 cards
    • Round 8: 10 cards
    • Round 9: 11 cards
    • Round 10: 12 cards
  3. Discard a card: If you did not play cards, discard one card from your hand onto the discard pile.

Laying Off:

Players can also lay off cards onto existing melds on the table. For example, if there is a set of three Queens on the table, a player with a fourth Queen can lay off that card to increase the set to four of a kind.

Buying:

If a player draws a card from the deck, other players can "buy" the top card of the discard pile. To buy, a player must have an existing meld on the table that can incorporate the card from the discard pile. For example, if a player has a set of three Aces and the top card of the discard pile is an Ace, they can buy it and add it to their set.

Going Out:

The first player to discard their last card triggers the end of the round. That player has "gone out." All other players must continue playing until they have also gone out or have no more legal moves.

Scoring:

After each round, players calculate their scores. Each card in a player's hand is worth points:

  • 2s-9s: 5 points
  • 10s-Kings: 10 points
  • Aces: 15 points
  • Jokers: 25 points

The player with the lowest score after 10 rounds wins the game.
