Discover the Step-by-Step Guide with Photographs: Your Essential Guide to Humanely Trapping and Releasing Mice


In the realm of household battles, mice are cunning adversaries that test our patience and hygiene. Their tiny frames and nocturnal antics can drive us to the brink of despair. But fret not, fellow home warriors! With our comprehensive guide to trapping mice, victory shall be yours. We embark on a journey through the treacherous terrain of mouse control, arming you with the knowledge and strategies to conquer these persistent pests.


How to Trap Mice: A Comprehensive Guide

Part 1: Choosing Traps and Bait

1. Selecting Traps:

  • Spring-loaded Traps: These classic traps feature a metal spring that snaps shut when the mouse triggers the mechanism. They are reusable and effective, but can be dangerous if handled incorrectly.
  • Electric Traps: These traps deliver a fatal electric shock to the mouse when it completes the circuit by touching both electrodes. They are highly effective but require caution and should only be used by experienced individuals.
  • Glue Traps: These traps consist of a sticky surface that traps the mouse when it walks on it. They are generally less effective than other types of traps, as mice may struggle to free themselves.

2. Avoiding Poison:

  • Mice can develop resistance to poison, making it ineffective.
  • Poison poses risks to children, pets, and wildlife if it is ingested or contacted.
  • Mice may die in hidden places, creating unpleasant odors and attracting insects.

3. Choosing Bait:

  • Peanut butter: A classic bait that is highly attractive to mice.
  • Fruit: Apples, bananas, and berries can be effective.
  • Seeds: Sunflower, pumpkin, or birdseed are good choices.
  • High-fat foods: Cheese, bacon, or lard can also work well.

Keep the following tips in mind when using bait:

  • Apply a thin layer of bait to the trigger of the trap.
  • Ensure the bait is securely attached to prevent the mouse from taking it without triggering the mechanism.

Part 2: Setting Traps

1. Placement of Traps:

  • Place traps against walls or in corners where mice are likely to travel.
  • Align the trigger of the trap with the wall or corner, ensuring that the mouse must pass through it to reach the bait.
  • Avoid placing traps in open areas where mice may feel exposed and reluctant to approach.

2. Multiple Traps:

  • Set multiple traps every 2-3 feet to increase the chances of catching a mouse.
  • Mice tend to follow paths, so placing traps along these routes will maximize your success.

3. Checking Traps Daily:

  • Check traps daily for caught mice.
  • If a mouse is caught, remove it immediately to prevent odors and bacteria buildup.

4. Disposing of Mice:

  • Wear gloves when handling traps with caught mice.
  • For disposable traps, seal them in a plastic bag and discard them in a dumpster.
  • For reusable traps, scrub them thoroughly with a disinfectant and rinse them well before resetting them.

Part 3: Making a Humane Trap

1. Creating the Tube:

  • Flatten one side of a paper towel tube to form a "bridge" with a flat surface on top.

2. Baiting the Tube:

  • Apply a small amount of bait to the end of the tube where the mouse will enter.

3. Balancing the Tube:

  • Position the baited end of the tube over the edge of a counter or table.
  • Ensure that the other end of the tube is clear and has enough space for the mouse to fall into.

4. Placing a Bucket Underneath:

  • Place a bucket or other container directly underneath the hanging end of the tube.
  • This container will catch the mouse when it triggers the trap.

5. Checking Frequently:

  • Monitor the trap regularly for signs of activity, such as the tube dropping or the bait being eaten.

6. Releasing the Mouse:

  • Transport the captured mouse in the bucket at least a mile away from your home.
  • Release the mouse in a safe area with cover, such as a field or brushy area.

Conclusion:

Trapping mice in your home can be a frustrating but necessary task. By following these simple steps, you can effectively eliminate these pesky rodents while minimizing risks and discomfort. Remember to dispose of mice properly and clean all traps thoroughly to prevent the spread of bacteria. In cases where humane trapping is preferred, the tube trap provides a safe and non-lethal method of catching and releasing mice. With patience and careful execution, you can reclaim your home from these uninvited guests and restore peace and cleanliness to your living space.
