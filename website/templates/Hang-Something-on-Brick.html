Mastering the Art of Hanging on Brick Walls: A Comprehensive Guide

Hanging artwork, shelves, or even lighting fixtures on brick walls can be a daunting task, but it doesn't have to be with the right tools and techniques. In this comprehensive guide, we'll delve into the three main methods for hanging on brick: drilling holes, installing anchors, and using non-drilling options. Get ready to transform your brick walls into a stylish and functional canvas!


Part 1: Drilling Holes

1. Selecting Suitable Anchors:

  • Weight Capacity: Determine the weight of the item you plan to hang and choose anchors with a higher weight rating. For instance, for a 7-pound picture, select anchors rated for 10 pounds or more.
  • Material Selection: Choose anchors designed for the specific type of brick you have. Mortar anchors are suitable for drilling into mortar joints, while brick anchors are ideal for directly drilling into brick.

2. Acquiring Drilling Equipment:

  • Drill: Get a drill with variable speed settings to allow for precise drilling.
  • Drill Bits: Opt for drill bits that are slightly smaller than the width of the screws you intend to use. This ensures a snug fit and prevents the anchors from wobbling or stripping.

3. Marking Drill Points:

  • Spacing: Plan the placement of your holes to distribute the weight evenly and prevent any potential cracking of the brick. Mark the desired locations, ensuring sufficient spacing between them.
  • Avoid Mortar: Aim to drill into the mortar joints whenever possible, as they are generally softer and easier to drill into compared to solid brick.

4. Drilling into Brick or Mortar:

  • Drill Depth: Determine the appropriate drill bit depth based on the length of the anchors you have. The bit should be long enough to penetrate the mortar or brick sufficiently and allow the anchors to be fully inserted.
  • Speed Control: Start drilling at a slow speed to prevent over-drilling or damaging the brick. Gradually increase the speed as you drill deeper.
  • Stabilization: Apply gentle pressure while drilling and ensure the drill is held perpendicular to the wall to avoid creating angled holes.

Part 2: Installing Anchors

1. Attaching Screws:

  • Screw Selection: Choose screws that are compatible with the anchors you have chosen. The length of the screws should be slightly shorter than the depth of the drilled holes.
  • Plate Anchors: Position the anchors over the screw heads and tighten them securely using a screwdriver or wrench.
  • Hook Anchors: Screw the hooks directly into the wall, ensuring they are firmly attached.

2. Tightening and Verification:

  • Manual Tightening: Hand-tighten all screws until they are snug but avoid overtightening.
  • Torque Control: For heavy-duty installations, use a torque wrench to ensure the screws are tightened to the specified torque value provided by the anchor manufacturer.
  • Visual Inspection: Double-check the security of the anchors by gently tugging on them to ensure they are firmly anchored to the wall.

3. Hanging the Item:

  • Hooks: Hang the item directly on the hooks attached to the anchors.
  • Additional Hanging Mechanisms: Use additional materials, such as wire, string, or hanging hardware, to attach the item to the anchors if necessary.

Part 3: Hanging Light Objects without Drilling

1. Pinch Hangers:

  • Shape: Pinch hangers are small, U-shaped devices that fit over the top of bricks.
  • Installation: Place the hanger on the brick and pinch it shut to secure it.
  • Weight Capacity: Check the weight rating of the hangers and ensure they are suitable for the object you wish to hang.

2. Adhesive Hooks:

  • Surface Preparation: Clean the brick surface before applying the hooks.
  • Application: Remove the backing paper from the adhesive strip and firmly press the hook onto the brick.
  • Weight Capacity: Choose adhesive hooks designed for heavy-duty use and make sure they are rated for the weight of the object you intend to hang.

3. Double-Sided Tape:

  • Application: Apply double-sided tape to both the back of the object and the brick surface.
  • Weight Capacity: This method is suitable for hanging lighter items.
  • Surface Considerations: Make sure the brick surface is clean and smooth for optimal adhesion.

Conclusion:

Drilling holes into brick can be a daunting task, but with proper preparation and technique, it's a manageable DIY project. By selecting the right anchors and drilling equipment, ensuring proper spacing and depth, and following the steps for both drilling and installing anchors, you can confidently and securely hang various items on your brick wall. Remember that for lighter objects, there are also alternative methods available without drilling, such as pinch hangers, adhesive hooks, and double-sided tape. With the right knowledge and materials, you'll be able to enhance your brick walls with beautiful décor and art while maintaining their structural integrity.
