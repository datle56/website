Building a Chicken Egg Incubator

Materials

  • Styrofoam cooler
  • Light bulb socket
  • Light bulb (40-60 watts)
  • Chicken mesh
  • Digital thermometer
  • Humidity gauge
  • Bowl of water
  • Sponge
  • Duct tape

Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Make the Incubator Box

  • Cut a hole in the styrofoam cooler lid large enough to fit the light bulb socket.
  • Secure the light bulb socket in the hole and insert a light bulb.
  • Divide the cooler in half using chicken mesh. This will create a protected area for the chicks.

2. Add Monitoring Tools

  • Place the digital thermometer and humidity gauge on one side of the cooler.
  • The thermometer should be positioned at egg level.
  • The humidity gauge will indicate the moisture levels inside the incubator.

3. Create a Humidity Source

  • Fill a bowl with water and place a sponge inside.
  • As the water evaporates, the sponge will absorb moisture, creating a humid environment.
  • The level of water in the bowl can be adjusted to control humidity.

4. Create a Viewing Portal

  • Cut a window in the cooler lid large enough to observe the eggs.
  • Secure the window with duct tape to prevent light from leaking out.

5. Test the Incubator

  • Turn on the light and run the incubator for a full day without eggs.
  • Monitor the temperature and humidity levels to ensure they are stable within the desired ranges.
  • The ideal temperature is between 99.5°F and 100.5°F (37.5°C and 38°C).
  • The optimal humidity level is between 50% and 60%.

Incubating the Eggs

Materials

  • Fertilized chicken eggs
  • Pen or pencil

Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Gather Fertilized Eggs

  • Obtain fertilized eggs from a reputable farm or poultry supplier.
  • Ensure that the eggs are less than 7 days old for best results.

2. Track Time and Data

  • Note the date the eggs are placed in the incubator in a logbook or on a calendar.
  • Record the temperature and humidity readings daily.

3. Rotate the Eggs

  • Eggs should be rotated three to four times a day for the first 18 days of incubation.
  • This prevents the embryo from sticking to the shell and ensures even development.
  • Use a pencil or pen to mark an "X" and an "O" on each egg to keep track of rotation.

4. Candle the Eggs

  • After the first week of incubation, candle the eggs using a bright light.
  • This will allow you to check for infertile eggs or eggs with developing embryos.
  • Remove any infertile or bad eggs from the incubator.

5. Hatching

  • Chicks typically take 21 days to hatch.
  • Expect "pips" or cracks in the eggshells on day 21.
  • Allow the chicks up to 12 hours to fully emerge from the eggs.
  • Once hatched, transfer the chicks to a separate brooder with a heat source and food and water.
