Example: Measuring Spark Plug Gap

Spark plugs play a critical role in the ignition system of a car. Measuring and adjusting the spark plug gap is essential for optimal performance. Here's a step-by-step guide:

1. Find the Specified Gap:

Consult your owner's manual or inquire at an auto parts store to determine the specified gap for your car's spark plugs.

2. Choose a Tool:

There are several types of feeler gauges available for measuring spark plug gaps:

  • Coin-style gauges have a series of coins with different thicknesses to measure the gap.
  • Wire gap gauges have a wire of a specific thickness that you slide between the electrodes.
  • Blade-style gauges have a thin metal blade that you insert between the electrodes and check the gap on a scale.

3. Clean the Spark Plug:

Before measuring the gap, remove any soot or dirt from the spark plug's electrodes to ensure an accurate measurement.

4. Measure the Gap:

Insert the chosen tool between the center electrode and the ground electrode. Gently move the tool until it touches both electrodes and note the thickness or gap size.

Adjusting the Gap

1. Check if it Needs Adjustment:

If the measured gap differs from the specified gap, it needs to be adjusted. A gap that is too wide or too narrow can affect the spark's strength and ignition performance.

2. Bend the Bottom Electrode:

To adjust the gap, use a tool or a flat surface to gently bend the bottom electrode of the spark plug. Bend it slightly until the gap matches the specified value.

3. Re-measure and Adjust:

After bending the electrode, re-measure the gap using the feeler gauge to ensure accuracy. Make small adjustments until the gap is correct.
