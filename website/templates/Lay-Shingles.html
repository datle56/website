Part 1: Laying Shingles

1. Cutting Shingles Appropriately

  • Determine the appropriate overhang for your roof type and local building codes.
  • Use a shingle cutter or utility knife to cut shingles to the desired lengths.
  • Cut shingles in varying sizes for the first five rows:
    • First row: Cut shingles in half, creating half tabs.
    • Second row: Cut shingles to 1/3 of their length.
    • Third row: Cut shingles to 2/3 of their length.
    • Fourth row: Cut shingles to 3/4 of their length.
    • Fifth row: Cut shingles to full length.

2. Laying a Starting Row

  • Position the starting row shingles along the roof's bottom edge, ensuring they overhang correctly.
  • Drive nails through the cutouts provided in the shingles, approximately 1-2 inches from the top edge.
  • Start the first row with a half tab offset, meaning the cut edge of the first shingle should be centered on the second shingle's tab.

3. Laying the First Row

  • Overlap the starting row with full shingles, ensuring the bottom edge of each shingle's tab aligns with the cutouts in the starting row.
  • Drive nails through the tabs of the first row shingles, spacing them evenly and approximately 1-2 inches from the cutouts.

4. Laying Subsequent Rows

  • Offset each subsequent row by half a tab to create a staggered pattern.
  • Ensure that the cutouts in the upper row overlap the full tabs in the lower row.
  • Use chalk lines to guide the placement of even and odd rows, ensuring even spacing and alignment.
  • Drive nails through the tabs of each shingle, following the same spacing and distances as in previous rows.

5. Shingling Around Obstacles

  • Protect openings such as vents and chimneys with metal flashing.
  • Interlace shingles with flashing to create a watertight seal and prevent leaks.
  • Use step flashing around chimneys to ensure proper waterproofing.

6. Interlacing Flashing Around the Chimney

  • Install flashing aprons around the chimney base to create a continuous seal.
  • Overlap the flashing aprons by 4-6 inches to ensure water cannot penetrate.
  • Interlace shingles with the flashing aprons to create a secure connection.

7. Creating a Ridge Cap

  • Cover the roof peak with ridge shingles designed for that purpose.
  • Alternatively, cut regular shingles into three pieces and nail them into place along the ridge.
  • Ensure the ridge cap shingles overlap sufficiently to prevent water penetration.

Part 2: Using Alternate Patterns

1. Straight-up Pattern

  • Lay each shingle directly above the one below, creating a straight vertical line.
  • This pattern provides basic protection from the elements.

2. Half Pattern

  • Start each row 6 inches lower than the previous one, ensuring the butt joints are protected.
  • This pattern offers increased protection against water infiltration.

3. 4-inch Offset

  • Start each row 4 inches lower than the previous one, providing additional protection from the elements.
  • This pattern is commonly used in windy areas.

4. 5-inch Offset

  • Start each row 5 inches lower than the previous one, offering maximum waterproofing.
  • This pattern is suitable for areas prone to heavy rain or snowfall.

5. Racking Shingles

  • Lay the shingles vertically in rows of alternating lengths.
  • Use staggered lengths of shingles to create a decorative effect.
  • While this technique can save time and effort, it may void manufacturer warranties in some cases.
