Brake Fluid Management

How to Check Brake Fluid Level:

  1. Park your car on a level surface and turn off the engine.
  2. Locate the brake fluid reservoir under the hood. It's typically made of a translucent material with a dark-colored cap.
  3. Clean any dirt or debris from the reservoir's lid and cap before opening.
  4. Remove the cap and inspect the fluid level. The fluid should be between the "min" and "max" markings on the reservoir.

How to Add Brake Fluid:

  1. Use only the type of brake fluid specified in your vehicle's owner's manual. Most vehicles require either DOT 3 or DOT 4 brake fluid.
  2. Wipe the reservoir and cap thoroughly with a clean cloth.
  3. Remove the cap and carefully add brake fluid until it reaches the correct level. Avoid overfilling the reservoir.

How to Drain and Replace Brake Fluid:

  1. Refer to your vehicle's owner's manual for specific instructions and safety precautions.
  2. Park your car on a level surface and jack it up using appropriate jack stands.
  3. Remove all four wheels.
  4. Fill the brake fluid reservoir with fresh brake fluid.
  5. Locate the bleed valves on the brake calipers. These valves are usually small, cylindrical components with a small hole in the center.
  6. Connect a clear plastic tube to the bleed valve of the front passenger side brake caliper.
  7. Have an assistant slowly pump the brake pedal several times until fluid begins to flow through the tube.
  8. Tighten the bleed valve securely and add more fluid to the reservoir as needed.
  9. Repeat steps 6-8 for the remaining three brake calipers, following the order specified in your owner's manual.
  10. Reinstall all four wheels and lower the car using the jack stands.
  11. Reinstall the reservoir cap and wipe up any spills.
