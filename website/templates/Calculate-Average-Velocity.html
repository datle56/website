Part 1: Calculating Average Velocity from Displacement and Time

Understanding Velocity

Velocity is a physical quantity that describes the rate at which an object is moving in a specific direction. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude (speed) and direction. Velocity is usually measured in meters per second (m/s) or kilometers per hour (km/h).

Displacement

Displacement is the change in position of an object from its initial location to its final location. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. Displacement is often measured in meters (m) or kilometers (km).

Time

Time is the duration of movement or the interval between two events. It is a scalar quantity, meaning it has only magnitude. Time is usually measured in seconds (s), minutes (min), or hours (hr).

Formula for Average Velocity

Average velocity is the total displacement of an object divided by the total time taken for the displacement. The average velocity is a constant value that represents the uniform motion of the object over the entire time period. The formula for average velocity is:

Average Velocity = Displacement / Time

Example:

An object travels 600 meters north in 300 seconds. What is its average velocity?

Average Velocity = 600 meters north / 300 seconds = 2 meters per second north

Part 2: Calculating Average Velocity from Constant Acceleration

Understanding Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction. Acceleration is usually measured in meters per second squared (m/s²).

Constant Acceleration

When an object experiences constant acceleration, its velocity changes at a constant rate. Constant acceleration means that the object's velocity increases or decreases by the same amount in equal intervals of time.

Formula for Average Velocity

If an object experiences constant acceleration, the average velocity is the arithmetic mean of its initial and final velocities. The formula for average velocity in the case of constant acceleration is:

Average Velocity = (Initial Velocity + Final Velocity) / 2

Example:

An object initially moves at a velocity of 5 meters per second right. It accelerates at a constant rate of 10 meters per second squared for 10 seconds. What is its average velocity?

Average Velocity = (5 m/s right + 15 m/s right) / 2 = 10 meters per second right
