Adding Fractions with Different Denominators

Method 1: Using Improper Fractions

Step 1: Find the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the Denominators

The LCM is the smallest number that is divisible by all the denominators of the given fractions. To find the LCM, list the multiples of each denominator and identify the least number that appears in all the lists.

Step 2: Multiply the Numerator and Denominator by the LCM

To make the denominators equal, multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by a number that makes the denominator equal to the LCM. This process is known as equivalent fractions.

Step 3: Add the Numerators and Leave the Denominators the Same

Once the denominators are equal, add the numerators while leaving the denominators unchanged.

Step 4: Convert to a Mixed Number if Necessary

If the result of the addition is an improper fraction (numerator larger than the denominator), convert it to a mixed number by dividing the numerator by the denominator and writing the remainder as a fraction.

Example:

Add the fractions 1/3 and 2/5:

  1. LCM of 3 and 5 is 15.
  2. 1/3 = 5/15 (multiply by 5/5) 2/5 = 6/15 (multiply by 3/3)
  3. 5/15 + 6/15 = 11/15
  4. 11/15 is an improper fraction, so convert it to a mixed number: 11 ÷ 15 = 0 with a remainder of 11 Therefore, 11/15 = 0 11/15

Method 2: Using Mixed Numbers

Step 1: Convert Mixed Numbers into Improper Fractions

Mixed numbers are converted into equivalent improper fractions by multiplying the whole number by the denominator and adding the numerator to the product.

Step 2: Find the LCM of the Denominators (if different)

If the denominators of the improper fractions are different, find the LCM as described in Method 1.

Step 3: Make All Fractions Equivalent

Multiply the numerator and denominator of each fraction by a number that makes the denominator equal to the LCM.

Step 4: Add the Numerators and Leave the Denominators the Same

Add the numerators of the equivalent fractions while leaving the denominators unchanged.

Step 5: Convert to a Mixed Number if Necessary

If the result of the addition is an improper fraction, convert it back to a mixed number using the same process as in Method 1.

Example:

Add the mixed numbers 1 2/3 and 2 3/4:

  1. 1 2/3 = 5/3 (convert to improper fraction: 3 x 1 + 2 = 5) 2 3/4 = 11/4 (convert to improper fraction: 4 x 2 + 3 = 11)
  2. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.
  3. 5/3 = 20/12 (multiply by 4/4) 11/4 = 33/12 (multiply by 3/3)
  4. 20/12 + 33/12 = 53/12
  5. 53/12 is an improper fraction, so convert it to a mixed number: 53 ÷ 12 = 4 with a remainder of 5 Therefore, 53/12 = 4 5/12
