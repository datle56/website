Hamster Care for Breeders

Preparation for Birth

Pregnancy Signs:

  • Noticeable weight gain
  • Active nest-building using bedding materials
  • Hoarding behavior with food and bedding
  • Increased aggression towards other hamsters or hands

Hamster Life Cycle:

  • Sexual maturity is typically reached around 4-6 months of age.
  • Breeding is not recommended until after 6 months of age to ensure physical and reproductive maturity.

Gestation Periods:

  • Syrian hamsters: 16 days
  • Dwarf breeds (e.g., Winter White, Campbell's): 18-21 days
  • Roborovski hamsters: Up to 30 days

Cage Preparation:

  • Clean the cage thoroughly approximately 2 weeks before the expected delivery date.
  • Remove any sharp objects, toys, or obstacles that could pose a hazard to the newborns.

Diet Changes:

  • Provide a diet rich in protein and fat to support the mother's increased nutritional demands. Aim for approximately 18-20% protein and 7-9% fat.

Separation of Father:

  • Remove the male hamster from the cage once pregnancy has been confirmed to prevent unwanted litters and aggression.

Nest Material:

  • Ensure the cage has an ample supply of soft and absorbent bedding material, such as toilet paper or shredded paper, for the mother to build a suitable nest.

Caring for Newborn Hamsters

Leave Nest Alone:

  • Avoid disturbing the nest for the first 2-3 days after birth. The mother will actively care for her young and defend them fiercely.

Handling:

  • Minimize handling of the newborns for at least 2 weeks to prevent stress and potential abandonment by the mother.

Cage Cleaning:

  • Refrain from cleaning the cage for the first 2 weeks unless absolutely necessary. Disturbances can be harmful to the delicate newborns.

Nutrition:

  • Ensure the mother has access to plenty of food and water to support her lactation and the growing pups.

Surrogate Mother:

  • In the unfortunate event that the mother dies or becomes incapacitated, a surrogate mother may need to be found to care for the newborns.

Care after 2 Weeks

Cage Cleaning:

  • The mother will become less protective after 2 weeks, allowing for regular cage cleanings.

Handling:

  • Gradually begin handling the hamsters to familiarize them with human contact. This will make handling easier for future examinations and care.

Weaning:

  • Separate the babies from the mother at approximately 4 weeks old. This will encourage the pups to become independent and prevent unwanted breeding.

Gender Separation:

  • Separate males from females once they reach sexual maturity (approximately 6 weeks old) to prevent unintended litters.

Adult Care:

  • Provide the same care and attention to the hamsters as you would adult hamsters, including a balanced diet, regular exercise, and a clean and spacious environment.

Conclusion

Breeding hamsters can be a rewarding experience, but it requires careful preparation and ongoing care. By following the guidelines outlined in this article, you can ensure the health and well-being of your pregnant hamster, her offspring, and the adult hamsters. Remember to provide a clean and nutritious environment, monitor their health closely, and exercise patience and gentle handling. With proper care, your hamsters will thrive and provide you with endless joy and companionship for years to come.
