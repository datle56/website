Comprehensive Guide to Converting a Computer Power Supply to a Lab Power Supply

Introduction:

Converting an old computer power supply (PSU) into a laboratory power supply is a cost-effective DIY project that provides a stable and versatile source of power for electronic projects and repairs. This guide provides a step-by-step approach to converting a computer PSU, including materials required, detailed instructions, and essential safety precautions.

Materials Required:

  • ATX power supply with a wattage rating of over 150 watts
  • Wire cutters
  • Pliers
  • Drill
  • Reamer
  • Soldering iron and solder
  • Electrical tape
  • Heat shrink tubing
  • Binding posts (+5V, +12V, +3.3V, ground)
  • LEDs (2x)
  • Current-limiting resistors (330 ohms for LEDs)
  • Power resistor (10 ohms)
  • Switch
  • Computer power cable

Preparation:

1. Discharge the Power Supply: Prior to working on the PSU, it is crucial to discharge any residual electrical energy stored within it. To do so, unplug the PSU from the computer and allow it to sit for several days.

2. Remove the Power Supply: Carefully remove the PSU from the computer case.

Power Supply Modification:

1. Sever Wire Connectors: Using wire cutters, remove and discard the bulky connectors attached to the wires of the PSU.

2. Color-Code Wires: Identify and separate the wires of the PSU based on their color coding:

  • Red: +5V standby
  • Yellow: +12V
  • Orange: +3.3V
  • Black: ground (GND)
  • Green: power-on (PS_ON)
  • Purple: remote standby (STBY#_N)
  • Grey: power-good (PG)

3. Power Resistor and Ground Connection: Connect a power resistor (10 ohms) between a red wire (+5V standby) and a black wire (GND). This resistor will serve as a load to the power supply.

4. Power-On LED Circuit: Connect the green wire (PS_ON) to one terminal of a switch. Connect the other terminal of the switch to a black wire (GND). Join a red LED (power-on indicator) in series between the green wire and the switch. Connect a 330-ohm current-limiting resistor in parallel with the LED.

5. Standby LED Circuit: Connect the purple wire (STBY#_N) to the anode of a second LED (standby indicator). Connect the cathode of the LED to a black wire (GND). Again, add a 330-ohm current-limiting resistor in parallel with the LED.

Binding Posts and Connections:

1. Mounting Binding Posts: Drill holes in the PSU case and mount binding posts for each voltage output (+5V, +12V, +3.3V) and ground (GND).

2. Voltage Output Connections: Connect the red wires (+5V standby) to the +5V binding post. Connect the yellow wires (+12V) to the +12V binding post. Connect the orange wires (+3.3V) to the +3.3V binding post.

3. Ground Connection: Connect all black wires (GND) to the ground binding post.

Testing and Reassembly:

1. Power Supply Test: Plug the power supply into a power outlet and flip the switch. Use a multimeter to verify the voltage outputs at the binding posts.

2. Encapsulation: Insulate any exposed wire connections using electrical tape or heat shrink tubing.

3. Reassembly: Reassemble the PSU case and secure it back into its original mounting location.

Safety Precautions:

  • Wear insulated gloves and work in a well-ventilated area.
  • Ground yourself by wearing an anti-static wrist strap.
  • De-energize the power supply before performing any modifications.
  • Never work on a live circuit.
  • Avoid probing high-voltage components.
  • Inspect wires for any damage or bare conductors.
  • Use insulated tools and take precautions against short circuits.

Conclusion:
