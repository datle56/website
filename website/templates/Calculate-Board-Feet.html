Method 1: Determining the Volume of Boards

Materials:

  • Measuring tape or ruler
  • Calculator

Steps:

  1. Utilize Nominal Size: Determine the nominal size of the board, which refers to the standard industry dimensions used for lumber, even though actual measurements may be slightly different. This information can typically be found on the board or lumber package.

  2. Measure Dimensions: Measure the thickness and width of the board in inches and the length in feet. Ensure precise measurements for accurate calculations.

  3. Calculate Board Feet: Multiply the measured thickness (in inches) by the width (in inches) by the length (in feet). Divide the result by 12 to convert from cubic feet to board feet. This value represents the volume of usable lumber in the board.

Formula: (Thickness x Width x Length) / 12 = Board Feet

Example:

For a board with a nominal size of 1x6x10 (1 inch thick, 6 inches wide, 10 feet long):

  • Thickness = 1 inch
  • Width = 6 inches

  • Length = 10 feet

  • Calculation: (1 x 6 x 10) / 12 = 5 board feet

Method 2: Estimating Board Feet in a Tree or Log

Materials:

  • Diameter tape or caliper
  • Height pole or tape measure
  • Doyle Scale (available at the provided link)

Steps:

  1. Measure Diameter at Chest Height (DBH): Use a diameter tape or caliper to measure the tree's diameter at a height of 4.5 feet above the ground. This measurement represents the DBH and is used for estimating board feet.

  2. Measure Height or Length: Measure the height of the entire tree, estimating in 16-foot increments, or measure the length of the cut log in 2-foot increments.

  3. Estimate Board Feet: Based on the DBH, height or length, and log end diameter, consult the Doyle Scale to estimate the approximate board feet. The scale provides a table that corresponds the measured dimensions to the estimated board feet.

Formula: Refer to the Doyle Scale table for the estimated board feet based on the specific measurements.

Example:

For a tree with a DBH of 16 inches, a height of 64 feet (four 16-foot increments), and a log end diameter of 12 inches:

  • DBH = 16 inches
  • Height = 64 feet

  • Log End Diameter = 12 inches

  • Reference the Doyle Scale table: Estimated Board Feet (Doyle Scale) = 132 board feet


Conclusion: Accurately Calculating Board Volume

Regardless of the method chosen, accurately measuring board volume is essential for minimizing waste and ensuring the optimal use of lumber resources. By following these methods carefully, you can determine the precise volume of boards, enabling you to plan construction projects with confidence. Whether you're a seasoned professional or a DIY enthusiast, these techniques will empower you to make informed decisions about your lumber needs, saving both time and money in the long run.
