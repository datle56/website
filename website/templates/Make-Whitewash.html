Making Traditional Whitewash:

Materials:

  • Hydrated lime (not garden lime): High-calcium lime that has been hydrated (mixed with water) to create a fine white powder.
  • Salt: Regular table salt or rock salt helps the whitewash adhere to surfaces.
  • Water: Clean, fresh water.
  • Protective gear: Gloves, mask, and eye protection

Instructions:

  1. Prepare the Salt Solution: Dissolve 2 cups of salt in a gallon of water. This helps bind the lime and improve its adhesion.
  2. Add the Lime: Gradually add 6-8 cups of hydrated lime to the salt solution, stirring constantly. Continue mixing until the lime dissolves completely, creating a smooth, creamy paste.
  3. Apply the Whitewash: Apply the whitewash to the desired surface using a brush, roller, or sprayer. For a thinner consistency suitable for spraying, use less lime and more water.
  4. Drying Time: Allow the whitewash to dry for several hours or overnight. As it dries, it will turn a bright white color.

Creating a Whitewash Look for Furniture:

Materials:

  • Furniture: Raw or sanded wood furniture
  • Sandpaper: Medium-grit sandpaper
  • White latex paint: Water-based paint in white
  • Water: Clean water
  • Brush: Natural or synthetic brush
  • Polyurethane (optional): Water-based polyurethane for durability

Instructions:

  1. Sand the Furniture: Use medium-grit sandpaper to remove any existing finish or imperfections on the furniture. Sanding helps create a smooth surface for the whitewash to adhere to.
  2. Dilute the Paint: Mix equal parts white latex paint and water in a container. This dilution creates a thin, more translucent whitewash effect.
  3. Apply the Whitewash: Dip the brush into the diluted paint and apply thin coats to the furniture, following the wood grain. Avoid applying too much paint at once, as it can create a blotchy look.
  4. Drying Time: Allow each coat to dry completely before applying another. Depending on the thickness of the coats and the temperature, drying can take several hours.
  5. Add Additional Coats: Continue adding thin coats of whitewash until you achieve the desired opacity and antique look.
  6. Seal the Whitewash (Optional): After the final coat has dried, apply a thin layer of water-based polyurethane to seal and protect the whitewash. This will enhance its durability and resistance to wear and tear.

Whether you're refreshing your home with traditional whitewash or mimicking the aesthetic on furniture, these techniques offer a simple and effective way to achieve a clean, classic look. By following these steps, you can create a versatile and timeless touch that enhances the character of your space. Remember to take necessary precautions, experiment with different methods, and let your creativity guide you as you bring the beauty of whitewash into your home.
