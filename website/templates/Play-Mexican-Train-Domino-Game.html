How to Play Mexican Train Dominoes

Objective:

The ultimate goal of Mexican Train Dominoes is to accumulate the fewest penalty points over a series of rounds. Players strive to discard all their dominoes before their opponents, effectively blocking them from doing the same.

Materials:

  • Double-twelve domino set (91 dominoes)
  • Markers or tokens for scoring

Setup:

  1. Shuffle the Dominoes: Mix the 91 dominoes face down and place them in the center of the table.
  2. Draw Dominoes: Based on the number of players, each player draws a predetermined number of dominoes:
    • 2-4 players: 15 dominoes per player
    • 5-6 players: 12 dominoes per player
    • 7-8 players: 10 dominoes per player
  3. Conceal Your Dominoes: Once dominoes have been drawn, players stand them up on edge so that they can see the faces while keeping them hidden from other players.

Gameplay:

  1. Start the Train: The player holding the double-twelve domino commences the game by placing it in the center as the "engine" of the Mexican Train.
  2. First Turn: On their initial turn, players must extend the train by placing a domino with a number that matches the double-twelve. This domino will form the first carriage of the train.
  3. Subsequent Turns: After the first turn, players take turns playing a single domino. Dominoes must be played perpendicularly to the previous domino, creating a chain or "train" of interconnected tiles.
  4. Matching Numbers: When placing a domino, the number at its open end must match the number at the end of the existing train.
  5. Doubles: If a player plays a domino with a double number (e.g., 6-6), they get an extra turn and must play again off the double.
  6. Mexican Train: Any player can start a separate train by playing a 12 domino off the engine. This train is open to all players and can be played upon by any player's turn.
  7. Blocked Doubles: If a double tile is played, it must be played off before other tiles can be placed on the same end of the train.
  8. Blocked Ends: If a player cannot play any domino because all available ends are blocked, they must draw a domino from the boneyard (the remaining pile of dominoes). If the drawn domino cannot be played, the player's turn ends.
  9. End of Round: When one player manages to discard all their dominoes, the round ends.

Scoring:

At the end of each round, players calculate their score by totaling the value of the dominoes remaining in their hands. The value of each domino is determined by the sum of the two numbers on its faces.

Winning:

The game is played over a series of 13 rounds. The player with the lowest cumulative score after all rounds is declared the winner. In the event of a tie, the player with the fewest points in the final round wins.
