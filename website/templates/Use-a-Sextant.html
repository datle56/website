Comprehensive Guide to Finding the Angle of Elevation Using a Sextant

Step 1: Correct for Your Height

  • Obtain Your Elevation: Determine your height above sea level using a topographic map, GPS device, or an online elevation database.
  • Calculate Dip: Use the following formula to calculate the dip, which accounts for the curvature of the Earth:

Dip = 1.7725' × √(Your elevation in meters)

Step 2: Measure the Horizon

  • Position the sextant horizontally.
  • Align the index mirror (the mirror on the movable arm) with the horizon mirror (the fixed mirror on the body of the sextant), creating a straight line of sight through the scope.
  • Adjust the horizon mirror until the horizon line is visible through the scope.

Step 3: Sight the Object

  • Keep the horizon line in the scope's field of view.
  • Move the index arm until the object you wish to measure appears in the index mirror, reflected from the horizon mirror.

Step 4: Adjust the Index Arm

  • Slowly move the index arm so that the object is near the horizon line.

Step 5: Fine-Tune with Micrometer Knob

  • Adjust the micrometer knob, which moves the horizon mirror slightly, to precisely align the object with the horizon line.

Step 6: Record the Time

  • Note the time of the sighting accurately, starting with seconds first.

Step 7: Read the Angle

  • Identify the degree measurement on the sextant arc and the minute reading on the micrometer knob or scale. Record these values along with the time.

Corrections for Accurate Measurement

Index Error: * The sextant may have a slight error in its alignment. Determine the index error by comparing the sextant's horizon reading with a known horizon. Adjust the angle measurement by adding or subtracting the index error.

Dip: * Subtract the calculated dip value from the observed altitude to account for your distance above sea level.

Refraction: * The Earth's atmosphere bends light, causing celestial bodies to appear higher than they actually are. Refer to the "Nautical Almanac" to find the refraction correction based on the observed altitude and the celestial body observed.

Semi-Diameter: * When observing the sun or moon, apply a semi-diameter correction to adjust for their apparent size.

Parallax: * The Earth's curvature causes the observer's position on Earth to affect the angle of measurement. Use the "Nautical Almanac" to determine the parallax correction based on the celestial body and the observed altitude.

Calculating Your Latitude

Daytime Method:

  • Measure the sun's altitude at noon.
  • Apply the necessary corrections for index error, dip, refraction, semi-diameter, and parallax.
  • Subtract the corrected altitude from 90° to find the declination.
  • Determine the sun's declination from the "Nautical Almanac" based on the date and time.
  • Add the declination to your previous calculation if the sun is north of the equator, or subtract it if it's south.
  • The result is your approximate latitude.
