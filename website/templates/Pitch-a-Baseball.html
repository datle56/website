How to Pitch Like a Pro

Grips

Four-Seam Fastball:

  • Place your index finger and middle finger slightly spread apart, barely covering the laces.
  • Grip the ball firmly with your thumb resting comfortably on the side.
  • This grip allows for a high-velocity pitch with excellent control.

Two-Seam Fastball:

  • Position your index and middle fingers along the seams of the ball.
  • Keep your fingers close together, creating a "V" shape.
  • This grip imparts a downward movement on the ball, making it effective for inducing ground balls.

Changeup:

  • Form a circle with your thumb and index finger.
  • Place your other fingers around the ball, centering it evenly.
  • This grip causes the ball to travel slower and with less spin, making it difficult for batters to time.

Curveball:

  • Place your index and middle fingers tightly together on the seams of the ball.
  • As you release the pitch, snap your wrist downward and to the side.
  • This grip generates a curving motion, making the ball difficult to hit solidly.

Slider:

  • Position your index and middle fingers across the widest seam of the ball.
  • Keep your fingers slightly off-center, towards the inside part of the grip.
  • Release the ball late, causing it to slide laterally as it crosses the plate.

Split Finger:

  • Grip the ball like a two-seam fastball, with your index and middle fingers along the seams.
  • Rotate the ball slightly and spread your fingers outwards, creating a "V" shape.
  • This grip imparts a downward and tailing movement on the ball, making it effective for inducing double plays.

Forkball:

  • Place your index and middle fingers firmly between the seams of the ball.
  • Jam the ball between your fingers, creating a "fork" shape.
  • This grip generates an extreme downward motion, making the ball difficult to lift.

Knuckleball:

  • Dig your fingernails into the middle of the ball, just below the seams.
  • Keep your fingers close together, creating a "knuckle" shape.
  • Release the ball with a straight arm motion, allowing it to flutter erratically.

Pitching Mechanics

Wind-up:

  • Stand with your feet shoulder-width apart, facing the batter.
  • Hold your glove in front of your chest, with your pitching hand extended to the side.
  • Stay balanced and relaxed.

Stretch:

  • Rotate your feet so that they point directly towards first and third base.
  • Step forward with your stride leg (usually the left leg for right-handed pitchers), placing it one to two inches ahead of the rear leg.
  • Keep your knee slightly bent.

Delivery:

  • Step with your stride leg, driving your power from the ground up.
  • Simultaneously, lift your glove leg and begin to break your pitching arm in a downward semi-circular motion.
  • Lower your glove leg as you swing your throwing arm up, keeping it loose and parallel to your body.
  • Push off your back foot and extend your arm, releasing the ball at the apex of your motion.
  • Follow through by extending your arm and back leg fully, maintaining balance.

Tips

  • Stay balanced and centered throughout the entire motion.
  • Keep your pitching arm consistent and repeatable.
  • Start slow and gradually increase your intensity to avoid arm soreness.
  • Ice your pitching arm and run after pitching to promote recovery.
  • Focus on accuracy before speed, developing a solid foundation and control.
  • Practice regularly to improve your technique and consistency.
