Identifying Raw Diamonds

Simple Tests:

  • Visual Inspection:
    • Observe the stone's shape. Raw diamonds typically exhibit crystal faces with four sides converging to a point, resembling an octahedron or a tetrahedron.
    • Examine the surface texture. Raw diamonds usually have a rough, unpolished texture with visible striations or etchings.
  • Scratch Test:
    • Obtain a piece of corundum, a mineral with a slightly higher hardness than diamond (9 on the Mohs scale).
    • Gently scrape the stone against the corundum. If the stone leaves a scratch on the corundum, it's a strong indication that it's a diamond, as most other minerals cannot scratch corundum.
  • Diamond Tester:
    • Utilize a diamond tester, a specialized device that measures electrical and thermal conductivity to distinguish diamonds from imitation materials.
    • Place the diamond tester on the stone. If it gives a positive reading (e.g., green light or audible signal), it suggests the presence of a diamond. However, note that diamond testers can sometimes give false positives or negatives.

Specific Gravity Test:

  • Density Calculation:
    • Determine the weight of the stone using a scale.
    • Suspend the stone in water and measure its weight in water.
    • Calculate the specific gravity by dividing the stone's weight by its weight in water.
  • Diamond Density Value:
    • Diamonds have a specific gravity range of 3.5 to 3.53 grams per cubic centimeter (g/cm3).
    • If the calculated specific gravity falls within this range, it indicates a strong possibility that the stone is a diamond. However, other heavy minerals like topaz or garnet can also have similar specific gravities.

Advanced Tests:

  • Microscopic Examination:
    • Examine the stone under a microscope or a loupe (magnifying glass).
    • Look for characteristic surface features that are indicative of diamonds, such as:
      • Rounded edges with indented triangles (trigons)
      • Parallelograms or rotated squares (cuboids)
  • Professional Evaluation:
    • For definitive confirmation, consult a qualified jeweler or gemologist.
    • They will use specialized instruments and techniques to analyze the stone's properties, including its refractive index, birefringence, and fluorescence, to determine its identity and authenticity.
    • They will also assess its quality and grade it based on factors such as carat weight, color, clarity, and cut.

Conclusion

Identifying raw diamonds requires a combination of knowledge, patience, and the right tools. While these simple and advanced tests provide valuable insights, it's crucial to note that they are not foolproof. Definitive confirmation of a diamond's authenticity requires professional evaluation by a qualified jeweler. Their expertise can provide peace of mind and ensure that you possess a genuine gem rather than an imitation.
