Checking Pipette Calibration: A Comprehensive Guide

Materials:

  • Target pipette
  • Pipette tips compatible with the pipette
  • Ultrapure water or other appropriate liquid
  • Volumetric beaker
  • Thermometer
  • Analytical balance with a precision of 0.001 grams
  • Aluminum or plastic weigh boats
  • Tweezers

Steps:

1. Preparation:

  • Collect all the materials listed above.
  • Ensure that the balance is calibrated and stable.
  • Measure the temperature of the water and note it down.

2. Zeroing the Balance:

  • Place an empty aluminum weigh boat on the balance.
  • Press the "zero" button to set the reference weight.

3. Pipetting Procedure:

  • Set the pipette to the desired volume.
  • Attach a pipette tip to the pipette.
  • Rinse the tip with the liquid to be pipetted by aspirating and dispensing a small amount.
  • Aspirate the desired volume of liquid by immersing the tip slightly below the surface.
  • Gently press the plunger to dispense the liquid into the weigh boat.
  • Avoid touching the weigh boat with the pipette tip.

4. Weighing and Recording:

  • Carefully remove the weigh boat from the balance using tweezers.
  • Record the weight of the liquid to the nearest 0.001 grams.
  • Repeat steps 3-4 multiple times (typically 5-10 trials) and record the weights for each trial.

5. Calculating the Average Weight:

  • Calculate the average weight of the liquid dispensed from the pipette.

6. Calculating the Pipetted Volume:

  • Use the formula V = w * Z, where:
    • V = calculated volume
    • w = average weight
    • Z = density of the liquid at the measured temperature (obtain from a reliable source)

7. Comparing the Results:

  • Compare the calculated volume to the volume set on the pipette.
  • Calculate the percentage difference between the two volumes.

8. Assessment of Calibration:

  • If the percentage difference is greater than the acceptable tolerance (typically 1% or less), the pipette requires calibration.
  • If the percentage difference is within the acceptable tolerance, the pipette is considered calibrated.

Importance of Calibration:

  • Accurate pipetting is crucial for precise measurements in scientific experiments, particularly in quantitative analysis, molecular biology, and medical diagnostics.
  • Regular calibration of pipettes ensures the reliability and accuracy of experimental results.
  • Uncalibrated pipettes can lead to significant errors and compromise the validity of research findings.
  • Calibration should be performed according to the manufacturer's recommendations or at regular intervals, typically every 6-12 months.

Conclusion:

Verifying pipette calibration is an essential practice to guarantee the accuracy and integrity of scientific experiments. By meticulously adhering to the steps outlined above, researchers can ensure that their pipettes are delivering the precise volumes required for precise measurements. Regular calibration not only enhances the reliability of experimental results but also prevents costly errors and maintains the validity of scientific findings. Moreover, it promotes a culture of meticulousness and attention to detail, fostering a rigorous approach to scientific inquiry.
