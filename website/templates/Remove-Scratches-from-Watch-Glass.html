Selecting the Right Polish for Your Watch Crystal

The first step in restoring the clarity of your watch crystal is to determine its material composition. Different types of crystals require specific polishes to effectively remove scratches without damaging the surface.

  • Acrylic Crystal: Acrylic crystals, commonly found on budget-friendly watches, are highly resistant to shattering but are prone to surface scratches. Avoid using abrasive polishes like steel wool or harsh chemicals. Instead, opt for:

    • Fluoride-free toothpaste: The gentle abrasives in fluoride-free toothpaste can effectively remove light surface scratches with minimal effort.
    • Watch polishes (Polywatch or Brasso): These products are specifically designed for acrylic crystals and are designed to gently polish away scratches without leaving residue.

  • Mineral Glass Crystal: Mineral glass crystals, often used on mid-range watches, offer improved scratch resistance compared to acrylic crystals. They can be polished using a wider range of polishes, including:

    • Any watch crystal polish: Look for polishes that are specifically designed for watch crystals, ensuring compatibility with mineral glass. These polishes gently remove scratches while restoring the crystal's clarity.

  • Sapphire Crystal: Sapphire crystals, found on high-end watches, are renowned for their extreme scratch resistance. However, they can still accumulate fine scratches over time. Use a specialized polish made for sapphire crystals:

    • 0.5 Micron Lapping Paste: This ultra-fine polishing paste gently abrades the crystal's surface, removing scratches without affecting its durability.
    • 3 Micron DP3 Dia-Paste: A slightly more aggressive paste used for deeper scratches. It effectively removes blemishes while maintaining the crystal's structural integrity.

  • Unsure of Crystal Type: If you are unable to determine the crystal type of your watch, contacting the watch manufacturer is advisable. They can provide you with specific guidance on the appropriate polish to use.

Polishing Scratches from Your Watch Crystal

Once you have selected the appropriate polish, follow these steps to carefully polish your watch crystal:

Materials:

  • Painter's tape
  • Selected watch crystal polish
  • Soft cloth
  • OPTIONAL: Replacement crystal if scratches are too deep

Instructions:

  1. Protect the Watch: Securely cover the watch case and other components with painter's tape to prevent accidental damage from the polishing process.

  2. Apply Polish: Apply a small dollop of polish to the center of the watch crystal using a clean soft cloth.

  3. Buff the Crystal: Gently rub the crystal in circular motions, applying light to moderate pressure. Continue buffing until the scratches gradually disappear.

  4. Deep Scratches: If the scratches are too deep for the polish to remove, it may be necessary to consider replacing the crystal. Consult with a watch repair professional for this service.

Note: Remember to follow the manufacturer's instructions for the specific polish you are using, as application methods may vary. Always use light pressure and avoid over-polishing, as this could damage the crystal's surface.
