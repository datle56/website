Method 1: Factoring Numbers and Basic Expressions

Factoring is a mathematical operation that involves expressing a number or algebraic expression as a product of its constituent factors. In simpler terms, it means finding the numbers or variables that, when multiplied together, result in the original number or expression.

  • Factoring Numbers: Factoring numbers involves finding pairs of numbers that multiply to give the original number. For instance, the number 12 can be factored as 1 x 12, 2 x 6, 3 x 4, and so on.

  • Factoring Variables with Coefficients: When factoring variables with coefficients, we seek common factors among the coefficients and variables. For example, 12x can be factored as 3 x 4x or 2 x 6x.

  • Distributive Property: The distributive property of multiplication states that multiplying a sum or difference by a factor is equivalent to multiplying each term in the sum or difference by that factor. This property can be used to simplify algebraic equations by finding common factors. For instance, 12x + 6 can be simplified as 6(2x + 1) by factoring out the common factor 6.

Method 2: Factoring Quadratic Equations

Quadratic equations are algebraic equations of the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, and c are coefficients and x is the variable. There are multiple methods to factor quadratic equations:

  • Factoring by Inspection: Some simple quadratic equations can be factored by inspection. For example, x² + 5x + 6 can be factored as (x + 3)(x + 2) by identifying two numbers whose sum is 5 and product is 6.

  • Completing the Square: Completing the square involves manipulating the quadratic equation to transform it into the form (x + h)² = k, where h and k are constants. Once the equation is in this form, it can be easily factored as (x + h)².

  • Algebraic Identity: The algebraic identity x² + 2xh + h² = (x + h)² can be used to factor quadratic equations that do not have simple factors.

  • Solving for x: After factoring the quadratic equation, we solve for the variable x by setting each factor equal to zero. For instance, (x + 3)(x + 2) = 0 => x + 3 = 0 or x + 2 = 0, which yields x = -3 or x = -2.

Method 3: Factoring Other Forms of Equations

Beyond numbers and quadratic equations, there are other algebraic expressions that can be factored:

  • Equation a² - b²: This equation factors to (a + b)(a - b).

  • Equation a² + 2ab + b²: This equation factors to (a + b)² (or (a - b)² for the equation a² - 2ab + b²).

  • Equation a³ - b³: This equation factors to (a - b)(a² + ab + b²).
