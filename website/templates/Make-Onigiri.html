Onigiri: An Exquisite Culinary Delicacy of Japan

Onigiri, or Japanese rice balls, are an essential part of Japanese cuisine, enjoyed as a portable snack or a hearty meal. Crafted with precision and filled with an array of delectable ingredients, onigiri offer a harmonious blend of flavors and textures that delight the palate.

Ingredients:

Rice:

  • 2 cups sushi rice (authentic for optimal stickiness)
  • 2.5 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Seaweed (Nori):

  • 4 sheets (optional, for wrapping)

Fillings (choose one):

  • Salmon: 2 teaspoons chopped salmon steak (baked or grilled)
  • Sour: 3 chopped pickled plums (pitted)
  • Salty: 2 pouches bonito flakes, moistened with 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • Fishy: 1 can tuna (drained) mixed with 1 tablespoon Japanese mayonnaise

Instructions:

1. Cooking the Rice:

  • Rinse and soak the sushi rice for 30 minutes in cold water to remove excess starch.
  • Drain the rice thoroughly.
  • Combine the rice, water, and salt in a covered pot.
  • Bring the mixture to a boil over medium-high heat.
  • Once boiling, reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes.
  • Remove the pot from the heat and let it steam for 10 minutes, undisturbed.
  • Fluff the cooked rice gently with a rice paddle or fork.

2. Preparing the Fillings:

  • Salmon: Bake or grill the salmon steak and chop it into small pieces.
  • Sour: Remove the pits from the pickled plums and chop them finely.
  • Salty: Mix the bonito flakes with soy sauce until moistened.
  • Fishy: Drain the tuna can and combine the tuna with Japanese mayonnaise.

3. Filling the Rice Balls:

  • Wet your hands with salted water to prevent the rice from sticking.
  • Scoop a handful of cooked rice into your palm and flatten it.
  • Create a small well in the center of the flattened rice.
  • Add your chosen filling to the well.

4. Molding the Onigiri:

  • Gradually wrap the rice around the filling, forming it into a ball with firm but gentle pressure.
  • Ensure that the rice is evenly distributed and holds together well.

5. Wrapping the Nori (Optional):

  • Cut the nori sheets into strips approximately 1 inch wide.
  • Wrap the nori strips around the onigiri, slightly overlapping at the top.
  • This step not only adds flavor but also helps prevent the rice from falling apart.

Tips:

  • Authentic sushi rice is highly recommended for its exceptional stickiness.
  • Wetting your hands with salt water prevents the rice from sticking to your palms while shaping the onigiri.
  • Experiment with different fillings to create unique and delicious variations. Popular choices include teriyaki chicken, pickled greens, or shrimp.
  • Store onigiri individually in plastic wrap for up to 3 days, depending on the nature of the filling used.
  • Onigiri can also be frozen for about 3 months and reheated in the microwave for convenience.
