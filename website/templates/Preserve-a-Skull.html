Preserving an Animal Skull: A Comprehensive Guide

Introduction

Preserving animal skulls is a fascinating and rewarding endeavor that allows you to create unique and educational specimens. Whether you're a wildlife enthusiast, a collector, or a student, this detailed guide will provide you with all the necessary steps and techniques to successfully preserve an animal skull.

Materials

  • Nitrile gloves and face mask
  • Sharp knife or scalpel
  • Enzymatic laundry detergent
  • Large container for soaking the skull
  • Hydrogen peroxide (3-6%)
  • Polyurethane spray

Step-by-Step Instructions

1. Removing the Flesh

  • Wear protective gear: Always wear nitrile gloves and a face mask to prevent the spread of zoonotic diseases.
  • Incise the skull: Using a sharp knife or scalpel, make a careful incision from the nose to the base of the skull.
  • Remove the flesh: Use your knife or hands to gently remove as much flesh as possible. Avoid damaging the bone.
  • Soak in detergent: Place the skull in a warm water bath with enzymatic laundry detergent. The detergent will help to loosen the remaining flesh. Soak for at least 12 hours or overnight.

2. Cleaning the Skull

Choose a cleaning method: You have several options for cleaning the skull:

  • Maceration: Submerge the skull in a solution of enzymatic laundry detergent and warm water. Keep the skull submerged for several weeks, changing the water and detergent regularly. As the flesh decomposes, gently remove the loosened tissue.
  • Hot water: Bring a large pot of water to a boil and submerge the skull. Boil for several hours, stirring occasionally. Remove the skull and scrape away the softened flesh.
  • Dermestid beetles: Place the skull in a container with dermestid beetles, which will feed on and remove the flesh. The process may take several weeks.
  • Burying: Bury the skull in a well-drained location and leave it for several months to decompose naturally.

3. Bleaching and Preserving

  • Defat the skull: Remove any remaining grease by soaking the skull in a water bath with dish detergent for several hours. Rinse thoroughly.
  • Air dry: Remove the skull from the water and allow it to air dry in a well-ventilated area.
  • Whiten the skull: Submerge the skull in a solution of 3-6% hydrogen peroxide for 3-4 hours. This will help to whiten and disinfect the bone.
  • Apply sealant: Once the skull is dry, apply polyurethane spray to protect it from deterioration and discoloration. Allow the spray to dry completely.

Tips for Success

  • Use hydrogen peroxide instead of household bleach, as bleach can damage the bone.
  • Practice your technique on a small skull before attempting to preserve a larger one.
  • Wear protective gear throughout the process to prevent the spread of diseases.
  • Allow the skull to air dry thoroughly in a well-ventilated area to prevent mold growth.
  • Let the polyurethane spray dry completely before handling the skull to avoid sticking or damage.

Conclusion

Preserving animal skulls is a challenging but rewarding process that requires patience, attention to detail, and an understanding of anatomy. By following these steps and adhering to the tips provided, you can successfully preserve and display these fascinating specimens, creating unique and educational artifacts for years to come.
